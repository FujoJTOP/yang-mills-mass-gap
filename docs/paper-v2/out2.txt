Abstract
We report the outcome of a sustained campaign -- Lean 4 formalization plus lattice numerics -- aimed at the 4D pure SU(N ) Yang­Mills mass gap. We do not prove the mass gap. We prove, instead, a reduction, a ledger, and a map of the wall drawn in two independent coordinates.
The reduction. (i) The gap is equivalent to the statement that the Källén­Lehmann spectral measure  of tr F 2 has its lightest atom m12 at positive mass with positive weight f12 = ({m21}) = |0| tr F 2|G|2, and that this atom is isolated. (ii) An exact spectral squeeze f12/  inf supp   M0/ shows that a lower bound on the mass needs both a residue floor f12  r0 and a susceptibility ceiling   1/; the condition H1 (  1/) is necessary but not sufficient, because it is blind to a thin atom. This is now a machine-checked statement: the module H1Sharpening proves formally that no positivity-only route from H1 yields a positive atom. (iii) On the order of a hundred and fifty routes, built in more than twenty waves, converge on one species: a nonperturbative, non-circular, sign-controlling upper bound on a conditioned variance, appearing once in the ultraviolet (the L3 node) and once in the infrared (H1).
The wall in two coordinates. This edition's new structural content is that the same wall has been reached from a second, independent coordinate, with a disjoint technique pool. (a) Measure / weak-: the atom is exactly the weak- discontinuity of the boundary density; every finite datum (moments, Taylor coefficients, point evaluations, bounded-kernel integrals, Wilson-loop images) is weak- continuous, and atomless measures are weak- dense, so any functional that forces the atom is weak- discontinuous -- hence either a reading (A3) or an infinite-data limit (H1). Eight falsification routes confirm this. (b) /RG/conformal: the gap is equivalent to pure Yang­Mills (Nf = 0) lying outside the conformal window, i.e. to the absence of a strong-coupling infrared fixed point. The statement "a gapless interacting 4D asymptotically-free Wilsonian IR attractor is conformal" is true -- Polchinski in d = 2; Dymarsky­Komargodski­Schwimmer­Theisen in d = 4, conditional on unitarity -- but it reduces the problem to the same dynamical fact. The conformal window is real (SU(3) with 9  Nf  16 is genuinely single-coupling and gapless-conformal), so "asymptotically free  gapped" is false in general; pure Yang­Mills is gapped because Nf = 0 lies far below the window, a dynamical statement. Eight routes through the modern /CFT toolset (single-coupling, nonperturbative  sign, central charges, large-N , conformal bootstrap, window boundary, holographic NEC, Dyson­Schwinger scaling) all fail,

1 Introduction
1.1 The Clay statement, exactly The Clay problem, as posed by Jaffe and Witten, is:

Prove that for any compact simple gauge group G, a non-trivial quantum Yang­Mills theory exists on R4 and has a mass gap  > 0. Existence includes establishing axiomatic properties at least as strong as those cited in Streater­Wightman (1964) and Osterwalder­Schrader (1973, 1975).
[Jaffe­Witten, Quantum Yang­Mills Theory, Clay Mathematics Institute]

Three features of this statement are habitually under-weighted and drive everything that follows.
(1) Uniformity. It demands a uniform gap: not merely that each finite lattice volume has a positive spectral gap (trivially true by compactness), but that the gap survives L   and a  0 with a volume- and cutoff-uniform lower bound, and that the resulting theory clusters exponentially at a rate m > 0 in every channel carrying the lightest state.
(2) Gauge invariance. It demands that the gap be a feature of the gauge-invariant, physical theory -- not of a gauge-fixed chart, and not of a perturbative expansion around a chosen background. This sounds like a technicality; it is not. Several of the campaign's apparent "positive signals" turned out to live on gauge-dependent modes, and one of the no-gos below is precisely that gauge invariance reaches the vacuum but not the first excited scalar.
(3) Existence plus gap are logically separate. A construction of the measure with the Osterwalder­Schrader axioms (existence) is a different task from the lower bound on the Hamiltonian (gap). They interact -- a uniform gap gives clustering, and clustering is one of the axioms -- but neither implies the other. The campaign treats them as two halves, G1 and G2, and is explicit that progress on one does not constitute progress on the other.

1.3 The two halves
The campaign splits Clay into two halves and a channel:
· G1 (existence). 4D continuum existence with OS axioms. The fixed-volume construction is essentially complete; the one genuinely open UV input is a uniform renormalization constant Za, equivalently a cutoff-uniform bound on the force Hessian (the node called L3). L3 is purely ultraviolet and orthogonal to the gap -- proving it would make fixed-volume existence unconditional but would not help G2.
· G2 (gap). The proof or transport of f12 > 0 (equivalently of the coarse curvature G(0) > 0 at k  0). This is where the problem lives.
· The physical channel. Identifying the lightest state as 0++ and showing it does not dissolve (the no-dissolution theorem). This half is, conditionally, complete.

2 How to read this audit: labels, provenance, and negative results
This chapter states the conventions of the paper. It is placed early because a reader who misreads the labels will misread the conclusions: the paper's central claim is not a theorem but a classification, and a classification is only as good as the discipline that produced it.
2.1 The label system
Every substantive claim carries one of five labels. The labels are not decoration; each licenses a specific kind of use.
· T theorem. The statement is proved, with every imported standard result named. A T statement may be used as a premise. Example: the spectral squeeze f12/  inf supp   M0/.
· C conditional theorem. The statement is a proved implication from explicitly named hypotheses. It may be used only together with those hypotheses. Example: the no-dissolution theorem, conditional on the gap and the ordering input.

A conditional reduction of the 4D pure Yang­Mills mass gap (v2)

11

· N no-go. The statement is a theorem that a class of approaches cannot succeed. It may be used to close a branch, never to conclude that no approach can succeed. Example: the rigidity no-go for discrete indices.
· M exact missing step. The statement is a precisely formulated open inequality or identity, with no slack hidden. It may be used to specify the target, never as a premise. Example: H1, the negative-association inequality, TCI.
· E numerical evidence. The statement is a measurement, with a configuration and a statistical method. It may be used as evidence and as a calibration, never as a proof. Example: f12 > 0 at 17 on SU(2).
A structural statement that is a theorem about the logic or the topology of the problem, rather than about Yang­Mills, is labelled T with an explicit scope. The weak- argument of Chapter 10 is the main example: its lemmas are theorems about measures, and the paper says so; the identification of the campaign's tools with the class of continuous functionals is evidence from the falsification wave, not a universal theorem.
2.2 The provenance rule
Every theorem in this paper names a file in the repository that contains its proof. The rule has three consequences. 1. No orphan claims. A claim without a file is not allowed. If a claim is a conjecture, it is
labelled as such and the label is the provenance.
2. Corrections are recorded, not hidden. When the corpus is found to be wrong, the paper corrects it and records the correction in Appendix G. The central-charge normalization is the clearest example: the earlier corpus value 31/36 was a normalization slip, the correct value is 31/18, and the correction is in both the paper and the corpus file.
3. Uncertainties are reproduced. Where the corpus flags a bibliographic or analytical uncertainty, the paper reproduces the flag rather than silently repairing it. The reader can then judge.
2.3 The acid test
The acid test is the campaign's quality control. Every purported new tool must fail on the free massless field, where f12 = 0 and G = 0. The test has three adversary classes (Chapter 7): the free massless field; the gapped composite :2:, which has a gap but no atom in the channel; and the free gapped scalar. A tool that "proves" a gap for any of them is vacuous.
The logic of the test is worth spelling out. The gap problem is not "prove that something is positive." It is "prove that a specific positivity is absent in the free case and present in the interacting case." A tool that cannot distinguish the two cases carries no information about the gap, even if it produces a positive number on Yang­Mills. The acid test is the operational form of that distinction. It is also why the campaign's positive results are so few: most natural functionals are positive on the free field as well, and are therefore blind.
2.4 What a no-go is, and what it is not

the rigidity no-go, and the analytic-transfer no-go are statements that a type of argument cannot work. They are the most valuable negative results because they close families, not individual attempts.
· Method no-gos. The wrong-half, wrong-direction, and wrong-regime umbrella is a statement about failure modes that recur across families. It is a diagnostic tool: when a new route appears, the first question is which of the three modes it exhibits.

A no-go is not a statement that the gap is unprovable. The paper never makes that statement. The no-gos are constraints on the shape of a proof, and the shape they leave open is the one specified in Chapter 15.
2.5 Why negative results are banked
An undiagnosed failure is a failure that will be retried. The immune map (Chapter 9) exists because the campaign's early waves repeatedly rediscovered the same dead ends in new costumes. 4.2 G1.1 -- the perturbative kernel is closed

8.2 Goldstone uniqueness
The only positivity-plus-analyticity theorem that forces an atom is Goldstone's theorem, and it lives at zero mass (broken continuous symmetry). Proof status of (). In two dimensions, Polchinski (1988) completed Zamolodchikov's argument: rigid scale invariance implies local conformal invariance under broad conditions. In four dimensions, the situation is conditional. Dymarsky, Komargodski, Schwimmer and Theisen (2013/2015) show that scale invariance plus unitarity implies that infinitely many matrix elements vanish in a suitable kinematic configuration, a nontrivial necessary condition for conformality, and argue that it is expected to be sufficient modulo possible exceptions. The a-theorem (Komargodski­Schwimmer 2011) excludes limit cycles in four-dimensional unitary theories, because the a-anomaly decreases monotonically along the flow and a cycle would force it to return. A massless dilaton branch is excluded by asymptotic freedom: the coupling grows in the infrared and never reaches zero, so the infrared theory is interacting, not a free dilaton theory. Putting these together, () is true modulo the d = 4 scale-implies-conformal step, which is itself conditional in the literature. The paper labels () as C for that reason.
Acid test of (). The acid-test question for a statement is whether it has a counterexample in the class it claims to classify. Known candidates:
· Walking technicolor: a slowly running coupling that almost reaches a fixed point. In the strict infrared it either reaches the fixed point (conformal) or confines; the "walking" regime is a crossover, not a gapless non-conformal endpoint. No counterexample.
· Limit cycles: a periodic coupling. Excluded in d = 4 unitary theories by the monotonicity of a. No counterexample.
· Massless dilaton: a Goldstone of spontaneously broken scale invariance. Excluded in the asymptotically-free gapless case by the non-IR-free theorem (the coupling is O(1) in the infrared, not zero), so the dilaton cannot be the only light state. No counterexample.
So () survives the acid test: no gapless interacting 4D asymptotically-free non-conformal theory is known. This is why the campaign treats () as the sharpest reformulation of the wall -- and why it is not a solution. It reduces the problem to the dynamical statement (G1), which is exactly where the campaign started, in a different language.

11.6 What each technique can and cannot see
It is worth stating the limitation of each technique in one sentence, because the sentences are the coordinate's real yield: they say exactly what the gap is not.
· Single coupling. A single coupling can have a zero; whether it does is a matter of the theory's matter content. The number of couplings is not the obstruction.
· Nonperturbative  sign. There is no exact sign theorem for the beta function of a non-supersymmetric gauge theory. · Large-N . The 1/N expansion organizes fluctuations around a saddle. It does not decide whether the saddle is confining; the 't Hooft coupling is O(1) precisely at the scale where the question is decided.
· Bootstrap. The conformal bootstrap tests whether a CFT with given operator content exists. The gap question is whether Yang­Mills flows to such a CFT. 12.1 The picture
The campaign now has two independent descriptions of the same obstruction. We state the picture once, plainly, and then say exactly what it does and does not prove.
Coordinate 1 (measure / weak-). The gap is the positive isolated atom f12 > 0 of the Källén­Lehmann measure of tr F 2 at the bottom of its support. The atom is exactly the weak- discontinuity of the boundary density (Chapter 10). Every finitely computable datum is weak- continuous, and atomless measures are weak- dense, so no forcing functional can be continuous. A proof in this coordinate must be a weak- discontinuous, acid-clean, (L, a)-uniform functional that is provably positive on the Yang­Mills measure. The conformal window is real, so asymptotic freedom alone does not imply a gap; no exact identity forbids an infrared fixed point; and every modern /CFT technique either tests the wrong thing (CFT existence rather than RG flow) or is phase-blind (central charges) or is compatible with a gapless endpoint (holographic NEC, DSE scaling). The invariant. In both coordinates the missing object is the same: the failure of the infrared theory to be a gapless CFT, equivalently the positive isolation of the 0++ atom, equivalently H1. The paper's structural claim -- a labelled conjecture, not a theorem -- is that H1 and (G1) are the same wall drawn in two coordinates.
12.2 Why the coordinates are genuinely disjoint
Disjointness is not rhetoric; it can be stated precisely, and it is the reason the two failures are independent evidence.
The measure argument does not apply to the  coordinate. The weak- theorem is about functionals on the space of measures. A beta function is not a measure, and the statement "Nf =0 has no zero" is not a statement about weak- continuity. One could try to encode  as a functional of the measure, but that encoding is precisely the dynamical dictionary whose proof is the gap. So the weak- obstruction is silent in the  coordinate.
The window argument does not apply to the measure coordinate. The existence of the conformal window is a statement about the massless-fermion content of a gauge theory. It says nothing about the atom of a given measure. The measure coordinate's obstruction would persist even if the window were empty; the  coordinate's obstruction would persist even if the measure were atomless by fiat. So the window argument is silent in the measure coordinate.
The technique pools are disjoint. The measure coordinate uses spectral theory, moment problems, complex analysis, optimal transport, dependence inequalities, and numerical eigenvalue methods. The  coordinate uses the conformal bootstrap, the a-theorem, central charges, large-N , holography, Dyson­Schwinger equations, and lattice step-scaling. The intersection is small (both use lattice simulations, but for different observables), and the key arguments are of different types: one is an analytic statement about measures, the other a dynamical statement about

12.6 Does two coordinates make it easier?
No. It makes it sharper. The honest answer has three parts. · Not easier. Neither coordinate supplies a new estimate. The measure coordinate proves that
a large class of tools cannot work; the  coordinate proves that a large class of techniques cannot decide the question. Neither produces the missing object.
· Sharper. The two coordinates specify the target in two independent languages, and an object satisfying the specification in one language must satisfy it in the other. This is a strong consistency condition: a candidate tool can be tested in both coordinates before it is believed. The campaign used this as a filter (for example, a candidate that looked like a -function argument but was secretly a moment inequality failed the measure acid test immediately).
· More honest. The two-coordinate picture explains why the campaign's many failures were not failures of effort. They were failures of type: the tools were in the wrong class. The picture does not turn a failure into a success; it turns a confusing failure into a diagnosed one.
12.7 The one-bit statement
The slot wave's sentence deserves the last word of this chapter, because it is the campaign's best summary of the situation. The exact relation that would close the gap is coextensive with the whole quantum field theory: in the measure coordinate it is the full Källén­Lehmann measure, in the  coordinate it is the full nonperturbative beta function, and in the analytic coordinate it is the full transseries. The equation is infinite-dimensional in every coordinate. But its irreducible core is one scalar sign condition: is there a sign-definite, Q-independent source at the zero? As a system, the problem is the theory; as a decision, it is one bit; and the one bit is the mass gap.
This is the campaign's final structural statement. It is not a proof. It is the precise description of what a proof would have to be.

13.1 Constructive field theory
The constructive programme asks for a measure on the space of gauge fields satisfying the Osterwalder­Schrader axioms. Its main achievements are ultraviolet: Balaban's renormalization group analysis of lattice gauge theory [11], and the infrared construction with a cutoff by Magnen­ Rivasseau­Sénéor [12]. The modern probabilistic approach (Bauerschmidt­Brydges­Slade for 34 [25]; Chatterjee's rigorous large-N and survey [65]) and the stochastic-quantization approach (regularity structures) are powerful but remain small-parameter theories in the relevant sense. None of them produces a uniform mass gap at the physical four-dimensional coupling; the obstruction is the same intermediate-coupling region that this paper identifies as (G1)/H1. The paper's G1 node board (Chapter 4) is organized around exactly this split: the perturbative kernel is closed, the ultraviolet node L3 is open, and the infrared node is the gap.
13.2 Lattice gauge theory
The lattice is where the mass gap is measured. Wilson's formulation [66] and Creutz's Monte Carlo study [67] established the framework; the strong-coupling expansion and its rigorous polymer form (Osterwalder­Seiler [4], the source of P3's technology) give the only unconditional gap theorems, at  < 1/48. Shen­Zhu­Zhu [5] extended the rigorous strong-coupling gap to a modern standard. On the numerical side, the glueball spectrum (Morningstar­Peardon [24]), the step-scaling method [68], the gradient flow [55], and the effective string description of the flux tube [59, 58] are the standard tools. This paper's numerics (Chapter 14) are small by comparison, and are presented as diagnostics rather than as a spectrum computation. The campaign's contribution on this side is the diagnostic set (P3 approach law, Re-slope, finite-size law) and the explicit statement of what a decisive computation would require.
13.3 Holography and AdS/QCD
The holographic approach constructs confining gauge theories from string backgrounds (Maldacena [62]; Gubser­Klebanov­Polyakov [63]; Witten [64, 69]), with hard-wall and soft-wall models (Polchinski­Strassler [70]; Sakai­Sugimoto [71]) reproducing the qualitative glueball spectrum. The campaign's holographic wave (Chapter 11, the NEC route) found the precise limitation: the bulk null-energy condition is compatible with a gapless infrared AdS throat, and a confining wall requires a modeling choice whose justification is the dynamical question. Holography gives a dictionary and a picture, not a proof for the four-dimensional theory.
13.4 The conformal bootstrap and CFT data
The conformal bootstrap (El-Showk et al. [72]; the review of Poland­Rychkov­Vichi [73]) has transformed the study of CFTs by turning crossing symmetry and unitarity into numerical bounds. Its relation to the mass gap is the subject of Chapter 11: the bootstrap tests the existence of a CFT with given operator content, whereas the gap is a statement about the flow. The conformal-collider bounds on a/c [47, 48] and the a-theorem [43] are exact constraints that are compatible with a gapless infrared; they constrain which CFT, not whether one is reached. The campaign's conclusion is that the bootstrap is necessary but not sufficient, and that a pure-glue bootstrap, if it existed, would exclude one candidate endpoint rather than prove the gap.
13.5 Resurgence and transseries
The resurgent transseries programme ('t Hooft's renormalons; the review of Aniceto­Baar­ Schiappa [74]) is the natural language for the essential singularity e-1/(20g2). The campaign's analytic-transfer no-go (Chapter 8.10) is a statement in this language: the nonperturbative scale is invisible to every analytic-in-g2 procedure, and the condensate's u = 2 renormalon ambiguity is the same size as the condensate. Resurgence supplies the correct bookkeeping; it does not supply the sign.

Autocorrelation and binning. Configurations generated by a local update are correlated. The integrated autocorrelation time int of the observable is estimated from the normalized autocorrelation function (summed until it first crosses zero, with the standard window), and configurations are grouped into bins of length  int before any average is formed. A residual correlation shows up as a bin-size dependence of the error; the protocol requires the error to be stable under doubling the bin size.
Jackknife. All errors quoted are jackknife errors over bins. For a ratio or a fit parameter (such as the residue from a two-exponential fit), the jackknife is performed at the level of the fit, not by propagating errors through the fit.
Detection versus bound. A detection is a statement that a central value is many standard deviations from zero: f12,bare = 7.14 ± 0.42 is a 17 detection at that coupling and volume. A bound is a statement that a one-sided confidence interval excludes zero: the k  0 probe gives a 95% one-sided lower bound of -0.098 (SU(3)), which does not exclude zero. The two statements answer different questions, and the paper never converts one into the other. In particular, a detection on a gauge-dependent mode is not a bound on the gauge-invariant object.
Systematics. The dominant systematic of the k  0 probe is the object choice (gluon mode versus composite); the dominant systematic of the residue measurement is the fit range and the smearing family. Where a systematic cannot be quantified, the paper reports the spread across choices rather than a single number, and the number is labelled E with the caveat.
Finite-volume discreteness. On a finite lattice the spectrum is discrete, so every correlator is a finite sum of exponentials and a two-exponential fit will always "work" over some range. This is why the diagnostics of §14 insist on a volume-scaling law rather than a single-volume fit: the question is not whether an exponential fits, but whether its rate survives L  .

14.9 What a decisive numerical test would need
The diagnostics make the requirements explicit. A decisive lattice computation would need: 1. several volumes at fixed coupling, large enough that the 1/L2 law is visible and the lowest
momentum is in the scaling regime (the present L = 8 is not); 2. a gauge-invariant 0++ operator whose two-point function is measured with a smearing family,
so that the residue and the mass are operator-independent;
3. a continuum extrapolation at fixed physical volume, with at least three lattice spacings;
4. a one-sided statistical statement (a confidence bound) that excludes zero for (L, a) uniformly, not merely a central value;
5. a systematic-error budget for the continuum extrapolation and for the scale setting. The campaign's throughput analysis (Appendix B) indicates that SU(3) L = 16­24 at three couplings is feasible in C/OpenMP on 16 cores over days, and faster on a GPU; the bottleneck is not the algorithm but the statistical precision needed for a one-sided bound at the percent level. The existing SU(2) and SU(3) data are consistent with the picture but do not meet these requirements.
14.10 Summary of the numerical status
The numerics do three things, and no more. They see the residue where it is resolvable (17 at  = 2.2 on SU(2)). They locate the sign of the k  0 curvature on a gauge-dependent mode (positive, volume-stable, but with a one-sided interval that includes zero). Their value is that they turn each open step into a falsifiable numerical question, which is the most a finite-volume computation can do for a continuum problem.

A The Lean formalization
The reduction and its sharpening are partially machine-checked in Lean 4. The toolchain is pinned to leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0-rc1; Mathlib is the frozen badcache tree in vendor/OSforGFF/.lake/packages. Two modules matter for this paper: lean/YMStatement/Reduction.lean and lean/YMStatement/H1Sharpening.lean. The companion check scripts are lean/check-*.sh, and the run record is docs/LEAN-STATUS.md.
A.1 The reduction module Reduction.lean contains a conditional theorem whose statement is, in substance,
clay_gap_conditional (T) (hH1) (hCP) (hWgeo) (hP3) (hno) : MassGap T.
The five analytic facts -- H1, (CP), Wgeo  0, P3, and no-dissolution -- are explicit hypotheses, and the theorem proves that they imply the gap statement. The module also contains the already-unconditional pieces of the squeeze: susceptibility_squeeze, m_sq_ge_of_squeeze, transported_residue, mass_gap_of_spectral. The five analytic facts remain sorry hypotheses, with their intended content named in docs/LEAN-STATUS.md. The theorem itself is checked with zero sorryAx, and its axiom list is exactly [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]. What is verified is the logic of the reduction, not its hypotheses, and not Clay.
A.2 The H1 sharpening module H1Sharpening.lean machine-checks the order-algebraic content of Chapter 3. The five statements, verbatim up to notation, are:

theorem lemmaG_twoSided (hm : 0 < m) (hchi : 0 < chi) (hlo : f <= chi * m) (hup : chi * m <= M) : f / m <= chi /\ chi <= M / m

theorem cert_necessity (hf : 0 < f) (hchi : 0 < chi) (hlam : 0 < lam) (hchile : chi <= 1 / lam) (hlo : f <= chi * m) (hm : 0 < m) : lam * f <= m

theorem cert_sufficiency (hM : 0 < M) (hchi : 0 < chi) (hlam : 0 < lam) (hup : chi * m <= M) (hgap : lam * M <= m) : chi <= 1 / lam

theorem H1_blind_to_thin_atom : exists f m M chi lam : R, 0 < lam /\ 0 < chi /\ chi <= 1 / lam /\ 0 < m /\ f = 0 /\ f <= chi * m /\ chi * m <= M

theorem H1_does_not_imply_atom : not (forall f m M chi lam : R, 0 < lam -> 0 < chi -> chi <= 1 / lam -> 0 < m -> f <= chi * m -> chi * m <= M -> 0 < f)
All five are proved by ordered-field algebra (linarith, norm_num, ring) with no sorry. The countermodel is (f, m, M, , ) = (0, 1, 1, 1, 1).
Reading. cert_necessity says that the certificate plus the squeeze implies m  f : a gap floor given a positive atom. H1_does_not_imply_atom says that the positivity of f cannot be derived from the certificate and the squeeze. Together they formalize the sentence "H1 is necessary but not sufficient / blind to a thin atom." The formalization is deliberately order-algebraic: it does not model the Källén­Lehmann measure, and it makes no claim about Yang­Mills. Its value is that the logical claim is no longer an informal remark.

G.1 Erratum: the central-charge normalization
An earlier corpus file reported the free-vector ratio as a/c = 31/36 and called it "strictly interior" to the unitary band. That number came from an inconsistent normalization: a was divided by 4 and c by 2. In the standard normalization (per real scalar a = 1/90, c = 1/30), the free vector has a = 62/90 and c = 12/30 = 2/5, so

The conclusion is unchanged: the central charges do not exclude a CFT infrared, because the a-theorem is a ceiling and the allowed region aIR  (0, aUV ], aIR/cIR  [1/3, 31/18] is two-dimensional. G.2 Corrections preserved from the first edition
The following corrections are preserved because they are easy to undo by accident. 1. P3 does not give I2. The strong-coupling cost grading grades polymer amplitudes, not
transfer-matrix eigenvalue spacing. P3 is a residue theorem (I1), not an isolation theorem (I2). (PROOF-P3-UNCONDITIONAL.md §5.)
2. The certificate is not the gap. The certificate is a sufficient route (cert + IN-10 + IN-11  gap), but cert > 0 is strictly weaker than the gap and the necessity gap  cert is unproved. "Stuck at the certificate" does not imply "stuck at the gap." (campaign-final.md §6.)
3. D/S  2% is a gauge-dependent number. It is measured on the gluon mode at weak coupling, not on the gauge-invariant 0++ at the physical coupling. The correct object's variance is V -suppressed. (Appendix C.)
4. The "75×" strong-coupling figure was a unit confusion. The CPS/CNS 2025 frontier is tH < 1/24, not a factor 75 from the physical coupling. (PROOF-EMERGENT-4D.md.)
5. G Var() = 1 only for a Gaussian marginal. Measured 2­5% at  = 2.5; the exact Ward identity is E[a f ] = 1. (§3.)
6. The atom = atomless-bump distinction is not visible to finite data. Any finitemoment match is blind to the atom; the two-coordinate picture records this as the weak- theorem. (Chapter 10.)
7. The two-coordinate identification is a conjecture. The statement that H1 and (G1) are the same wall is labelled C (structural identification), not T . (Chapter 12.)
