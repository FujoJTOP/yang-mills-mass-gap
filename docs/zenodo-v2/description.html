Status. This is a conditional reduction and an audit, not a proof of the Clay problem. The mass gap for 4D pure SU(N) Yang–Mills remains open. Clay = 0%. No step of this paper is labelled QED.

What the paper does. (i) It rewrites the gap as the statement that the Källén–Lehmann spectral measure ν of tr F2 has a positive, isolated atom f12 at the bottom of its support. (ii) It proves the exact squeeze f12/χ ≤ inf supp ν ≤ M0/χ, which shows that a mass lower bound needs both a residue floor and a susceptibility ceiling; the condition H1 (χ ≤ 1/λ) is necessary but not sufficient, because it is blind to a thin atom. The countermodel is machine-checked in Lean 4 (module H1Sharpening, zero sorry): H1 plus the squeeze gives only m ≥ λf, and H1 alone does not imply f > 0. (iii) It states a conditional theorem whose gluing lemmas are machine-verified, and it records the no-gos and the exact missing steps.

New in the second edition. Part III draws the same obstruction in two independent coordinates. (a) Measure / weak-*: the atom is exactly the weak-* discontinuity of the boundary density; every finite datum is weak-* continuous and atomless measures are weak-* dense, so any functional that forces the atom is weak-* discontinuous — hence either a reading or an infinite-data limit (H1). (b) β/RG/conformal: the gap is equivalent to pure Yang–Mills (Nf=0) lying outside the conformal window. The conformal window is real (SU(3) with 9 ≤ Nf ≤ 16 is asymptotically free and gapless-conformal), so asymptotic freedom alone does not imply a gap; no exact identity forbids an infrared fixed point; and eight routes through the modern β/CFT toolbox fail, each for a sharp reason. The Dyson–Schwinger scaling solution is the gauge-fixed cousin of the worry, not a massless particle.

Ledger. Positively: a strong-coupling residue theorem (f12 > 0 for β < 1/48, volume- and L-uniform; I1, not I2); a kinematic no-dissolution theorem; Wgeo ≥ 0 by Prékopa; gauge-invariant Perron–Frobenius and its edge no-go; the coarse Poincaré lemma; the Feshbach–Bogoliubov two-block lemma; the exact variance decomposition G″ = S − D; the residue–level-gap lemma; and a machine-checked conditional theorem. Negatively: the Schur-convex master obstruction, Goldstone uniqueness, the periodic, nested-cone, rigidity, Feshbach-angle and Krein no-gos; the elimination of the order-parameter, topological-index and Markov-functional paradigms; and a structural no-go for complex-function-theory forcing tools (the Hardy L2 functional detects atom presence but not its weight).

Numerics. The residue is seen where it is resolvable (f12 > 0 at β = 2.2 on SU(2), seventeen sigma); it dissolves into the cutoff at β = 1.0, as the strong-coupling picture predicts. A k → 0 curvature probe is positive and volume-stable but is measured on a gauge-dependent gluon mode, not the 0++ object; its one-sided interval still includes zero. The diagnostics (P3 approach law, one-sided isolation bound, Re-slope, finite-size law) are genuine and continuum-robust in the ideal, but each is a read gated by L → ∞.

Verdict. The wall did not fall; it became transparent, ruled, instrumented, and drawn twice. What is missing is one object: a nonperturbative, non-circular, sign-controlling, free-field-failing, cutoff-uniform upper bound on a conditioned variance — equivalently a nonperturbative sign theorem for βNf=0. That object does not exist in the present toolbox, and naming it precisely is the paper’s real result.
