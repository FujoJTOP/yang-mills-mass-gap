\section{The twelve dead ends, each with one number}\label{sec:dead}

A dead end is a theorem, not a mood. Each row below names a route, the mechanism of its death,
and one number that makes the death checkable; every row is \LN{} in the corpus's sense (a
statement about a whole class of approaches) or an exact counterexample to the route's central
claim. The rows are grouped only for reading: rows 1--6 kill the analytic inequalities, rows 7--9
kill the decompositions and the tool families, rows 10--12 kill the model-level and structural
shortcuts.

\subsection{Why rows 1--6 are a single statement}

Rows 1--6 are six different attempts to manufacture a lower bound on $H1$'s left-hand side, and
they fail in a pattern: each supplies a bound in the direction that the inequality does not need,
or with a constant that is smaller than the quantity it must dominate by an explicit factor
($38\times$, $44\times$, $2.6\times$, or a whole sign). The one structural remark that unifies
them is that $H1$'s difficulty is not a shortage of inequalities but the sign of the Savvidy
Hessian: the negative mode $\lambda_-$ with $c^{2}=(\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12)$ is an exact $50/50$
split, and every convexity-based budget spends its entire allowance on the positive mode. \LN{}
That is why the SOS row (4) is not a technicality: a non-negative-coefficient pairing would need
$\Var\le-0.0367$, which is impossible for $\Var\ge0$, so the expression is not merely hard to
represent --- it is not representable in that form.

$\lambda\gtrsim6.3$); \code{Q12-SOS.md} \S3 (the exact $-0.0367$ and the $a=0$ arithmetic
impossibility), \S0 (the counterexample $S-D(0.75)=-0.005888$); \code{Q14-RCBL.md} \S1.2--\S1.3

\subsection{Why rows 7--9 are a single statement}

Rows 7--9 all die of the same cause: they work with the $\Phi$ sector. Row 7 shows that $H1$ does
not contain $\Phi''$ at all, so a decomposition cannot help; row 9 shows that every $\Phi$-only
quantity is invisible to the acid test, because at $\theta=\pi$ the measure is Haar for all
$\beta$ and a $\Phi$-only functional cannot see $\beta$; row 8 is the same disease in the form of
a conditional theorem. The positive content is the surviving pair
$\{m_{G'},-\Cov(G,\Phi')\}$, both of which contain $G=\partial_vS_W$ --- the only object in the
candidate list that carries a power of $\beta$. \LT{}/\LN{}

\code{P21-REBUILD.md} \S1--\S2 (the acid test and the surviving pair); \code{P13-ACID.md}:12
(the $\beta_0$ failure); \code{LOOP-RUNBOOK.md}:3036 (``the $\beta_0$ conditional theorem did not
pass the acid test'').}

\subsection{Why rows 10--12 are a single statement}

Rows 10--12 die of quantifier and identity errors rather than of estimates, and they are the rows
most likely to be re-proposed. Row 10: the sign of $G''(a)$ is a \emph{projective} property
($G''(0;tv)=t^{2}G''(0;v)$ for $t\ne0$, an exact quadratic homogeneity along rays), so any
statement about it must fix the normalisation and the point; the uniform-in-$a_0$ form is false,
and the corpus says so explicitly in the v2 register. \LT{}/\LN{} Row 11: $S_W\equiv\beta$ on the
slice is a statement about a \emph{value}; $G$ is a derivative off the slice, and conflating them
is the single most expensive error of the wave --- it produced a reported $S\equiv D$ that would
have made $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ look \emph{positively} hopeless for the wrong reason. Row 12: an
identity cannot be a target. $S-D\equiv G$ makes ``$D'\le S'$'' literally ``$G'\ge0$'', and since
the third cumulants are $17\%$ and $37\%$ of the derivatives, no two-variable closure exists.

\subsection{The ledger of the dead}

Twelve routes, each with a number, and no route in the coarse-mode family survives them: the
analytic ones die on direction or constant, the decompositions die on neutrality, the tool
families die on the acid test, and the shortcuts die on quantifiers. This is the sense in which
\S\ref{sec:obs1}--\S\ref{sec:obs2} are a \emph{verdict} rather than a setback: the route was not
abandoned for lack of effort or lack of ideas, it was closed by its own internal accounting. The
correct object, together with what is actually known about it, is \S\ref{sec:target}.
