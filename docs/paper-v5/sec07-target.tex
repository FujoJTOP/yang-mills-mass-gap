\section[The correct target: the pole residue is one step away]{The correct target: $\fone$ is the pole residue, and it is one step away}
\label{sec:target}

\subsection{The object, stated once and without aliases}

If the route of \S\ref{sec:obs1}--\S\ref{sec:obs2} is closed, the question becomes what the
corpus's own strongest unconditional theorem is actually about. It is about a residue. With
$O=\tfrac14\tr F^{2}$ the $0^{++}$ composite operator of the $A_{1g}$ channel,
\begin{equation}
\fone:=\nu_O(\{E_1\})=\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{E_1t}C(t)
=\bigl|\langle\Omega|O|\mathbb{G}\rangle\bigr|^{2}\ \ge\ 0,
\qquad
C(t)=\int e^{-Et}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_O(E),\quad \nu_O\ge0,
\label{eq:f1def5}
\end{equation}
equivalently the residue of the $O$-channel return Green function at its lowest pole,
$\fone=-\lambda_1\operatorname{Res}_{z=z_1}\mathcal{G}(z)
=-(1/z_1)\,N_O(z_1)/\mathcal{D}_O'(z_1)$ with $\mathcal{D}_O(z)=\det(1-zT_O)$ and
$z_1=e^{am_1}$ the smallest-modulus zero. \LT{} It is worth saying which residue it is
\emph{not}, because the corpus contains all the near misses: it is not the residue of the gluon
propagator, not that of a Savvidy or free propagator, and not that of a Wilson loop. It is the
atom of the spectral measure of one specific composite operator.

\subsection{The four implications that are actually proved}

The reason $\fone$ is the correct object is not aesthetic: it is that four implications from it
are theorems, and each of them is a statement one would want. \LT{}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I-1)] $\fone>0\Rightarrow\Var_\Omega(O)\ge\fone>0$, since $\nu_O\ge0$ and
  $C(0)=\int\mathrm{d}\nu_O$. This is an identity, and the converse fails: for free Maxwell
  $\Var>0$ while $\nu$ is a zero-threshold continuum, i.e.\ $\fone=\nu(\{0\})=0$.
\item[(I-2)] $\fone>0\Rightarrow C(t)\ge\fone e^{-E_1t}$ for all $t>0$. Note what kind of
  statement this is: a \emph{one-sided floor}, with no upper bound and no rate.
\item[(I-3)] $\fone>0$ \emph{together with} I2 ($\Lstar>m_1^{2}$) is \emph{equivalent} to sharp
  double-exponential decay, $C(t)=\fone e^{-m_1t}(1+\eta(t))$ with $|\eta|\le Be^{-\delta t}$.
  I2 is a hypothesis here, and it is precisely the hypothesis that is missing.
\item[(I-4)] In the strong-coupling regime $\beta<\beta_1=\min(1/48,c/N)$ at fixed lattice spacing
  $a$ one has $\fone\ge r_0(\beta)>0$ uniformly in $L$ (modulo Osterwalder--Seiler /
  K\"all\'en--Lehmann), with the mechanism being the four-wall tube: $|P|\ge4t/a$,
  $m_1=4\ln(1/u)/a$, positive amplitude, and a Koteck\'y--Preiss remainder $O(u)$ uniform in
  $t,V,L$.
\end{enumerate}
What (I-4) is \emph{not} is equally important, and the corpus states it: it contains neither I2,
nor the continuum limit $a\to0$, nor the crossing to $\beta\approx2.5$, and it is not a gap
theorem --- its own words are ``residue theorem, not a gap theorem''. Also, on the periodic torus
$T^4_L$ the statement is ill-posed, because the propagator is bounded and $\lim_{t\to\infty}$ does
not exist; the correct geometry is the cylinder $\mathcal{C}_L=T^3_L\times\mathbb{R}$, and the
corpus records the platform drift as an erratum-level notation slip.

\subsection{The distance to $m>0$ is exactly one step}

Here is the sentence that replaces the whole of \S\ref{sec:obs1}--\S\ref{sec:obs2}:
\begin{equation}
\Bigl(\fone>0\Bigr)\ \wedge\ \Bigl(\Lstar>m_1^{2}\Bigr)
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{sharp double exponential}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{an isolated lowest atom},
\label{eq:onestep5}
\end{equation}
and the missing conjunct is I2: the isolation of the bottom of the support of $\nu_O$, i.e.\ a
positive distance between the lowest atom and the rest of the measure. \LT{}/ \LM{} This is not
an inequality gap; it is a structural fact about $\operatorname{supp}\nu_O$ that neither
$\Gpp>0$ nor $H1$ nor any statement in the curvature family contains. The corpus's own bookkeeping
calls I2 the Clay-previous level, and it is the second half of $(CP)=I1\wedge I2$.

Two supporting facts make clear that transport cannot substitute for I2. First, the multiplicative
residue transport $\fone(\mu)=Z_O(\mu,\mu_0)^{2}\fone(\mu_0)$ is exact and it transports the
\emph{sign and positivity} of the residue; it transports neither the size nor the isolation,
because a pointwise positive scalar multiplication cannot move the support of $\nu$. \LT{}/ \LC{}
Second, the residue--level-gap lemma runs in the direction $\Lstar>\bar R\Rightarrow\fone>0$ and
fails in reverse: a positive atom with a heavy tail can have $\bar R>\Lstar$. \LT{}

\subsection{Why the curvature cannot reach it: an exact counterexample}

The relation between the old object and the new one is settled by a number, not by an argument:
at $\beta=0$ one has $G''=0.2688$ while $\fone=0$. \LT{} (quoted: the corpus flags this pair as
proved by quotation, its generating run not repeated --- \code{T02-F1SQ.md} \S6). The corpus also
records the reason the attempt cannot be repaired by adding a constant: the thin-atom proposition,
which would say that a positive curvature floor forces the bottom of the K\"all\'en--Lehmann support
to be an atom, has been machine-checked \emph{false} (\code{H1_does_not_imply_atom}); this is
\LT{} as reported in \code{ch15-uvface.tex}, and the Lean file itself was not re-opened in this
round (\code{T02-F1SQ.md} \S6). A second, independent instance is 2D Yang--Mills, where
$\delta=G''(0)\ge2.01>0$ holds uniformly in $t$ while $\fone(\tr F^{2})\equiv0$ exactly. \LT{} At
most, a positive curvature floor supplies a susceptibility ceiling $\chi_{k=0}\le1/c_0$, and even a
proven $H1$ would not force the bottom of the support.

\subsection{The verdict on ``one object, several names''}

The programme's charter lists $\Gpp>0$, the transport of $\fone$, $H1$, $(CP)$ and the gluon
condensate as ``the same object under several names''. On the \LT{} level that sentence is false,
and the split is exactly the two rows of the table above: a \emph{static/susceptibility family}
$\{\Gpp>0,\ H1,\langle\tfrac14\tr F^{2}\rangle\}$ and a \emph{spectral/atom family}
$\{\fone>0,\ (CP)\}$. Within the first family the identity $H1(\delta)\iff\Gpp\ge\delta$ is exact,
so $\Gpp\ge0$ is the $\delta=0$ member and is strictly weaker than the physical point. Across the
families nothing is proved; the only bridge in the corpus is the residue--level-gap lemma, whose
hypothesis is I2. \LC{} Hence the correct statement of the key is not $H1$ but
\begin{equation}
\text{key}\ =\ H1\ +\ \text{``the bottom of the support is an atom''}\ =\ (CP)\ =\ I1\wedge I2 ,
\label{eq:key5}
\end{equation}
and $H1$ alone is strictly weaker than the atom statement, as the machine-checked counterexample
shows.

\subsection{What would make it \LT: two revival conditions, stated as tasks}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(b)$\to$(a).} Supply a uniform lower bound $\Lstar\ge m_1^{2}+c$ for the support
  separation, i.e.\ upgrade the Koteck\'y--Preiss remainder from $t$-uniform $O(u)$ to
  $O(e^{-\delta t})$: a second exponential scale. The strong-coupling integer grading of (I-4)
  cannot do this structurally --- the remainder is $O(u)$ for every $t$ by construction.
\item \textbf{(c)$\to$equivalence.} Build a bridge that puts $\Gpp$ (a static curvature of the
  field's coarse mode) and $\nu_O$ (a spectral atom of a composite operator) into one object. The
  only candidate in the corpus is the thin-atom proposition, which is machine-checked false, so
  the bridge must change species; no constant added to $H1$ can produce it.
\end{enumerate}
Both conditions are stated so that failure is decidable, which is the most a paper of this kind
can honestly offer. Neither is claimed here.

$O(e^{-\delta t})$).}

\subsection{What this section establishes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \LT{} $\fone$ is the atom of $\nu_O$ at $E_1$, equivalently the residue of the $O$-channel
      Green function at $z_1=e^{am_1}$; four implications (I-1)--(I-4) are proved from it.
\item \LT{} The distance from $\fone>0$ to $m>0$ is exactly one condition, I2
      ($\Lstar>m_1^{2}$), which is structural --- about the support of a measure, not the size of
      an inequality.
\item \LT{} $\Gpp>0$ does not imply $\fone>0$: at $\beta=0$, $G''=0.2688$ while $\fone=0$; in 2D,
      $\delta\ge2.01>0$ while $\fone\equiv0$.
\item \LM{} The key must be written $(CP)=I1\wedge I2$, not $H1$; the two revival conditions are
      stated above and neither is met.
\end{itemize}
