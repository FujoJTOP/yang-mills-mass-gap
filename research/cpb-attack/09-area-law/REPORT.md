# Method 9 — area law / positive string tension ⟹ gap

> CPB-attack probe. Verdict: **DEAD as a standalone route.**

## Rigorous area law at β≈2.5 (4D SU(3)): NO

- Osterwalder–Seiler 1978: strong coupling only.
- Borgs–Seiler 1983: widest explicit published range `β < 12/17 ≈ 0.706`.
- Rémondière (computer-assisted, SU(2), unreviewed): `β ≤ 0.813`, planar loops.
- CNS25 / arXiv:2509.04688: "certain regime" / 't Hooft regime (large-N, fixed 't
  Hooft coupling) — not the finite-N crossover.

Crossover SU(3): `β = 2N/g² ≈ 2.5` (`λ = h^∨g² ≈ 7.3`) — **~3.5× beyond the best
explicit range (0.706)** and outside the large-N regime.

## Area law ⟹ gap: conditional, not automatic

The area law constrains one observable (Wilson loop); the spectral gap constrains all
correlations. A route exists via the Durhuus–Fröhlich mass-gap condition (used in
2509.04688), but it requires the area law at the coupling in question.

## Verdict

**Dead as a standalone CPB route.** It needs a rigorous area law at `β≈2.5` — i.e.,
confinement at the crossover — as hard as the gap. **Needs X:** area law for 4D SU(3)
beyond Borgs–Seiler's `β≈0.71`.

## Honest boundary

Reach assessment, no new proof. Unreviewed all-coupling claims (Tomboulis; Zenodo
roadmaps) not accepted.
