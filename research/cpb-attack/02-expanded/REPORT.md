# Method 2 — expanded convergence regimes

> CPB-attack probe. Verdict: **DEAD for the (finite-N) crossover.**

## Works and their exact hypotheses

- **Cao–Nissim–Sheffield, *Expanded regimes of area law for lattice Yang–Mills***
  (arXiv:2505.16585, → Ann. Probab.), Thm 1.2: `G=U(N)`, all `d≥2`, area law for all
  `β ≤ β₀(d)`, all `N≥1`. But the **proof** requires `N ≥ 10¹⁰ d¹⁰` and
  `β ≤ 10⁻¹⁰ d^{d−1}` — ≈ **6×10⁻⁸ at d=4** — with Remark 2.8: "β tiny, B huge, N even
  larger". So the expansion is **in N**, not in β (vs OS78's `β < c_d/N²`).
- Companions: *Dynamical approach to area law* (arXiv:2509.04688, 't Hooft regime);
  *U(N) in the 't Hooft regime* (arXiv:2510.22788, mass gap + uniqueness, large N).

## Structural ingredient

The master loop equation (Makeenko–Migdal) as a linear inhomogeneous equation + an a
priori bound; the merger term via a truncated model. The win is **large-N
factorization**, **not** a larger β radius.

## Reach at the crossover

SU(3), `β≈2.47`, `λ≈7.3`. β is **~10⁸ above** CNS's `β₀`; N=3 ≪ 10¹⁶. Not reached by
orders of magnitude.

## Verdict

**DEAD for the finite-N crossover.** "Expanded" = expanded in **N at astronomically
small β**, not to `β≈2.5`. Breaking it would need finite-N, intermediate-β control of
the loop-equation merger terms (no factorization) — absent.

## Honest boundary

A reach assessment from the source hypotheses, not a mass-gap proof.
