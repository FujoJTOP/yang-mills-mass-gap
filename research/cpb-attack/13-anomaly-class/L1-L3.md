# Anomaly route — the three missing lemmas L1–L3

> CPB-attack probe. Verdict: **does not close; three lemmas remain.**

## Source correction

`hep-th/0305200` is Nikolov–Stanev–Todorov, *"Globally conformal invariant gauge field
theory with rational correlation functions"*, Nucl. Phys. B670 (2003) 373–400. It is
**not** a no-go theorem: it classifies the 4-point function of a dim-4 scalar `L` under
Wightman positivity **assuming** (4.1): no dim-2 scalar (`a₀=0`) and no dim-4 scalar
(`2b₁+b₂=0`). Positivity gives `−3a′ ≤ b ≤ a′/3` (eq 4.5), so `a′=0 ⟹ b=0` ⟹ free
Maxwell. The authors make this conditional.

## L1 (`2n`-point ⟹ `a′=0`): hard-open

The paper writes "If the 6-point function analysis gives `a′=0`, then…" — it is **not
done**; d=4 6-point has **31,990 structures** (vs 8 in d=2). The 2005 follow-up
(`hep-th/0504146`, NPB 722) builds partial-wave machinery but does **not** settle 6-point
positivity ("fully explicit 6-point partial waves in 4D remained open"). Not one-more-step.

## L2 (`a₀>0` branch): open

Dropping (4.1) gives `−6a′ ≤ b ≤ 3(a₀+a′/2)` (eq 4.6), permitting `b≠0` at `a′=0`; an open
region of the 5-parameter space survives. Known composites (YM curvature, quartic
`L_ξη`, induced YM connection) **are** excluded (Remark 4.2) by an induced d=2 scalar; the
general region is not.

## L3 (single-`L` reduction): ansatz, likely false for non-abelian

(4.1) is an *assumption* motivated by abelian odd-point vanishing. For non-abelian
`⟨FFF⟩≠0 ⟹ ⟨LLL⟩≠0`, so `2b₁+b₂=0` "appears not to follow from locality and conformal
invariance alone"; the required OPE field sits in an **indecomposable** representation
("unpractical", non-perturbative). The framework's key assumption is abelian-motivated and
**questionable for non-abelian YM**.

## Bottom line

Does not close. **Three lemmas remain** (L1 hard-open, L2 open, L3 structurally
questionable). **L3 is decisive:** the ESI setup may not apply to non-abelian YM without
indecomposable representations. The anomaly route is further from closing than "one lemma".
