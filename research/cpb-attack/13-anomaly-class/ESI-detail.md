# ESI no-go: what exactly is missing

> The `esi1335` result is **published** (arXiv hep-th/0305200, Nikolov–Stanev–Todorov
> 2003), but it is **conditional** and does **not** close the anomaly route.

## The four-point result

It classifies gauge-invariant **four-point** functions of a dimension-4 scalar `L` (a
gauge-field Lagrangian) under Wightman positivity, **assuming** the `L L` OPE contains no
dimension-2/4 scalars (their eq. (5.1), pp. 6–8). Positivity for twists 4 and 6 gives
`−3a′ ≤ b ≤ (1/3)a′`; hence **`a′=0` forces `b=0`**, leaving only a multiple of the free
abelian (Maxwell) four-point function. The authors note this would "confirm the general
belief that a (pure) non-abelian gauge theory necessarily involves a mass gap, thus
violating conformal invariance."

## It does not close

The authors themselves call it indirect. A genuine no-go needs the **`2n`-point
(`n≥3`) positivity analysis to yield `a′=0`**, and the **`a₀>0` / dimension-4-scalar
branch is not excluded** — an open region of the 5-parameter projective space survives.
So an interacting non-free 4D CFT is **not** ruled out by the four-point analysis alone.

## Discrete vs continuous 1-form

Irrelevant to *this* argument — it is conformal/Wightman positivity, not anomaly matching.
The `Z_N`-vs-continuous issue bears only on the separate anomaly-matching route.

## Verdict: OPEN, not closed

Precise missing results:

- **(L1)** `2n`-point positivity ⟹ `a′=0`;
- **(L2)** exclude the `a₀>0` scalar branch (or show pure YM forces triviality);
- **(L3)** justify reducing pure-YM correlators to a single dim-4 `L` with that OPE
  structure.

No counterexample is known, but the source's own interacting branch remains unexcluded.

## Boundary

A conditional four-point result (published), **not** a no-go theorem. The earlier
"strongest evidence" framing overstated it.

Sources: ESI 1335 / arXiv hep-th/0305200 (Nikolov–Stanev–Todorov);
Hofman–Iqbal arXiv:1802.09512 (continuous 1-form theorem).
