# PROOF-TRANSPORT-ANALYTIC — `f₁²(β)` is real-analytic across `1/48` wherever the `0⁺⁺` is simple; the wall is nonvanishing, not analyticity

> 2026-11-23 · Attempt to transport the strong-coupling residue `f₁²≥r₀>0` (P3, `β<1/48`) to `β≈2.5` by analytic continuation, testing whether `1/48` is a series radius, not a spectral one. **Verdict: the hypothesis is half-right. `f₁²(β)=⟨Ω|O P₁(β)O|Ω⟩=|a(β)|²` is real-analytic in `β` wherever the lightest `0⁺⁺` eigenvalue `λ₁` is simple and isolated (Kato), and the Wilson transfer matrix is *entire* in `β` — so `1/48` does not bound analyticity and Kato continues `f₁²` straight across it. But analyticity transports the **value**, not the **sign**: the massive free scalar has a simple, isolated lightest `0⁺⁺` and still `f₁²(:φ²:)=0` by a `Z₂` (particle-number parity) selection rule. Analyticity + gap **⇏** atom. The unproved input is (K2): plaquette–glueball overlap `a(β)≠0` on `[1/48,2.5]`, i.e. the `0⁺⁺` stays a genuine bound atom, not dissolved into the `(gg)` cut.**

**Setup.** Fixed finite torus `Λ=T⁴_L`, spacing `a`, Wilson action; `T_β=e^{−aH_β}` compact self-adjoint, discrete spectrum `1=λ₀>λ₁≥…`, mass `m₁=−(1/a)ln λ₁`. `O=N⁻¹Re tr U_p` projected to `0⁺⁺`; with `P₁(β)=|G⟩⟨G|` the lightest projector, `f₁²(β)=⟨Ω|O P₁(β)O|Ω⟩=|a(β)|²`, `a(β)=⟨G(β)|O|Ω⟩`.

**1. Analyticity (Kato).** Each Wilson factor is `e^{−β·c}`, `c∈[0,2]` real, so the kernel is *entire* in `β`; `T_β` is a holomorphic family of compact self-adjoint operators. Kato's analytic perturbation theory (Ch. VII §3): if `λ₁(β₀)` is simple and isolated, then `λ₁(β)` and the spectral projection `P₁(β)=∮(z−T_β)^{−1}dz/2πi` are real-analytic about `β₀`. With `Ω,O` `β`-independent, `a(β)` and `f₁²=a²` are real-analytic, `≥0`. Base point: at `β₀<1/48` P3 gives `f₁²≥r₀>0`, and L1 gives simplicity/isolation; the Kato neighbourhood is `L`-uniform whenever the gap and level spacing `m₂−m₁` are, which SZZ/P3 supply there.

**2. Continuation across `1/48`; the finite radius.** `1/48` is the Ursell/cluster-series convergence radius and never enters Kato — the entire kernel carries analyticity arbitrarily far. The *only* singularities of `f₁²` are those of `P₁(β)`: the first `β*` where `λ₁` loses simplicity — a `0⁺⁺` **level crossing** (`λ₁=λ₂`, generically a square-root branch point of the rank-one projector), or a **dissolution** `m₁(β)→2m_g` where the discrete pole merges into the `(gg)` cut and the atom weight `→0`. The real-axis radius extends to the first such `β*`; complex crossings bound the disk sooner. So the radius is set by the *level structure / crossover*, **not** `1/48`. Whether `β*>2.5` is glueball stability through the smooth crossover `β_c≈2.47` — open.

**3. The crux — free scalar.** Massive free scalar: gapped transfer matrix; the lightest `0⁺⁺` is the elementary `φ` at mass `m`, **simple and isolated**. Yet `f₁²(:φ²:)=|⟨1\text{-part}|:φ²:|Ω⟩|²=0`: `:φ²:` is even while `|1\text{-part}⟩` is odd under `φ→−φ`, and `T` commutes with that `Z₂`, so `a≡0` for *all* `β`. (`O=φ` instead gives `f₁²=Z>0`.) Exact counterexample: a simple isolated eigenvalue of the resolvent may have **zero coupling**. Analyticity propagates whatever value `a(β)` carries, including identically `0`.

**4. What YM has, and where it can still fail.** In YM the plaquette `O` and the lightest `0⁺⁺` glueball share *every* symmetry (gauge, `P`, `C`, cubic `A1g`); there is **no** selection rule, so `a(β)≢0` — identical vanishing, the free-scalar mode, is excluded. But `a≢0` with `a(β₀)≠0` and `a` real-analytic only forces the zero set of `a` to be **discrete**: `f₁²=a²>0` off a measure-zero set of *overlap zeros* `β_a` (where `f₁²` touches `0` quadratically), and `f₁²` ends at a dissolution `β*`. The non-accidental failure is dissolution — the `0⁺⁺` ceasing to be an atom (`m₁→2m_g`): the free-scalar mechanism (lightest coupling to a continuum) realized dynamically rather than by symmetry.

**Transport theorem (conditional).** Suppose on `I=[1/48,2.5]`: **(K1)** `λ₁` simple isolated, `m₂−m₁>0` and `m₁<2m_g`, uniform; **(K2)** `a(β)≠0`. Then `f₁²(β)=a(β)²>0` on `I`, real-analytic, by Kato continuation from the P3 base point. (K1)=gap + glueball below threshold; (K2)=plaquette–glueball overlap nonvanishing. Both are *dynamics*; neither follows from analyticity. Drop (K2): the best honest statement is `f₁²≥0`, positive off a discrete zero set, with possible dissolution at `β*`.

**Sharpest line.** The `1/48` wall *is* dissolved for analyticity — the entire kernel and Kato continue `f₁²` across it, refuting "series radius = spectral barrier." But analyticity is transport of a *value*, and the free scalar proves a simple isolated `0⁺⁺` residue can be exactly `0` (a `Z₂` selection rule). YM's rescue is the *absence* of that symmetry (`a≢0`), upgrading "identically zero" to "zeros only on a discrete, unexcluded set plus a possible atom→cut dissolution." The exact open input is uniform nonvanishing of the plaquette–glueball overlap through the crossover: the atom's *existence* — the dynamical residue problem itself, not analyticity.
