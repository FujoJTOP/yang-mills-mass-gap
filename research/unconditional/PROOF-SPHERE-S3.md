# PROOF-SPHERE-S3 — fuzzy-sphere / S³ truncation: rigorous finite-mode gap, not the mass gap

> 2026-11-23 · Fresh-framework probe on H1 (the Clay gap). **Verdict. Three answers, exact.** (1) The fuzzy-sphere truncation is *rigorous as a finite-mode matrix QM*: at fixed radius R and matrix size N_f the Hamiltonian has discrete spectrum and a certified gap; but *untruncated* YM on S³×ℝ is the same unconstructed 4D continuum object as on the lattice, and "S³ compact ⇒ gap" is false — compactness discretizes only the *free* modes at ~c/R, which →0. (2) There is no rigorous R→∞ bridge: the one clean implication, [Δ(R)≥m>0 uniformly in R ⇒ ℝ⁴ gap m], has the Clay problem as its antecedent; finite-R S³ is a different manifold with no exact map to ℝ⁴. (3) Genuinely different in *structure, not in wall*: the real gains (exact SO(4), finite matrix integral, gauge-invariant states) are computational; the decisive non-differences (finite-mode gap = upper bound; removing N_f and R→∞ are the same UV-continuum and IR-volume walls) are exact, and the noncommutative route *adds* UV/IR mixing.

**1. What is rigorous — the finite model only.** YM on S³×ℝ truncated by a fuzzy-sphere / finite-matrix regulator (Madore; Hoppe; Aoki–Iso–Kawai–Kitazawa–Tada) is a finite-dimensional matrix QM in the BFSS/BMN family (Banks–Fischler–Shenker–Susskind; Berenstein–Maldacena–Nastase). Its gap Δ_{N_f}(R) is a finite symmetric-eigenvalue problem, certifiable by the same Kato/interval machines as the finite lattice (`PROOF-COMPUTER-ASSISTED`): a true theorem, "for fixed (R,N_f), Δ>0." It is manifestly gauge-invariant (Gauss law, no gauge fixing) — no Gribov copies, but the lattice *Hamiltonian* has none either, so that is not a sphere-specific gain. But the interacting continuum S³×ℝ theory is not constructed: 4D YM is open (Jaffe–Witten; Seiler), and only the cutoff model is a rigorous object.

**2. Compactness is not a gap.** b₁(S³)=0 with Ric>0 forbids harmonic 1-forms (Bochner), so the coexact Hodge Laplacian has a positive gap ~c/R² — free Maxwell's lightest mode, ~c/R, which →0 as R→∞ and which a *massless* theory has too. This free finite-volume threshold is not the dynamical mass m. Conflating them is the whole error: 2026 unrefereed Zenodo preprints assert exactly this √3/R "threshold" as the mass gap, plus a Mosco-convergence R→∞ limit — the threshold is the free gap and the limit *is* the open problem. For a gapped theory Δ(R)→m with Lüscher corrections ~e^{−mR} (Lüscher), so a finite-R number is an *upper* bound on m (Δ(R)≥m), never a lower one without uniform control.

**3. The R→∞ limit is the mass gap.** S³_R is locally ℝ³ at scales ≪R, so one *expects* local observables to converge to ℝ⁴ YM; the clean logical statement is [uniform-in-(N_f,R) gap ⇒ ℝ⁴ gap m] via OS reconstruction + uniform clustering (Osterwalder–Seiler). Proving that uniformity *is* the mass gap; the R→∞ limit needs tightness of the S³ family plus uniform continuity of clustering — neither known in 4D. The sphere relabels the two limits; it does not remove them.

**4. Genuinely different? — the exact obstruction.** Gains over the lattice: exact rotational symmetry (no preferred axes) and a clean finite matrix integral. But (a) the finite-mode gap is an upper bound (Δ_{ℝ⁴}≤Δ_{N_f}); (b) the fuzzy→commutative limit N_f→∞ is uncontrolled past perturbation theory and suffers UV/IR mixing (Minwalla–Van Raamsdonk–Seiberg; Steinacker) — a *new* hazard the lattice lacks; (c) R→∞ is the volume wall. Pure YM also forfeits the maximal SUSY that makes the BMN S³ matrix model (N=4) analytically controllable (Kim–Klose–Plefka; cf. Cabo-Bizet's discrete→continuum N=4 spectrum). **Exact obstruction:** a uniform-in-(N_f,R) lower bound = the mass gap; plus UV/IR mixing for N_f→∞.

**Sharpest line.** The S³/fuzzy-sphere truncation is the cleanest *finite* gauge model here — discrete matrix-QM spectrum, exact SO(4), no Gribov copies — and proves a true theorem: for fixed (R,N_f) the gap is positive and certifiable. But "S³ compact ⇒ gap" mistakes the free threshold c/R (→0) for the dynamical mass, the fuzzy cutoff is a UV wall with its own UV/IR mixing, and R→∞ is the mass gap in spherical coordinates: it moves the wall, adds an obstruction, and proves only an upper bound.
