# RANDOM-CLUSTER — FK / random-surface representation and the geometric gap criterion

> 2026-11-23 · **Verdict: an FK-type positive representation exists exactly for abelian gauge theory, and is sign-killed for continuous non-abelian SU(N).** The mass gap ⟺ subcritical-percolation criterion is a *theorem* for Z₂/Z_N (via Wegner duality + Fortuin–Kasteleyn) and *undefined* for SU(N): the surface measure it needs is not positive. Distinct from the cluster expansion (a small-β series) and the Perron/reflection-positivity route (transfer-matrix PSD); this is a *geometric coupling* with a connectivity order parameter whose positivity can fail locally.

**What is constructible (abelian).** For G=Z₂ and U(1) the gauge-string surface expansion of Wilson loops has **all surface weights positive** (Brydges–Giffen–Durhuus–Fröhlich, NPB 275 (1986) 459): the flux-sheet sum is a genuine positive Gibbs measure. Z₂ gauge in d=4 is Wegner-dual to the 4D Ising model, whose FK/Edwards–Sokal random-cluster coupling (q=2) *is* the percolation measure; U(1)₄ is dual to a positive integer-2-form random surface. The order parameter is theorem-level in 3D: Z₂-gauge excited-plaquette loops and their FK clusters percolate exactly at the Ising T_c (Agrawal et al., PRL 135 (2025) 120601).

**The criterion, precisely.** Duality renders gauge correlators as dual-spin correlators; Edwards–Sokal gives ⟨σ₀σₓ⟩ = FK connection probability up to normalization (Grimmett). Hence, rigorously, on the confining (dual high-T) side:
$$\text{mass gap at rate } m \iff \text{dual FK subcritical},\quad P_\beta(0\!\leftrightarrow\! x)\le e^{-m|x|},\ \ m=-\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\tfrac1{|x|}\log P_\beta(0\!\leftrightarrow\! x),$$
with sharp threshold β_c ↔ β*_c. This is the cleanest "gap ⟺ subcritical percolation" instance, and it is *not* a conjecture.

**The exact non-abelian obstruction.** For SU(N) the all-β surface/map sum (Cao–Park–Sheffield, arXiv:2307.06790, Comm. AMS) has edge weights equal to the **Weingarten function**, which is signed: for SU(2) a surface's sign is its **Euler characteristic** (Brydges et al.); for SU(N) nearly half the Weingarten weights are negative (Unger). Individual surface weights take both signs, so the surface sum is **not a positive measure** — no Gibbs/FKG structure, no Edwards–Sokal coupling, hence no connection probability to be subcritical. Available positivity is only *aggregate*: Z_{SU(N)}>0 trivially, and Unger's manifestly-positive f_λ²M_λ(σ)M_λ(τ⁻¹) rewriting is a bilinear (positive-definite-kernel) positivity of the *summed* series, not a non-negative weight on surfaces. Continuous symmetry compounds this: a continuous group admits no finite-q random-cluster, and its high-temperature analog — the loop/surface (O(n)-type) expansion — carries the same signed Weingarten vertices for SU(N).

**The β≈2.5 attempt fails at the same wall.** SU(2) β≈2.5 (SU(3) ≈5.7) is the crossover — the non-abelian signed regime. The positive FK route gives a rigorous gap only at strong coupling / dual high-T (abelian); reaching the scaling window needs a sign-cancelling positive measure, i.e. the activity-radius barrier of W2 (proved radius ≈1/48 ≪ 2.5). The abelian percolation gap lives where the weights are positive; β≈2.5 does not.

**Exact missing lemma.** A sign-cancelling regrouping of the SU(N≥3, d=4) surface sum into a positive Gibbs measure with FKG/Edwards–Sokal whose connection probabilities equal the OS two-point function. Done only for d=2, N=∞ ([BCS25], cited in Cao–Park–Sheffield); open in d=4. The Euler-characteristic / Weingarten signs obstruct *per-surface* positivity, so this is not a small-activity accident but the sign problem in geometric form.

**Sharpest statement.** FK percolation gives a rigorous mass-gap criterion for lattice gauge theory precisely when the surface weights are positive — i.e. abelian. For SU(N) the criterion is not merely unproved but has **no positive measure to be stated about**: the known surface expansion is signed at the local (Weingarten/Euler-characteristic) level, and a positive regrouping is exactly the open sign problem.
