# OUT — Certified computation (4CT-style) of a β≈2.5 lattice gap

**Verdict. Split, and the split is the answer.** (A) A rigorous interval-arithmetic proof that a *specific finite* SU(2) lattice at β=2.5 has gap ≥ m_L>0 is feasible in principle, and genuinely outside the analytic-framework war — but it yields **Δ_{R⁴} ≤ Δ_L, an *upper* bound** (P4 §2). (B) The "proven finite-size criterion to extend in volume" that would turn it into a lower bound does not exist without the gap: every *proven* volume-uniform lower-bound machine needs the gap as input. So the composite proves nothing about the continuum; it meets the **same wall at the extension step**, relabelled.

## A — the finite computation is feasible and non-framework
Certify a finite-volume transfer-matrix gap at β=2.5 by a *validated* eigenvalue enclosure (interval / Kato–Temple dual bounds, or a certified SDP relaxation) on the regulated Hamiltonian, plus a truncation tail bound (a Deift-type relative bound from the SU(2) Laplacian on each link). This is a closed finite-dimensional statement: no RG, no Savvidy point, no crossover, no cluster expansion. It is the one artifact in this whole program that the "10-method" map does not already contain as a failure mode. **Largest feasible L ≈ 4, maybe 6:** the gauge-invariant Hilbert space grows ~exp(c·L³) (L=4: 192 links, tens of DOF each, ~10³⁰⁰). Exhaustive enumeration is hopeless; a *structured* enclosure is not — but only at tiny L.

**Two things then kill its relevance.** (i) **Direction.** Δ_{R⁴}=inf_L Δ_L, so a certified *lower* bound on Δ_L is an *upper* bound on Δ_{R⁴}; Δ_L *decreases* toward the answer. Even a perfect, arbitrarily large certified lattice never gives Δ_{R⁴}≥m. (ii) **Scale.** ATOM shows the transverse k→0 turnover needs L≈24–32 (plateau seen only at 64⁴–128⁴, Cucchieri–Mendes); at L=4–6 the k→0 mode is completely unprobed. The feasible certificate and the physics sit ~4× apart in linear size, and bigger L would not help the direction anyway.

## B — the extrapolation needs the gap (same wall)
The premise "a proven finite-size criterion to extend in volume" is where this dies. The only proven volume-uniform-lower-bound machine is Nachtergaele's martingale criterion; its input (ii), *local gap stability*, is provably **equivalent to the k→0 transverse dynamical mass — i.e. to the gap** (IDEA-finite-size). At β=2.5 it fails; rigorous strong-coupling bounds (Dobrushin; Shen–Zhu–Zhu) reach only β<1/48. Quantitatively, any criterion carrying a residual ε(J) after J block-scales has ε(J) set by the marginal mode (energy ~(2π/2^J)²); bounding it *below* needs it gapped — circular. This is exactly P4 §5: **L\* holds iff YM is gapped.** So extrapolation does not "extend," it presupposes.

## The 4CT analogy breaks
4CT reduces an infinite class to ~10³ checkable configurations via a *finite* combinatorial lemma. Here the "cases" are L→∞ across *all* boundary conditions — infinitely many and **not finitely reducible**, because the obstruction is scale-invariant: a massless mode sits at momentum ~2π/L at *every* L. There is no finite case analysis to run.

**Sharpest line.** A hardware-verified finite-lattice gap is real, publishable, and genuinely outside the framework paradigm — but it is an *upper* bound (Δ_{R⁴}≤Δ_L), and the only way to convert it touches the gap. It hits the same wall at the volume step, wearing numerical clothes.
