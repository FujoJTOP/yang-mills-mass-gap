# G2F-ATOM-PLUS-ISOLATION — one holomorphic datum carries both faces: the first Fredholm zero is the atom *and* its residue at once, acid-clean — but it sits *on* the orbit-series convergence edge, and the far side is the state-density `I2` itself; no single argument closes

> 2026-11-23 · Target: a single argument giving `I1∧I2` **together** for the `0⁺⁺` (`tr F²`) channel, i.e. `ν = f₁²δ_{m₁²} + ν_rest`, `supp ν_rest ⊂ [Λ,∞)`, `0<m₁²<Λ` — atom (positive residue) and isolation in one stroke. **判词 (verdict, up front): the single *object* exists and is sharp — the spectral/Fredholm determinant `D_O(z)=det(1−zT_O)` has its smallest zero at `z₁=e^{am₁}` and residue `f₁²=N_O(z₁)`, so *location (I2) and weight (I1) are one holomorphic datum*, and it passes the free-field acid test cleanly. It is NOT a single *proof*: `z₁` is the radius of convergence of the orbit series `−Σ_k z^k/k Tr T_O^k`, so separating `z₁` from the next zero needs `D_O` past its own edge, where the only bound is Hadamard `∏(1+|z|λ_n)=exp(O(u^{−1/2}))`, swamping the Rouché margin; the far side *is* the self-energy/state-density that `I2` names. Scorecard 2/5.**

**1 · The single object, and why both faces collapse into it.** `C(t)=Σ_n f_n²e^{−aE_nt}`, atom `f₁²=|⟨g|O|ω⟩|²`, `T_O` the transfer operator compressed to the `O`-cyclic subspace. The resolvent `G(z)=Σ_t C(t)z^t=⟨ω|O(1−zT_O)^{−1}O|ω⟩=N_O/D_O`, `D_O=det(1−zT_O)`. Then **exactly** (finite `T⁴_L`, every `L`):
`f₁² = −λ₁·lim_{z→z₁}(z−z₁)G(z) = −N_O(z₁)/(z₁D_O'(z₁)) = N_O(z₁)` (at a simple zero, `D_O'(z₁)=−λ₁`).
A **simple zero** of `D_O` at `z₁` is, by definition, an *isolated* eigenvalue `λ₁` — that is `I2`; and `N_O(z₁)≠0` gives the *positive residue* `f₁²>0` — that is `I1`. By the **residue–level-gap lemma** (`f₁²≥(ΛM₀−M₁)/(Λ−m₁²)`, equality iff the rest is one atom at `Λ`) `I1⟺I2` given moments — but here we do not even trade: one zero gives *both*. With the exact threshold `Λ=(1.5m₁)²` (the C/P-blind excited `0⁺⁺*`, PROOF-ISOLATION §4), `Λ−m₁²=1.25m₁²>0` pointwise. So `I1∧I2 ⟺ {D_O has a simple zero z₁∈(0,z_cut) with N_O(z₁)≠0}` — Theorem A's sharp two-exponential decay, re-read as a determinant-zero statement. This is the sharpest *form* of "single argument" the corpus admits.

**2 · The exact failure — the zero sits on the edge.** `D_O`'s power series `log D_O=−Σ_k z^k/k Tr T_O^k` has radius of convergence `z₁` itself (the smallest `1/λ_n`). To prove the zero is *isolated* (separate it from `z₂=1/λ₂`) one needs `D_O` on a disc `|z−z₁|<ρ` reaching `|z|>z₁`. The gap in the `z`-plane is genuinely *large*: `z₂/z₁=λ₁/λ₂=u^{p₁−p₂}`, and the `0⁺⁺` channel has `c₁=p₁=1`, second level `p₂≥1.5` (the `1.5m₁` excitation, or `2` two-glueball), so `z₂/z₁≥u^{−1/2}≫1` and `δ:=m₂−m₁=(p₂−p₁)m₁≥½m₁`. **This is the one place a genuine positive `δ` would come from** — SUB2's `t`-uniform `O(u)` cannot resolve `λ₂` as an *eigenvalue*, but the determinant's `z`-plane separation `∼u^{−1/2}` is robust. The catch is control: on `|z|∈(z₁,z₁u^{−1/2})` the orbit series diverges; the only bound is the Hadamard/polynomial estimate `|D_O(z)|≤∏_n(1+|z|λ_n)=exp(O(u^{−1/2}))`, exponentially large against `D_lead=1−zu`'s `O(u^{−1/2})` modulus, so **no Rouché**. Analytically continuing `D_O` past `z₁` requires the self-energy and the density of states above `m₁` — which is precisely the state-counting/isolation `I2` one set out to prove. The single argument relocates the wall to one concrete target; it does not cross it.

**3 · Acid test (mandatory).** The criterion `f₁²=N_O(z₁)` is correctly sharp on all three controls. *Free massless* `tr F²`: a 2-gluon cut, `ρ∝s²`, no zero below `z_cut`, `N_O(z₁)=0` ⇒ **`f₁²=0`** (PASS). *Free massive* scalar, `O=:φ²:` (the `:`-normal-order atom): `ν` a pure cut on `[4m²,∞)`, `N_O=0` below threshold ⇒ **`f₁²=0`** (PASS — the crucial one: a *gapped* theory with no atom). *Massive free* `O=φ`: isolated zero at `e^{am}`, `N_O=Z>0` ⇒ **`f₁²>0`** (PASS). The determinant splits cut from pole on the *same* low moments; the free `:φ²:` is the overlap-zero case excluded only by `N_O(z₁)≠0`.

**4 · Five-point verdict (target = `I1∧I2` together, `0<m₁<Λ`).**
1. **Nonperturbative — PASS.** KP polymer + Rouché; no coupling expansion.
2. **Sign-controlling atom positive — HOLLOW.** `f₁²=N_O(z₁)≥0` is RP-forced and `>0` from P3's positive recoupling — but this *carries* a zero-location it cannot place; it manufacturers no separate lower bound.
3. **Non-circular — FAIL.** Rouché needs `D_O` past `z₁`, i.e. self-energy + higher-state weight = the isolation `I2` itself; circular at exactly the edge.
4. **Non-SUSY + fails free field — PASS (cleanest).** Pure YM; free `:φ²:` → `N_O=0` → `f₁²=0`.
5. **Uniform `≥λ` beyond `1/48`, to `2.5`/`a→0` — FAIL.** The zero is at the orbit-series edge; continuum/transport of `D_O` untouched.

**Exact missing estimate.** Control of `D_O` on `|z|∈(z₁,z₁u^{−1/2})` — i.e. a *uniform multiplicative bound* `|D_O/D_lead−1|=O(u)` past the first zero, equivalent to `Σ_{n≥2}λ_n=O(u²)` (all but `O(u²)` of `Tr T` is the single top eigenvalue). The trace gives only `Tr T=a₀u+O(u²)`; *how it splits among eigenvalues* is the moment-blind datum (GAP-G2-edge: `δ₀+δ₂` vs `2δ₁`), and resolving it past the edge is `I2`. **2/5. Not 5/5.**

**Sharpest line.** A pole below a cut is one holomorphic datum — its first zero is the atom's mass *and* its residue is the atom's weight, so `I1∧I2` is a single determinant statement, and it passes the acid test exactly. But the determinant's first zero is the *radius of convergence* of its own orbit expansion: the atom lives on the edge of the only series that computes it, and the far side — where isolation (`z₂/z₁∼u^{−1/2}`) would be read off — is the state-density `I2` itself. The single argument localizes the wall to one analytic continuation; it does not perform it. Clay stays 0%.
