# PROOF-LANDAU-GAUGE-IR — the Landau-gauge IR gluon mass and the Gribov horizon: no transfer to a physical gap

> **Verdict. NO rigorous statement, and NO transfer.** The Landau-gauge `D(0)>0` near β=2.5 is an empirical lattice fact — large-volume decoupling at nearby couplings, *not resolved* at the project's β=2.5, L≤8 — not a theorem; and even so it does **not** imply a gauge-invariant mass gap. The exact obstruction is twofold: (i) the gauge-fixed gluon propagator is **not reflection-positive** (OS positivity fails; `ρ_A` is sign-indefinite by Oehme–Zimmermann), and the gluon is **not in the physical Hilbert space** `H_phys = ker Q/im Q`, so a scale in `D(k)` is not a state; (ii) the Gribov mass `γ_G` is generated by the **Landau-gauge** horizon and, in that defining action, is gauge-fixing-dependent. No BRST/Kugo–Ojima or Gribov–Zwanziger theorem carries either scale into `H_phys`. `D(0)>0` is evidence for dynamical mass generation — not a theorem at β=2.5, not the physical gap.

## 1. Part (1): what is actually rigorous at β=2.5

Nothing beyond the finite lattice. Continuum Landau gauge is not globally definable: **Singer** (CMP 60, 1978) — the orbit bundle admits no continuous global section, so *every* gauge carries Gribov copies and "the Landau-gauge propagator" has no canonical continuum definition. On a finite lattice a fixed gauge-fixing algorithm defines a finite expectation, but Gribov-copy (algorithm) dependence is real. The project's β=2.5, L≤8 measurement (`ATOM-gluon-propagator`) finds `D` finite and positive at every resolved k but **does not resolve the k→0 transverse mode**; the massive fit gives `m²<0` and the Gribov–Stingl form is volume-degenerate. The numerical `D(0)>0` rests on large lattices: **Cucchieri–Mendes** (PRL 100, 2008), SU(2) to 128⁴ at β=2.2, with rigorous finite-volume **bounds** `D(0) ≳ 2.5 GeV⁻²`; **Bogolubsky et al.** (PLB 676, 2009), SU(3) to 96⁴. Lattice facts — not continuum theorems.

## 2. Part (2): the IR mass does not transfer — positivity fails

`D(k)` is gauge-dependent, not observable. **Reflection positivity** fails: the transverse-gluon Schwinger function is not positive-definite (lattice and FRG reconstructions: **Cyrol–Pawlowski–Rothkopf–Wink**, SciPost 5, 065 (2018)), and the **Oehme–Zimmermann** superconvergence `∫ρ_A = 0` forces a sign-indefinite `ρ_A` — a "mass pole" in such a function is not a physical mass. The physical space is the BRST cohomology (**Kugo–Ojima**, PTPS 66, 1979); the gluon is absent from it (no colored asymptotic states). A BRST transfer needs the scale in a BRST-closed, positive-norm composite; none is known. Kugo–Ojima `u(0)=−1` is a **confinement** criterion (ghost enhancement = scaling), not a gap derivation — and the **decoupling** solution carrying the IR mass violates it (`u(0)≈−0.7…−0.8`; Fischer, arXiv:0812.2745), with BRST argued soft-broken: the mass-bearing solution is where the transfer tool is weakest.

## 3. Part (3): the Gribov horizon is a gauge-fixing scale, not a gap

**Gribov** (NPB 139, 1978) and **Zwanziger** (NPB 323, 1989; 364, 1991) restrict the measure to the Gribov region `Ω` (`∂·A=0`, FP operator ≥0); the horizon condition then generates `γ_G`. But the original **GZ** propagator `D = k²/(k⁴+γ_G⁴)` gives **`D(0)=0`** (scaling) — contradicted by the lattice decoupling; only the **refined** action (**RGZ**, Dudal et al., PRD 78, 065025 (2008); letter PRD 77, 071501) with a dimension-2 condensate recovers `D(0)>0`, at the price of further gauge-dependent condensates. `γ_G` is defined relative to the Landau horizon; in the original action it is gauge-fixing-dependent, and the gauge-invariant `A^h` reformulation (Capri et al.) only *proposes* a gauge-invariant gap equation — not a theorem, and still a sign-indefinite `ρ_A`. Zwanziger's horizon↔area-law arguments are bounds under the horizon assumption, not a gauge-invariant gap. So `γ_G` is not a gauge-invariant mass gap, and the GZ/RGZ dichotomy shows the framework itself disagrees with the lattice on the IR branch.

**Sharpest line.** The Landau-gauge gluon mass and the Gribov mass `γ_G` are both artifacts of a gauge slice that is neither globally definable (Singer) nor reflection-positive (Oehme–Zimmermann, negative `ρ_A`); the only rigorous route to `H_phys` is BRST cohomology, and the gluon is not in it. Hence no gauge-invariant mass gap follows — `D(0)>0` is real evidence for dynamical mass generation, but it is neither a theorem at β=2.5 nor the physical gap.
