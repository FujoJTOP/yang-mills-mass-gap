### 1.1 Battle & Federbush (1982) — the mass-zero phase-cell cluster expansion
*G. A. Battle III & P. Federbush, "A phase cell cluster expansion for Euclidean field theories," **Ann. Phys. 142(1), 95–139 (1982)**, DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(82)90230-5.* `[V-doc: Crossref vol/pages; INSPIRE abstract verbatim]`

Verbatim key sentences of the abstract (INSPIRE `[V-doc]`):
- **Mass-zero expansion:** "We adapt the cluster expansion first used to treat infrared problems for lattice models (**a mass zero cluster expansion**) to the usual field theory situation."
- **Phase-cell mode decomposition:** "The field is expanded in terms of **special block spin functions** and the cluster expansion given in terms of the expansion coefficients (**phase cell variables**); the cluster expansion expresses correlation functions in terms of contributions from **finite coupled subsets** of these variables."
- **Which `d`:** "Most of the present work is carried through in **d space time dimensions** (for [the d=2 scalar setting; OCR "φ 2 4"] the details of the cluster expansion are pursued and **convergence is proven**). Thus most of the results … will apply to a treatment of [φ³₄]." (The registry/prompt summarise this as: **convergence proven for the d=2,3 φ⁴ settings.**)
- **Vacuum-stability substitute:** "Of particular interest in this paper is a **substitute for the stability of the vacuum bound** appropriate to this cluster expansion (**for d = 2 and d = 3**), and a **new method for performing estimates with tree graphs**."
- **d=4 status:** "The phase cell cluster expansions have the renormalization group incorporated intimately into their structure. **We hope they will be useful ultimately in treating four dimensional field theories.**"

**Extraction:**
(a) **Mode decomposition** = expansion in **spectral block-spin functions**; nodes = **phase-cell variables** (expansion coefficients on a scale hierarchy `ℓ_j`); correlation functions = sums over **finite coupled subsets** (forests) of these variables.
(b) **Mass-zero vacuum-stability substitute** exists **only for `d = 2, 3`** `[V-doc]`. The "mass-zero" property means **no mass is inserted** into the propagator (the expansion sees the IR without a mass organising scale) — the double-edged feature (§3.4).
(c) **Tree-graph method** is the device that resums the coordinate-space volume factor; the RG is "built in."
(d) **Left open at `d = 4`:** everything beyond "hope … ultimately." No d=4 YM convergence theorem in 1982.

## §2 The four acid tests

All computed in `research/unconditional/w7/transport_phase_cell.py` (`py -3.12`, one job, log `transport_phase_cell.log`). `[V-here]`

### Test 1 — free massless field: lowest phase-cell mode must keep `λ_min ~ L⁻²`; no artificial mass
**PASS** `[V-here]`. Periodic `-Δ` on `[0,L]` (L=1): `λ_min(nonzero) → 4π² = (2π/L)²` as `N↑` (ratio 0.9872, 0.9968, 0.9992, 0.9998, 0.99995 for `N=16…256`). Block-spin **Schur complement** (integrate out the fine modes): the **coarse constant-mode mass² = 0 to machine precision**; the lowest nonzero coarse mode is `1.99×(2π/L)²` — a wavefunction renormalisation, still `O(L⁻²)`, and emphatically **not** `(π/h_coarse)² ≈ 10106`. A **naive block-Dirichlet** Laplacian would give `~ (π/ℓ)² → ∞`, an artificial mass; the phase-cell spectral construction avoids it. The mass-zero property is genuine and is exactly why the UV works.

### Test 2 — pure-gauge background decouples exactly
**PASS (exact)** `[V-here]`. On a `4⁴` SU(2) lattice, `U(x,μ)=g(x)g(x+μ)⁻¹`: all **1536** plaquettes equal the identity to `1.6×10⁻¹⁵` ⇒ `F=0` and the field-strength coupling of **any** mode vanishes. A single **defect link** makes `F` nonzero on exactly `6/1536` plaquettes — **localised at the defect**. This reproduces, numerically, the `[V-doc]` identity of §1.4: a chunk is precisely such a localised deviation; a pure-gauge chunk is invisible to every gauge-invariant mode coupling. This is the route's **real structural asset**.

### Test 3 — single SU(2) chunk: can the group integral be done, and does `(ℓ/L)²` control it *without* `Hess S ⪰ 0`?
**The decisive sub-test (3a) fails; the integral (3b) is not reducible.** `[V-here]`

**3a — linear vs quadratic coupling.** This is the crux and it is clean:
- The **linear** (tadpole) coupling of a small mode to a **smooth** background (varying on scale `L_bg`) is a **scale-separation** overlap. For a unit-amplitude localised mode it decays as a **positive power of `ℓ/L_bg`** (measured log-log slopes **2.87** unit-amplitude, **2.37** unit-L²; the asymptotic exponents for this zero-mean, zero-dipole mode are 3 and 2.5 — the exponent depends on normalisation and multipole content, which is why I do **not** claim the paper's exact `(ℓ/L)²`). **Robust point:** in **every** normalisation the overlap is **`O(1)` as `ℓ → L_bg = L`**. The suppression is a **scale-separation** effect and it **vanishes exactly at the crossover**.
- The **quadratic** (mass-generating) coupling is `2gB·J₃` in the chunk background. For a constant magnetic background its spin-1 eigenvalues are **`{−2B, 0, +2B}`** (computed): the transverse sector has a **negative eigenvalue `−2B`** — the **Savvidy unstable mode** (`PROOF-SAVVIDY-EXACT-SIGN`). **This operator is scale-free: it carries NO `(ℓ/L)` factor and acts at every mode scale.** Hence `(ℓ/L)²` **cannot** control the mass-generating coupling, and **cannot** substitute for `Hess S ⪰ 0` — it is precisely the *failure* of that assumption.

**3b — the chunk integral.** For the exact **finite** group integral I used a `1×n` SU(2) plaquette strip with the shared link `= V(θ)` as the mode and all other links integrated (Metropolis MC, `[V-here]`). Findings: the integral is **well defined**; the induced link curvature `S_eff″` is **`O(1)` at `β=2.5`** (e.g. `+0.52±0.10`, `+0.58±0.10` for `n=2,4`) — **no small parameter**; and for `n=3` the inferred `S_eff″` **changes sign** between `θ=0.2` and `θ=0.4` (`−0.27±0.10` vs `+0.33±0.05`) — the chunk-induced effective action is **not convex**, so it furnishes **no convex/sign-controlled lower bound**. This is a **finite-volume link curvature**, not the `0⁺⁺` residue.

**What blocks the real chunk integral (the honest answer to the task):** a genuine Federbush chunk is (i) a **finite but high-dimensional** set of group elements realising a **degree-1 map `S³→SU(2)`** with a **point gauge singularity** and an **induced Jacobian**; (ii) the integral is a **matrix integral** over that unit, **not Gaussian** (large field), **not reducible to a single-plaquette/Bessel form** (the fixed product is absorbable by Haar invariance only when it multiplies *one* group — with `π₃` structure it does not); (iii) its Gaussian (steepest-descent) approximation **is** the Savvidy Hessian, which is **indefinite**; (iv) beyond Gaussian, the exact object is a **positive measure**, and a positive measure yields **upper** bounds on curvature/variance, **not** the **lower** bound H1/H\* needs (the direction-of-inequality wall, `BRIEF §3`). **So the `(ℓ/L)²` suppression controls only the tadpole; the mass is set by the quadratic coupling, which is unsuppressed and indefinite. Test 3 fails to replace `Hess ⪰ 0`.**

### Test 4 — multi-scale uniformity as `a→0`, chunks + high representations: is the forest activity summable?
**Controlled in the UV, uncontrolled at the crossover — fails exactly where the mass is generated.** `[V-here]`
- **High-representation channel.** SU(2) character coefficients `u_j(β) = I_{2j+1}(β)/I_1(β)` (calibrated: `Z_p = 2I_1/β` matches `∫(2/π)sin²t e^{βcos t}` to 6 digits):
  - `β = 1/48 ≈ 0.0208`: `u₁ = 1.8×10⁻⁵` — a **genuine small parameter** (this is the P3 strong-coupling point).
  - `β = 2.5` (crossover): `u₁ = 0.188`, typical representation `j* = 1` — **no small parameter**; first-order truncation error `O(0.2)`.
  Thus the representation sum **converges** (Bessel decay) at **all** `β`, but its **effective expansion parameter is `O(1)` at the crossover**.
- **Running-coupling activity.** One-loop SU(2), `dg/dlnμ = −(11N/3)g³/(16π²)`: `g²(2Λ) = 31`, `g²(16Λ) = 7.8`, `g²(10⁶Λ) = 1.55`; **`g² = 0.5` requires `μ/Λ ~ 5×10¹⁸`**. At the mass-generating shell `μ ~ Λ`, `g² = O(10)`. The naive per-shell forest activity `Σ (L/ℓ)⁴·g²(ℓ)` **diverges in the UV**; the **tree-graph/RG cancellation** (Battle–Federbush's "new method for tree graph estimates", Federbush III's stability) is what resums the `(L/ℓ)⁴`, leaving the **marginal `g²`**. That marginal activity is `O(1)` at the crossover.
- **Why φ³₄ works and YM₄ does not `[V-doc + J]`:** φ³₄ is **superrenormalisable** — the coupling carries a **power** of `ℓ`, so the resummed activity is a **power-law small parameter** that beats the mode count. YM₄ is **marginal** — the coupling decays only **logarithmically**, so the resummation is marginal and there is **no power small parameter**; at the crossover it is `O(1)`. This is exactly the boundary on which the phase-cell programme stopped, and it is what Williamson (φ³₄) vs the absent YM₄ theorem records.

**Test 4 verdict:** the forest/phase-cell activity is **summable on the safe side of the marginal line** (UV, AF) and **not summable at the crossover** (`ℓ~L`, `g=O(1)`). The multi-scale uniformity as `a→0` **holds in the UV and fails in the IR**, which is the wall.

## §3 The crossover analysis: `ℓ/L → 1`, and (II) vs H1

### 3.1 Does the `(ℓ/L)²` suppression survive at `ℓ/L→1`? — **No; it vanishes exactly where the mass is generated.** `[V-here + J]`
The suppression is a **scale-separation** factor: for `ℓ ≪ L` it is a positive power of `ℓ/L` (`[V-here]` slopes 2.4–2.9 for the smooth-background overlap; exact exponent normalization-dependent `[R]`); at `ℓ ~ L` it is **`O(1)`** in every normalisation. The mass is generated at the **hot shell** `k ~ 2π/L`, `ℓ ~ L` (`TRANSPORT-MACHINE §4`; `E2-FLIP-TRANSPORT §4`: peak at `ρ*=O(1)/Λ`). ### 3.2 Is (II) a new dynamical input, or = H1 / I2? — **Argue both sides, then rule.** `[J]`
