# PROOF-TRANSPORT-MONOTONE — is any β-monotone / protected quantity positive at strong coupling and ⇒ f₁²(2.5)>0?

> 2026-11-23 · Direct hit on the transport wall. **Verdict: no such quantity exists.** The right *object* is the isolation margin `Δ(β)=Λ−m₁²`, and `Δ>0` does give `f₁²>0` (elementary, below) — but `Δ(β)` is **neither β-monotone nor protected**. Every exactly-β-monotone object (free energy / action density; the `a`-theorem charge) is a thermodynamic **total** that the moment lemma turns into an *upper* bound on the edge — the free field (`g=0`) realises the monotonicity with `f₁²=0`. The one protected quantity (`F_T`, trace-anomaly decay constant) is **per-state**, not the edge. Every expectation's β-derivative is a sign-indefinite covariance, and SU(N) has no FKG to fix its sign. Local-in-β transport is a theorem *given uniform isolation*; uniform isolation on `[1/48,2.5]` is `H*(r₀)`, one rung above the gap. No fabrication.

## The correct object is Δ — and Δ>0 already gives f₁²>0.
With GAP-G2-edge's `Λ:=inf supp(ν−f₁²δ_{m₁²})`: if `Δ=Λ−m₁²>0` then `f₁²=0` would force `Λ=m₁²`, so `f₁²>0`. This is the qualitative half of the residue–level-gap lemma (the quantitative bound additionally needs `Λ>M₁/M₀`, the `I2` isolation of PROOF-EDGE-E2 / Theorem A). The gapped free scalar `O=:φ²:` does **not** refute this: it has a mass gap but the `0⁺⁺` is a *cut* at `4m²`, so `Δ=0`, `f₁²=0` — mass gap ⊅ isolation, and it is isolation, not the gap, that forces the atom (PROOF-F1SQ). Hence the transport target is `Δ(β)`.

## Δ(β) is not β-monotone.
`m₁,Λ` are transfer-matrix eigenvalues; their *difference* carries no min–max monotonicity. Rigorously `Z=∫e^{−βA}dU` (`A=Σ_p(1−Re tr U_p/N)≥0`) gives `∂_β⟨O⟩=−Cov_β(O,∂_βA)`, sign-indefinite absent FKG — and SU(N) has no FKG (torsion ⟹ no group-compatible lattice order; PROOF-COMPARISON-FKG). No FKG ⟹ no expectation, hence no eigenvalue difference, is coupling-monotone (Perron positivity attaches only to the *ground* object, INEQ-R9). The dynamical failure is concrete: at the crossover the lightest `0⁺⁺` approaches the two-glueball threshold `2m_g` and `Δ↓0` — exactly the dissolution that makes `f₁²` upper-semicontinuous (it jumps *down*, never up).

## No protected proxy — the protected objects are the wrong type.
- **β-monotone ⇒ total ⇒ upper.** `F(β)=−(1/V)logZ` is concave (`F''=−Var(A)/V≤0`), so `⟨S_W⟩/V=F'` is monotone nonincreasing; the `a`-theorem makes `a` a protected monotone flow charge (`a_UV≥a_IR≥0`). Both are **totals**; the moment lemma converts totals to *upper* bounds on the edge, and the free field satisfies both with `f₁²=0`. Free-field-blind.
- **Protected ⇒ per-state.** `F_T=⟨0|T^μ_μ|G⟩` is exactly RG-invariant, `f₁=F_T/(β/2g)` (INEQ-R7); but `F_T` tracks a *state*, `f₁²` is the *edge*. The `0⁺⁺` spectrum is unprotected and may reorder, so nonzero `F_T` does not imply nonzero edge residue — it collapses to a no-level-crossing theorem `=H*(r₀)` (INEQ-T4).
- **Variational overlap ⇒ upper.** Rayleigh–Ritz and Cauchy–Schwarz bound energies/overlaps from **above**; a from-below overlap needs a certified `|G⟩` (Kato–Temple), i.e. the isolation it was meant to supply (PROOF-BOGOLIUBOV, LEVER).

## W7 cross-check.
W7-RERUN resolves the measured `G″(0)>0` as `G″=W+(E[H]−Var)`: the positive part is the **Haar–Jacobian** `W` (present at `β=0`: pure-Jacobian `V_eff″≈0.248`), β-independent *geometry*; the action bracket `E[H]−Var≈0` is the thin dynamical margin. So the curvature carries no β-monotone *dynamical* signal. Consistent with, not a route around, the no-monotone verdict.

## What survives.
**Local transport (theorem).** If isolation holds uniformly on a β-interval `I` (`Λ(β)−m₁²(β)≥δ>0`) and the finite-volume transfer matrix is analytic in β, `f₁²` is continuous on `I` and `f₁²≥r₀` extends across `I`. A global `f₁²(2.5)≥c·f₁²(1/48)` with β-independent `c>0` is **false** in general: a usc atom dissolving at an interior threshold drops `f₁²` to `0`; only `c=1` transport over a uniform-isolation interval is available.

**Exact missing monotonicity.** A functional `Ψ(β)` that is simultaneously (a) atom-coercive, `Ψ(2.5)≤C·f₁²(2.5)` (Schur-**convex**, INEQ-T3), (b) `≥r₀` at `β₀<1/48`, and (c) β-monotone or RG-protected. None exists: (a) puts `Ψ` on the Schur-convex side; the only β-monotone/protected objects (free energy, `a`-charge, `F_T`) are totals or per-state — Schur-concave side or off-edge. Equivalently, the missing input is a **β-monotone, atom-coercive functional = uniform isolation margin `Δ(β)>0` on `[1/48,2.5]`, which is `H*(r₀)` one rung above the gap.**

**Sharpest line.** Transport needs a quantity *both* atom-coercive and β-monotone; but atom-coercive ⇒ Schur-convex (a total bounded by the atom, which the free field flips to `f₁²=0`), while the monotone objects are thermodynamic totals — the two live on **opposite sides of the Schur order**. `Δ` is atom-coercive but not monotone/protected; `F_T` is protected but not atom-coercive; free energy is monotone but not atom-coercive. No object is all three; positivity does not propagate.
