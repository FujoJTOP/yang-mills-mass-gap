# PROOF-NO-DISSOLUTION-KINEMATIC — the lightest `0⁺⁺` is pinned below every continuum; dissolution needs a from-below level crossing (or a factor-2 hierarchy), not a smooth crossover

> 2026-11-23 · Kinematic angle on the dissolution half of K1 (PROOF-TRANSPORT-ANALYTIC). **Verdict: while the `0⁺⁺` is the overall lightest and the gap is open, it cannot dissolve — it is the bottom atom of its channel, sitting strictly below every continuum by a margin equal to its own mass.** Pure YM has no asymptotic gluon, so there is no `(gg)` cut; the only continuum is multi-glueball at `θ ≥ 2m_g`, and `θ ≥ 2m_{0⁺⁺} > m_{0⁺⁺}`. Dissolution needs a continuum at/below `m_{0⁺⁺}`, i.e. a state lighter than `½ m_{0⁺⁺}` — a level crossing **from below** — or gap closure. A mere `{0⁺⁺,2⁺⁺}` ordering flip (IN-11) is **relabeling, not dissolution**: the atoms and their residues are unchanged, and the `0⁺⁺` stays isolated below `2m_{2⁺⁺}` unless it is `≥2×` heavier than the 2⁺⁺. The argument is a genuine kinematic theorem but *conditional*: it converts "is the `0⁺⁺` an atom?" into "is it below the 2-body threshold?", and the antecedent (uniform gap + `0⁺⁺` lightest through `β_c≈2.47`) is exactly the dynamics still open.

**Setup.** Transfer-matrix Hamiltonian `H_β ≥ 0` (fixed `T⁴_L`, or OS continuum), vacuum `0`, gap `m_g(β)=inf σ(H_β|_{Ω^⊥})>0`. Reflection positivity gives, per channel `c`, `ν_c ≥ 0` with `C_c(t)=∫e^{−√s t}dν_c`, `supp ν_c ⊂ [m_g²,∞)`; lightest atom `m_c=inf supp ν_c`, `f_c²=ν_c({m_c²})`. The multi-glueball continuum in `c` opens at `θ_c=inf{m_a+m_b: a⊗b ∋ c}`; since every single-particle mass `≥ m_g`, `θ_c ≥ 2m_g`. No gluon is asymptotic in the confined phase, so no `(gg)` cut: the lowest continuum is two glueballs.

**1. Pinning lemma.** If `m_{0⁺⁺}(β) < θ_{0⁺⁺}(β)`, the lightest `0⁺⁺` atom is an isolated point of `supp ν_{0⁺⁺}`, separated from `[θ_{0⁺⁺},∞)` by `θ_{0⁺⁺}−m_{0⁺⁺}>0`; it cannot merge into a continuum at that `β`. *Corollary (lightest).* If the `0⁺⁺` is overall lightest, `m_{0⁺⁺}=m_g`, then all pairs `≥ 2m_{0⁺⁺}`, so `m_{0⁺⁺}<2m_{0⁺⁺} ≤ θ_{0⁺⁺}`: the margin is exactly `m_g=m_{0⁺⁺}`, uniform iff the gap is. Dissolution is then impossible for **every** `β` at which the `0⁺⁺` is lightest and `m_g>0`.

**2. Why continuity cannot substitute for the margin.** `f₁²=ν({m₁²})` is only **upper**-semicontinuous (PROOF-F1SQ): at a dissolution `m₁→θ` the eigenvalue leaves the discrete spectrum, Kato's isolation hypothesis fails *exactly there*, and the atom weight may drop to `0` (usc permits the drop). So analyticity does **not** forbid dissolution — it holds only on the pinned stretch and stops at `β*`. The no-dissolution conclusion rests on the kinematic **margin** (`m_{0⁺⁺}<θ`, uniform), not on transport.

**3. The loophole, sharpened.** Dissolution needs `θ_{0⁺⁺} ≤ m_{0⁺⁺}`; since `θ ≥ 2m_lightest`, this forces **`m_{0⁺⁺} ≥ 2m_lightest`**. Hence:
- *Same-channel* (`0⁺⁺` vs excited `0⁺⁺*`, both A1g): von Neumann–Wigner **avoided crossing** — their order cannot flip; no same-channel relabeling risk.
- *Cross-channel* (`0⁺⁺` A1g vs `2⁺⁺` E/T2g, distinct irreps): crossing unprotected — IN-11's open ordering. If the 2⁺⁺ descends below the `0⁺⁺`, the `0⁺⁺` becomes the first *excited* glueball. That is **relabeling**: residues unchanged, and `m_{0⁺⁺}<2m_{2⁺⁺}` still holds unless `m_{0⁺⁺}≥2m_{2⁺⁺}`. True dissolution (into a pair cut) needs the factor-2 hierarchy `m_{0⁺⁺}≥2m_lightest` — strictly stronger than any ordering flip, and contradicted by the measured `0⁺⁺` 1710 < `2⁺⁺` 2390 MeV.

**Exact condition.** The kinematic argument **succeeds** — the `0⁺⁺` persists as an atom — under (K-A) a uniform gap `m_g≥μ>0` and (K-B) `m_{0⁺⁺}<2m_lightest` (implied by `0⁺⁺` lightest). It **fails** only on (¬K-A) gap closure — deconfinement, *not* the zero-`T` smooth crossover — or (¬K-B) `m_{0⁺⁺}≥2m_lightest`, a factor-2 hierarchy (the lightest misidentified as a `≥2×`-lighter species). Both failures are dynamics (the gap and IN-11's ordering), neither a smooth-crossover effect. Below threshold, the atom is pinned; the only door out is from below.

**Sharpest line.** The lightest `0⁺⁺` is the *bottom* of its channel: with no lighter state and no asymptotic gluon there is nothing at or below its mass to dissolve into, and the multi-glueball cut opens at `2m_g>m_g` — the atom sits strictly below every continuum by a margin equal to its own mass. A plain `{0⁺⁺,2⁺⁺}` ordering flip merely relabels which glueball is ground state; the `0⁺⁺` stays a stable, isolated atom unless it is twice as heavy as the true lightest. The kinematic angle is airtight; what it *inherits rather than proves* is the regime where the `0⁺⁺` is lightest through `β_c` — IN-11's ordering and the continuum gap itself.
