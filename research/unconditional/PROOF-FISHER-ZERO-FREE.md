# PROOF-FISHER-ZERO-FREE — the real β-axis is automatically zero-free (`Z_Λ>0`); the whole question is complex zeros pinching it, and the rigorous zero-free region reaches only `β≲1/48`, never 2.5

> 2026-11-23 · Fisher/Lee–Yang zero test of a zero-free tube on `[1/48,2.5]`. **Verdict: the criterion is clean; the target is unreached from either side. On finite `Λ`, `Z_Λ(β)` is entire in β and strictly positive for every real β (pointwise-positive integrand) — no Fisher zeros on the real axis, any coupling; the analyticity question is purely complex zeros accumulating on it. The only rigorous zero-free region for continuous SU(N)₄ is the strong-coupling cluster/character-expansion neighbourhood, real-axis reach `β<1/48` (`β_W≲0.19`, SU(3); refined `195/7168≈0.027`, ~1.3×). By Pringsheim the positive-activity disk is maximal, ending on the positive real activity axis and un-crossable by resummation; the only route past it — RG blocking — re-imports the Savvidy sign. Nothing reaches `β=2.5`; the missing bound is complex-β zero-freeness at activity `O(0.1–0.4)`, ~10²× the proven radius. If granted, `f` — and, with Kato simplicity, `m₁` — is real-analytic on `[1/48,2.5]`, forbidding a discontinuous melt; but analyticity transports the *value*, not the residue *sign*, so glueball persistence as an atom still needs (K2).**

**1. The Fisher-zero criterion.** `Z_Λ=∫∏dU e^{−S_W}` is entire in β (each plaquette factor `e^{(β/N)Re tr U_p}` is entire), `f_Λ=−(1/V)log Z_Λ` meromorphic. For real β the integrand and `dU` are `>0`, so `Z_Λ(β)>0` on `ℝ` — **no real-axis zeros, any β**; *Fisher zeros* are complex-β zeros. By Yang–Lee/Fisher/Ruelle, `f=lim_Λ f_Λ` is holomorphic on `Ω∋β₀` iff the `Z_Λ` have no zeros accumulating in `Ω` and `f_Λ→f` uniformly on compacts (Vitali); zeros accumulating on the real axis *are* the transition points. Separately (Kato), `T_β` entire ⇒ `m₁=−(1/a)log λ₁` is real-analytic where `λ₁` is simple and isolated. Zero-free `Z` gives analytic `f`; analytic `m₁` also needs (K1).

**2. Any rigorous zero-free region reaching 2.5? No — from either end.** The only one for continuous SU(N), `d=4`, is the strong-coupling cluster/character-expansion neighbourhood (Osterwalder–Seiler 1978; Shen–Zhu–Zhu 2023, `β<1/48`), real reach `1/48` (~1.3× refinement). Asano/Lee–Yang circle theorems need a positive (ferromagnetic) representation — available for `Z_N`/`U(1)` by duality and in the complex *fugacity/field* plane, but the SU(N) character/Weingarten surface expansion is **signed** (PROOF-RANDOM-CLUSTER), so no contraction confines zeros off the β-axis. Large β is likewise open for continuous SU(N) (weak-coupling results are finite-group; Balaban RG incomplete). `β≈2.5` is uncovered from both sides.

**3. Pringsheim — is the true region larger, and provable?** `1/48` is the strong-coupling convergence radius. At β>0 the Wilson character coefficients are nonnegative (no sign cancellation), so Pringsheim puts a singularity on the positive real activity axis at that radius: the disk is *maximal*, and resummation meets a positive-axis singularity (the renormalon wall, W2). The true `f` is expected analytic on all of `[1/48,2.5]` (no bulk transition), so the true region is larger — but Pringsheim forbids reaching it from the positive series, and the one route past (position-dependent RG blocking) reintroduces the Savvidy negative modes = the sign problem. **Not proven larger; no known method.** (The bracket mixes SZZ β with Wilson `β_W`; honest distance ≈13× in `λ`, ≈10²× in activity.)

**4. If a zero-free tube did reach 2.5 — theorem and yield.** Suppose `Ω⊃[1/48,2.5]` has no Fisher-zero accumulation, `inf|Z_Λ|>0`, `f_Λ→f` on compacts. Then `f` is real-analytic on `[1/48,2.5]`; with (K1) Kato continues `m₁` real-analytic, with (K2) so is `f₁²=a²` — **no discontinuous melt**, one analytic mass branch through 2.47, extending PROOF-NO-MELT-PHASE past its single-phase assumption. But analyticity continues the *value*, not the *sign* (free scalar: simple isolated `0⁺⁺`, `f₁²≡0`): zero-free `Z` gives analytic `f` and (with K1) `m₁>0` except isolated touch points, **not** `f₁²>0` (that is (K2)). The glueball persists as an analytic mass branch; the atom residue is not transported.

**Sharpest line.** The real axis is trivially zero-free (`Z_Λ>0`); the whole problem is complex zeros pinching it. The rigorous region is the strong-coupling disk of real reach `~1/48`, maximal by Pringsheim and un-crossable without the Savvidy sign — it does not reach 2.5. The missing bound is complex-β zero-freeness at activity `O(0.1–0.4)`, ~10²× the proven radius; were it granted, `f` and (with Kato) `m₁` would be real-analytic on `[1/48,2.5]`, excluding a discontinuous melt — yet analyticity transports the value, never the residue sign.
