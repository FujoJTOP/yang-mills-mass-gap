# E1-FRG — the exact RG flow of the constrained effective potential: convexity is a theorem, the strict floor at the Savvidy crossover is the gap

> **判词 (verdict).** The Wetterich/Polchinski flow of the coarse marginal `p_k(a)` gives an **exact, nonperturbative, one-loop-per-scale** Fokker–Planck flow on `V_eff,k(a)=−log p_k(a)`. Two outputs are rigorous, without truncation. **(A)** The regulator-modified effective action is convex, `Γ_k^{(2)}+R_k⪰0`, so the flow is globally well-defined and `V_eff,0` is convex: `V_eff,0″≥0` on every mode including `k→0` — a **universal ≥0 floor**, hence `=0` for the free massless field (acid test *passes*: the tool does not manufacture a gap). **(B)** At β=0 the Haar/Jacobian `Φ` is convex, Prékopa makes the pushed marginal log-concave, and the flow **preserves** it: `V_eff,k″≥N/12` uniformly in `k,L` — the β=0 gap becomes a flow **invariant**, stronger than the fixed-β value of `k0-CONSTRAINT-EFFPOT`. **Neither gives `≥λ>0` at β=2.5.** The convexity the flow proves is `≥0`, the free-field value; the strict floor is the `p→0` behaviour of `Γ_0^{(2)}`, i.e. the death of the Savvidy negative eigenmode under the flow — which *is* the gap. Genuine partial closure + exact failure at the crossover: a RENAME of H1 at 2.5.

## §1 · The exact flow (Fokker–Planck / viscous HJ)
Let `μ_k` be the scale-`k` exact-RG measure (`μ_0` the sharp one) and `a(A)=⟨v,log U⟩`. The marginal `p_k(a)=E_{μ_k}[δ(a(A)−a)]` obeys the **exact** drift–diffusion l
`∂_k p_k = −∂_a(β_k p_k) + ∂_a²(σ_k p_k)`, with drift `β_k(a)=E_k[−ȧ|a]` and diffusion `σ_k(a)=½E_k[⟨ȧ,ȧ⟩_c|a]`, both conditional supertraces over the `a`-fiber. Hence `V_eff,k=−log p_k` solves the viscous Hamilton–Jacobi
`∂_k V_eff = −σ_k V_eff″ + σ_k(V_eff′)² + (2σ_k′−β_k)V_eff′ + (β_k′−σ_k″)`.
The "one-loop-exact-per-scale" content sits in `σ_k`: it is a single conditional supertrace built from `(Γ_k^{(2)}+R_k)^{-1}(k∂_kR_k)(Γ_k^{(2)}+R_k)^{-1}`, with the stripped shell Hessian `H_ff=−D²+2adF_B` as the leading part of `Γ_k^{(2)}`. Its negative eigenvalue `−2g|F_B|` on the cone `|k|<√(2g|F_B|)` is exactly where the integrand is largest — the Savvidy concavity enters as a denominator, not a value.

## §2 · Convexity theorem ⇒ universal `≥0` floor, and the acid test
The modified Legendre transform makes `Γ_k+½∫φR_kφ` convex, hence `Γ_k^{(2)}+R_k⪰0` **for all `k`** (Dupuis et al. 2021 §2.2). The flow is therefore well-defined through any fiber, and `V_eff,0` is convex: `V_eff,0″≥0`. For the free massless field `Γ_k^{(2)}=k²`, `σ_k` is the free variance, `V_eff,0″=0` — **the tool returns 0** (no false gap). But `≥0` is a floor the free field also attains: structural convexity **cannot** separate free from YM. The separating half `≥λ` needs the floor to be *strict at the symmetric point*, `V_eff,0″(0)>0`, which convexity does not force.

## §3 · β=0: the flow preserves Prékopa
`Φ=−log J` convex on the compact group ⇒ `e^{−Φ}dX` log-concave ⇒ every linear marginal log-concave ⇒ `V_eff,k` convex with `W_geo=E[Φ″]−Var(Φ′)≥N/12`. Convexity is preserved along the (reversible, gradient) flow, so `V_eff,k″≥N/12` **uniformly in `k,L`** — a flow-invariant strong-coupling gap. This is the honest banked theorem: the β=0 gap is a *fixed-point property of the RG*, not merely a point value.

## §4 · The 2.5 wall: unclosed curvature, circular extraction
Differentiating `V_eff,k″(0)` under `k` couples to `V_eff,k⁗(0)`, `∂²β_k`, `∂²σ_k` — **not closed**. A strict floor would need a maximum principle or Bakry–Émery `Γ₂≥λ` on this nonlinear diffusion; `Γ₂` is the sign of the regularized propagator on the fiber, which on the Savvidy shells is indefinite (`D>0` at 2.5, `PᵀHP|ran P≈−0.3`). So the flow reproduces `G″=Δ_dyn+W_geo+X` term-by-term but gives no `Γ₂` floor at 2.5. Precisely: the negative mode is UV boundary data `Γ_Λ^{(2)}=S_W^{(2)}`; taming it is `R_k`'s job, and `R_k→0` as `k→0`. What survives is `Γ_0^{(2)}⪰0` (convex, **not** strictly positive). The strict floor is `V_eff,0″(0)≥λ ⟺ 1/Var(a₀)≤1/λ ⟺ m_eff²(k→0)≥λ` — **H1**, not supplied by the flow.

## §5 · Five-point scorecard (honest)
1. **Nonperturbative — PASS.** Exact FP/viscous HJ; `Γ_k^{(2)}+R_k⪰0` makes it well-defined; one loop per scale, no expansion.
2. **Sign control `m_eff²(k→0)≥λ` — PARTIAL.** Universal `≥0` (convexity), `≥N/12` at β=0 (Prékopa, flow-invariant). FAILS strict `≥λ` at 2.5 (`Γ₂` indefinite).
3. **Non-circular — PASS structurally, FAIL at closing.** The flow is non-circularly well-defined (convexity theorem), but extracting the *strict* floor at 2.5 = closing the curvature equation = H1.
4. **Non-SUSY + fails free field — PASS (cleanest).** Free field `Γ_k^{(2)}=k² ⇒ V_eff,0″=0`; the discriminator is `Φ≥N/12`, absent for Lebesgue — a genuine acid-test separation on the value.
5. **Uniform `≥λ` — FAIL.** Only `≥0` universal and `N/12` at β=0/weak UV; no uniform λ at 2.5.

**Sharpest line.** The exact flow **passes the free-field half** (global 0, non-vacuous) and **passes at β=0** (`N/12`, flow-preserved), but **fails the discriminating half** — strict `≥λ` at the Savvidy crossover. The Wetterich flow is exact and its convexity is a theorem, but that convexity is a `≥0` statement the free field also satisfies; the flow "controls" the Savvidy mode only to `R_k`-dominated non-negativity, and the vanishing regulator hands back exactly the residual curvature we set out to bound. It does not control the negative mode — it *presupposes* its absorption and calls the leftover the gap (H1 in flow language).
