# PROOF-CONCENTRATION — does measure concentration on SU(N)^Λ give the gap, or only assume it?

> 2026-11-23 · Ledoux / martingale / Talagrand route, tested on the Wilson measure at β≈2.5. **Verdict: concentration is downstream of the gap, not upstream — it cannot deliver a global one, and the brief's chain "concentration ⇒ LSI ⇒ Poincaré" is broken at its first arrow.** Three exact facts. **(i)** Product Haar on SU(N)^E tensorizes to a volume-uniform LSI constant `C₀=O(1)`, but the physical measure `μ_Λ=e^{−βH}dHaar` is *not* a product: every bounded-difference / Holley–Stroock bound carries the extensive constant `e^{2βN_□}=e^{cL⁴}`, and the Doob–martingale version needs the one-sided conditional gap (LGP) that fails (`C(β)≈15`). **No volume-uniform variance bound at β≈2.5.** **(ii)** Herbst proves `LSI ⇒ tails`, never the converse; concentration ⇏ LSI ⇏ Poincaré (a bounded space concentrates trivially, a bottleneck has Poincaré →∞). **(iii)** The Savvidy negative modes do **not** break *concentration* — bounded oscillation is Hessian-sign-blind and gives the proven block-local gap `e^{−2βN_p(B)}/C_G` — they break only Bakry–Émery. The **volume, not the curvature**, kills uniformity. The exact missing bound is a volume-uniform LSI/Poincaré, equivalently `C(β)<1`, equivalently `m_eff²(0)>0` — the k→0 transverse mass, i.e. the gap.

## 1. Product Haar concentrates uniformly; the physical measure does not
Product Haar on `G^E` has single-site LSI constant `C₀=1/ρ_N=O(1)` (Ric SU(N)≥ρ_N>0: ½g SU(2), ¾g SU(3)), and LSI tensorizes with **no loss in |E|** — genuine dimension-free concentration. But `μ_Λ` is this Haar tilted by `e^{−βH}`, `H=Σ_p(1−Re tr U_p/N)∈[0,2N_□]`, `N_□=6(L/a)⁴`.
- **Holley–Stroock:** `osc(βH)=2βN_□` over the torus, so `LSI(μ_Λ)≤e^{2βN_□}C₀=e^{cL⁴}`. For a *local* `F` the gradient budget `E|∇F|²` is `O(1)`; the tilt inflates the **constant**, not the gradient: `Var_{μ_Λ}(F)≲e^{cL⁴}`. Volume-uniform: **false**.
- **McDiarmid / bounded-difference** is a theorem for *product* measures. Its constants are local (each link lies in 6 plaquettes, `c_x≤2β·6=12β`), so locality is *not* the obstruction — **μ_Λ is not a product**. The Doob–martingale (Lu–Yau / Nachtergaele) version is volume-uniform **iff** the one-sided conditional gap `γ_j≥γ` holds uniformly in scale and boundary (the LGP). At β=2.5 the inter-block influence sum is `C(β)≈15≫1`, the Neumann series diverges: no uniform bound.

Honest answer to (1): product Haar yes, Wilson measure no. Block-locally the constant *is* volume-uniform (do HS at the block, `osc=2βN_p(B)` fixed — PROVE-H2); gluing blocks is the failing step.

## 2. Herbst runs one way only
Herbst: `LSI(C) ⇒ P(|F−EF|≥t)≤2e^{−t²/2C}`. The implication web is unidirectional rightward:
`BE(Γ₂,ρ) ⇒ LSI(2/ρ) ⇒ {Poincaré(2/ρ),  T₂ ⇒ Gaussian concentration}`.
Neither Gaussian concentration nor Talagrand `T₂` upgrades to LSI without extra convexity; Bobkov–Götze give the *equivalent* strong form for **all** `f`, not merely Lipschitz `f`. And concentration alone cannot give Poincaré: a *bounded* space has sub-Gaussian tails for every Lipschitz `F` trivially, yet two equal clusters joined by an ε-neck have Poincaré constant `~1/ε→∞`. A gap needs the LSI/Poincaré constant *itself*; concentration is its shadow.

## 3. Savvidy: sign vs volume
Bakry–Émery is dead at β=2.5: `min(Ric+Hess S_W)≈−2.3` (Hess reaches `−1.1β`; W6-BE), no `CD(ρ,∞)`, no BE-LSI. But bounded-oscillation concentration is *blind* to the Hessian sign — `osc(βH)` on a fixed block is `2βN_p(B)=O(1)` whether `H` is convex or not. So the negative modes do **not** break concentration as they break BE: the block-local gap `e^{−2βN_p(B)}/C_G` is proved uniformly in `U′,L,a,boundary` (PROVE-H2). What breaks *global* uniformity is extensivity (`e^{cL⁴}`) plus the failing inter-block contraction, **not** the curvature sign. The large `L⁴` volume is the culprit; the concavity is orthogonal to it. Answer to (3): concentration survives the negative modes block-locally; the volume, not the sign, kills it.

## 4. Exact missing bound — and it is the gap
A global gap follows from a volume-uniform Poincaré/LSI: `∃C` independent of Λ with `Var_{μ_Λ}(F)≤C Σ_x μ_Λ(|∇_x F|²)`. Its martingale equivalent is `C(β)<1` (Zegarlinski), iff the boundary-uniform conditional gap `γ_j≥γ` (Nachtergaele LGP), iff `m_eff²(0)≥γ>0` — the k→0 transverse mass. These are one object (CONVERGENCE-redteam §c). Numerically `G″(0)≈+0.043−0.208=−0.165<0` (VAR-FINAL): the missing positivity is disfavoured, and concentration supplies none of it.

**Sharpest line.** Concentration is the shadow the gap casts, not a lever to lift it: Herbst is LSI⇒tails (never back), bounded oscillation is Hessian-blind but extensive, and BE is sign-broken. Every concentration route terminates on the same volume-uniform constant — the LSI/Poincaré constant, `C(β)<1`, the k→0 transverse mass. Concentration cannot manufacture it; it *is* the gap.
