## 1 · Steelman — the color factor is exactly attractive
Two gluons in the color singlet `δ^{ab}`; one-gluon (t-channel) exchange. With `C₂(adj)=N` and `C₂(singlet)=0`, the exchange eigenvalue is `(0−N−N)/2=−N=−C_A` (verified `N=2,3`), magnitude **larger** than the `qq̄` value `−(N²−1)/2N=−4/3`. So the singlet glueball channel is the *most* strongly color-attractive two-body channel in the theory — the standard reason one-gluon-exchange constituent models order `0⁺⁺<2⁺⁺` (Barnes–Close). This is exact group theory, nonperturbative in `g`, and vanishes for the free field (`g²=0`) ⇒ the *color* sign is genuine and acid-test-clean. The cubic vertex `A·[A,A]` is, as K0-TRANSVERSE-DECOUPLING states, simultaneously the mass generator and this J=0 binder.

## 2 · Attack (a): attraction ≠ positive-definiteness
The J=0 BS kernel is a matrix in helicity/Lorentz space, `K_{λ₁λ₂,λ₁'λ₂'}(p,p')`. Only its **color** projector is sign-definite. The pieces are opposite-signed: the magnetic (transverse) exchange is attractive, the instantaneous Coulomb is attractive in the singlet, but the **4-gluon seagull** `ff`-contact is *repulsive* (opposite sign, screening the exchange) and the **ghost/self-energy** insertions (covariant gauge) are sign-indefinite — the effective propagator inherits the Oehme–Zimmermann `∫ρ_A=0` sign change. The J=0 helicity projection `⟨0|K|0⟩` is therefore **indefinite**; "positive-definite at low momentum" (ask a) is **FALSE**. It is attractive *on average* in color, indefinite mode-by-mode.

## 3 · Attack (b): the threshold obstruction (the crux)
Two **massless** gluons have continuum threshold `s_th=0`. A stable bound state is a pole at `m_B²<s_th`; if `s_th=0` that demands `m_B²<0` — no particle. Any J=0 pole at `m_B²>0` lies **in** the two-gluon continuum (a resonance), not below it. Hence attraction alone **cannot** produce an isolated `m_{0⁺⁺}>0` "lifted from 0": it binds *relative to a threshold it cannot itself raise*. An isolated massive glueball needs the constituent threshold `2m_g>m_B>0`, i.e. the **single gluon must carry a mass gap `m_g>0`** generated by the kernel's self-energy. That `m_g>0` is exactly the gauge-fixed, sign-indefinite, non-cohomological dynamical mass of ATOM-FRG-mass / PROOF-FRG-DSE-GAP-EQUATION; nothing transports it to a physical positive-norm `0⁺⁺` threshold. **The BS route is circular at the threshold** (MS-GAP-EQUATION: the positive branch needs a `k→0` tachyon absent at β=2.5).

## 4 · Attack (b'): direction and ladder control
Even granting `m_g>0`, the BS eigenvalue is the **minimum** of a Rayleigh quotient over two-gluon wavefunctions ⇒ a rigorous computation yields `m_{0⁺⁺}≤(trial)` — a **lower bound on binding / upper bound on mass**, the wrong way for the gap. The ladder truncation is IR-uncontrolled: `g²N/4π=O(1)` at the binding scale, crossed-ladder/vertex corrections are same order, and the truncation breaks the STI so its output is scheme/gauge-dependent (the DSE precedent). A lower spectral bound needs **coercivity of the full two-body Hamiltonian**, not the sign of an attractive force — a strong attraction is precisely what could drive `m→0` (the dissolution channel), never what proves `m≥λ`.

## 5 · Attack (c): the Savvidy tachyon enters the loop
The tachyon is `1⁻⁻`; `1⁻⁻⊗1⁻⁻⊃0⁺⁺`, so a **tachyon pair feeds the J=0 kernel at second order** (K0 §2). The BS loop integrals run over all internal momenta, and at β=2.5 the negative directions are **90–97% orthogonal** to the coarse positive slice (W6-BE; min `≈−1.1β`, 22–49% negative) — so coarse-mode positivity (W7, `G″>0`) does **not** decouple from the kernel's internal integration.
