# G1-fixed-volume — UVS(L) without the gap: what actually closes

**Verdict (判词).** At fixed L the k→0 and box-scale-strong-coupling obstructions **drop out of a-uniformity**: the free box gap 4π²/L² is a-uniform and positive (P1-L0), and the box scale is an a-independent finite factor, so its strong coupling cannot break UVS(L). What remains is exactly one UV inequality — **BREAK-IN-1's large-field negative mode, un-rescued by GAP-G1's typical-field cone-emptiness because coercivity controls the action's value and not its Hessian** — plus the separate (U2) scheme match. Not closed; but the residue is now a weak-coupling *UV* statement, and "large-field at strong coupling" is *misplaced* as a UVS(L) residual (it is the a-independent G2/volume-uniform wall).

**What genuinely closes at fixed L.**
1. *UV summability (scale-sum).* Σ_{j≤J}g_j⁴ → (2β₀)⁻⁴ζ(4) < ∞ (BREAK-IN-1) splits into an a-independent O(1) (small-j) term plus a UV tail → 0. Only the tail could break a-uniformity; it vanishes. So the UV contribution to any moment bound is uniformly controlled — legitimate.
2. *No G2.* β(a)=2N/g²(a)→∞ as a→0, so every UV shell has g_j≤g*. The Gaussian reference contracts at c_L=4π²/L², a-uniform (P1-L0); the k→0 residue (Λ_{j+1}→0) that forced a mass input is cut off at 2π/L>0. UVS(L) needs bounded moments, not clustering — G1⊥G2 is genuine here.
3. *Box scale does not obstruct a-uniformity.* At fixed L the box-scale (small-j) coupling is g²(L)≳1–3 (a-independent), the intermediate β≈2.5 regime. It affects only the size of the finite constant C(L); a fixed, a-independent subsystem cannot itself make the bound diverge as a→0. So the Savvidy strong-coupling wall is a stage-2 / G2 object, not a UVS(L) residual.

**The one residual, pinned.**
- **(c) UV large-field log-concavity.** Coercivity ∫TrF²≥0 bounds the action's *value* below, giving the Gaussian tail and E[|F_j|]≲g_j (GAP-G1 (a)). It says nothing about the **Hessian**: on |F_j|≫g_j the Savvidy cone √(2g_j|F_j|) re-enters the shell band and the lowest fluctuation eigenvalue ∝−g_j|F_j|<0 survives (BREAK-IN-1, every j). Cone-empty (GAP-G1 (i)) removes the *typical* cone at weak coupling, but marginal log-concavity needs G″≥δ, and Var≥0 **subtracts** (G″≤E[H]); it is strictly harder than E[H]>0 — not implied by cone-empty or coercivity. This is the W4 §1 one-loop box / L1, at the weak-coupling UV where it is *plausible* (large-field weight e^{-c/g_j²}) but unproven.
- **(U2) subtraction.** Scale-sum controls R_j, not the tree a⁻⁴ divergence of tr F². UVS(L) needs the moments renormalized in the **same scheme C** as the RT — a lattice↔RT all-orders match (W2 §2 marginal BPHZ). A separate input; the three tools are silent on it.
- *Not residuals:* uniqueness (IN-1 is subsequential, GL3; a unique limit is IN-6/G2, separable and not needed as stated); large-field at strong coupling (see point 3).

**Honest reduction.** The live content of UVS(L) is (c) — a two-sided UV large-field log-concavity — together with (U2) scheme matching; the factorial clause (U3) is bookkeeping once (c) gives n-uniform control. Both live residues are UV and neither is the gap: fixed volume isolates them cleanly, which is why IN-1 is ⊥ G2. No fabricated proof: the three proposed tools are necessary and each removes one obstruction (scale-sum: summability; cone-empty: typical cone; coercivity: tail mass), but **none supplies the two-sided inequality (c)** — the residue is one missing sign, the same one-sidedness that blocks EDGE-f₁² project-wide.
