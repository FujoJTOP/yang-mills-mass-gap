# H1-T7 — Equivariant / topological localization vs. the pure-YM mass gap

> 2026-11-23 · Fresh-framework probe on H1. **Verdict: localization is genuinely different as a *solvability* tool and structurally *incapable* as a *gap route*, for one reason: it computes Q-cohomology (indices, anomaly coefficients, exact partition functions), and the mass gap is precisely a non-Q-closed spectral datum. It needs a nilpotent supercharge pure YM lacks; every "solvable point" supplying one (SUSY, S⁴, 2D, large N) does not continue to the 4D pure-YM IR. A localized lower bound on `f₁²` dies on the SCHUR-CONVEX master obstruction. No fabrication.**

**1. What localization needs.** Witten's equivariant/BV localization: a nilpotent supercharge `Q` (`Q²=0` or `Q²`=isometry+`L_ξ`), a `Q`-exact deformation `t{Q,V}`; `Z` is `t`-independent, and `t→∞` localizes the path integral onto the fixed locus of `V` — a finite-dimensional integral/sum over saddles (Atiyah–Bott; Berline–Getzler–Vergne). This solves Pestun's N=2 on S⁴, Nekrasov's Ω-background, Vafa–Witten, Witten's 2D YM. **The essential ingredient is a fermionic `Q` with a compact fixed locus.** Pure YM has gauge BRST, but `tr F²` is closed, not exact; there is no second, scalar supercharge and no fermion to twist.

**2. Even granted `Q`, it cannot see the gap.** Localization evaluates `Q`-closed observables and exact `Z`. The spectrum — hence the gap — lives in the KL measure / poles of correlators, not in `Q`-cohomology. In a topological twist `T_{μν}={Q,G_{μν}}`, so all metric/energy dependence drops out: "mass gap" is not even defined. In a SUSY-QFT localization (Pestun) `Z` is exact but carries only the integrated density of states; saddle contributions *sum to `Z`* — they are not eigenvalues, so "read the gap off the saddle spectrum" is a category error. In N=2 the prepotential gives exact **BPS** masses — protected, and the pure-YM lightest glueball (`0⁺⁺`) is not among them. Where the gap *is* known with SUSY (N=1 SYM, gaugino condensation) the proof is an EFT–anomaly argument, **not** localization.

**3. Non-SUSY twist?** A twist needs a scalar supercharge from extended SUSY/R-symmetry; pure YM has none. One may add adjoint fermions (N=1), localize, then decouple — uncontrolled at strong coupling, and pure YM is not a small perturbation of any SUSY point. The S⁴ result is the finite-`R` curved theory; the R⁴ gap is the `R→∞` limit, uniform control of which is G1/G2. The genuine non-SUSY cousin — Lefschetz-thimble localization of the real path integral — is *asymptotic*, and at the crossover the relevant saddles are exactly Savvidy's negative-Hessian directions (W6-BE-at-2.5, `β≈2.47`): the same wall.

**4. A localized lower bound on `f₁²`?** Every localization output is `g`-continuous and survives `g→0` (a `Q`-cohomology/anomaly datum, deformation-invariant; the free anomaly is present at `g=0`). At `g=0`, `f₁²=0`. By SCHUR-CONVEX no such datum can imply `f₁²≥r₀` — it returns the wrong cone (atom ≤ total), identical to H1-T5-NCG. Constructing one *is* `H*(r₀)`.

**Sharpest line.** Localization's power is exactness; its blind spot is the same exactness: it computes what is *protected*, and the gap is the unprotected residue. New tool? No — a sharpened **negative** result: no exact / topological / localization method can see the gap, because the gap is not a `Q`-cohomology datum. That isolates the wall as irreducibly dynamical, and the "localization ⇒ gap theorem" as non-existent.
