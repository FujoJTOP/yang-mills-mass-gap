# PROOF-GAUSS-INVARIANT-PF — Perron–Frobenius on the physical (Gauss-law) Hilbert space

> 2026-11-23 · 4D pure SU(N) Yang–Mills, Wilson lattice (β=2N_c/g², γ=β/N_c=2/g²). Target: does the transfer matrix restricted to gauge-invariant states — H_phys = ker Gauss = L²(𝒜/G, μ̄) — give a Perron–Frobenius positivity the full/gauge-fixed space lacks, and does it reach the first excited 0⁺⁺? **Verdict: the gauge-invariant transfer matrix is positivity-improving (Perron–Frobenius) — a genuine finite-volume theorem that cleanly avoids the Gribov/FP-sign obstruction of gauge fixing — and it gives a simple strictly-positive vacuum and a Krein–Rutman gap. It does *not* reach the first excited 0⁺⁺.** The obstruction is structural and gauge-blind: the vacuum is 0⁺⁺ and the lightest scalar glueball is *also* 0⁺⁺, so the Perron vector is the vacuum and the glueball sits in Ω^⊥, the one face where the single reflection-positivity cone degenerates. Gauge invariance buys the vacuum and the gap, never the edge. No fabrication.

**(1) The gauge-invariant transfer matrix is positivity-improving.** In the link basis the OS/Lüscher transfer matrix T=e^{−aH} (H=(g²/2)ΣE²+magnetic ≥ 0) has kernel T(U,V). By Trotter it is a limit of the electric heat kernel of the Laplacian on the connected compact 𝒜=G^{E} — K_t(g,h)=Σ_λ d_λ χ_λ(g⁻¹h)e^{−tC_λ}>0 for t>0 — times e^{−a·magnetic}>0. Hence T(U,V)>0 for **all** U,V. Since T(gU,gV)=T(U,V), it descends to a strictly positive kernel T̄(ω,ω') on 𝒜/G; and for 0≤f∈H_phys, f≠0, (Tf)(U)=∫T(U,V)f(V)dV>0 everywhere. So T maps the cone 𝒞 of nonnegative invariant functions into its interior: Perron–Frobenius at *every* β>0, finite V. This uses the **full positive Haar** measure, no gauge slice, so no Gribov copy or FP sign enters — the honest content of "a positivity gauge-fixed PF lacked": gauge-fixing replaces Haar by sign(Δ_FP)·(signed), killing the cone, while the invariant pushforward μ̄ restores it. The *full* space carries the same heat-kernel positivity, but its λ₁ may lie in a gauge-charged superselection sector (unphysical); the invariant restriction is what makes the PF vacuum and gap **physical**.

**(2) Strictly positive vacuum and Krein–Rutman gap — finite volume only.** At finite V, 𝒜/G is compact and T̄ is Hilbert–Schmidt, positivity-improving, self-adjoint. Jentzsch/Krein–Rutman: λ₀=1 simple with Ω≡1 strictly positive, and 0≤λ₁<λ₀ (RP gives T≥0), so Δ_V=−(1/a)ln λ₁>0. But (i) λ₁ is the lightest state over **all** channels, not the 0⁺⁺ specifically; (ii) inf_V Δ_V>0 is the mass gap and is **not** obtained — PF/Dobrushin constants scale like e^{−cV} (P4 §3), so finite-V gaps are *upper* bounds on Δ_{ℝ⁴}; (iii) at a→0 compactness fails, and the free field (RP, PF, strictly positive vacuum) has Δ_V→0. Krein–Rutman yields the finite-box gap, an upper bound on the mass gap.

**(3a) Gauss law / characterizing 𝒜/G is circumvented, not solved.** One never parametrizes 𝒜/G (singular, no global section — Singer). Positivity is inherited by restriction to the invariant subspace: the cone is nonnegative *invariant* functions on G^{E}, the measure μ̄ is the Haar pushforward. Solving Gauss's law blocks explicit coordinates, which the PF argument never needs.

**(3b) It does not reach the first excited 0⁺⁺ — the exact obstruction.** The vacuum is J^PC=0^{++} and the lightest scalar glueball is also 0^{++}: they share the channel. The 0⁺⁺-projected T_{0++} is still positivity-improving (symmetry projection = group average of strictly positive kernels), so its Perron vector is the **vacuum** (top of the sector) and the glueball is the second state, in Ω^⊥∩(0⁺⁺) — exactly where the single RP cone degenerates (a Perron functional annihilates every excited vector; INEQ-R1's "second cone absent"). Two structures would be needed, neither supplied by gauge invariance: **(a)** a second invariant cone / total positivity (Karlin flag) with O|Ω⟩ interior to the ψ₁-cone — but G^{E}/G carries **no order**, so no Gantmacher–Krein/TP cone exists (PROOF-F1SQ iii); **(b)** isolation of m₁ below the two-glueball threshold — strictly stronger than the gap (gapped RP theories with f₁²=0 exist: massive free scalar, mass-spectrum Thm B). Gauge invariance **does not help vs full-space PF at the edge**: vacuum and glueball share the channel on both spaces, and PF pins the top of a sector, not the separation of two states *within* one. Decisive check **on the physical space**, not the full one: free Maxwell (Gaussian, g→0) gives T̄ positivity-improving, Ω=1 simple strictly positive, a finite-V gap — yet the 0⁺⁺ spectrum is a two-photon cut from 0, f₁²=ν({0})=0, no isolated glueball. Gauge-invariant PF + strict positivity + finite-V gap **⇏** a glueball atom — INEQ-R1's free-field counterexample, relocated onto ker Gauss and uncured by it.

**Sharpest line.** Gauge invariance makes Perron–Frobenius *legitimate and physical* — positive Haar pushforward, no Gribov, physical vacuum and gap — retiring the Gribov/FP-sign worry. But PF is anchored at the top eigenvalue of a channel, and the first excited 0⁺⁺ lives in the very channel (0⁺⁺) whose top is the vacuum; the Perron vector is the vacuum and the glueball is in Ω^⊥, the one face where the RP cone degenerates. Gauge invariance reaches the vacuum and the gap, never the glueball; the edge obstruction is exactly as gauge-blind as INEQ-R1 found on the full space. The glueball needs a second invariant cone on an orderless quotient **plus** threshold isolation — the same edge/H*(r₀) wall, untouched.
