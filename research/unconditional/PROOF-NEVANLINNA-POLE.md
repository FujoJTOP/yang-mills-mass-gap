# PROOF-NEVANLINNA-POLE — can OS positivity plus the ∞-germ FORCE a real-axis pole (residue `f₁²>0`) of the Stieltjes transform?

> 2026-11-23 · Nevanlinna–Pick / Schur–Potapov / AAK probe of the pole-versus-cut question. `G(z)=∫dν(s)/(z−s)` is Herglotz (`Im G>0` in `ℂ⁺`); `f₁²=ν({m₁²})>0` ⟺ `G` has a **pole** on the real axis at `m₁²`, while the a.c. part of `ν` is the **cut**. **Verdict: the germ does NOT force the pole — verified by two mechanisms; `m₁>0` does not help; the only forcing subclass is `{isolated edge}` + a threshold `Λ`, whose bound is verbatim the residue–level-gap lemma, i.e. `H*(r₀)`. Nothing constructed.**

**Dictionary — KL already gives a cut.** `C(t)=Σf_n²e^{−m_nt}` makes every a.c. component a branch cut of `G`; the pole is the *extra*, singular (atomic) datum `f₁²`. The germ is `G(z)=ΣM_kz^{−k−1}`, `M_k=∫s^k dν` — the moments, UV-renormalized (`k≥1` divergent). Is a residue at a *finite* interior point a function of data at `∞`? That is the analytic-continuation step INEQ-T1 flagged.

**Mechanism 1 — the germ fiber is convex; the residue ranges over `[0,λ_N]`.** For any *interior* truncated germ, Christoffel–Darboux gives the **upper** end `f₁²≤1/K_N(s₀,s₀)=:λ_N` at every order; the atom-free representative realises `0` (Krein–Nudelman: an interior point's mass fills `[0,λ_N]`). At `m₁²=1`, `(M₀,M₁)=(2,4)`: `δ₁+δ₃` has edge atom `1`, Lebesgue on `[1,3]` has `0` — **identical germ, same edge, atom `1` vs `0`** (verified). At every finite order the lower end `0` is attained.

**Mechanism 2 — Schur free parameter / semicontinuity.** The Cayley image of `G` is a Schur function whose germ is pinned by a Potapov–Redheffer map with a **free Schur parameter**; real poles of `G` are the parameter's boundary-value-`1` set, fixed arbitrarily (zero to infinitely many). AAK ties rational approximation to Hankel singular values; only *finite-rank* (rational ⟹ finitely atomic) symbols force real poles — not YM. And the atom is **upper**-semicontinuous (portmanteau) while germ-functionals are continuous: a continuous functional cannot lower-bound one jumping `0↔λ_N` at fixed germ. This is the Nevanlinna face of INEQ-R5.

**Free field — same germ TYPE: YES.** Free massless `ν` is a.c. on `[0,∞)`, pure cut, `f₁²=0`, a legitimate element of the same Herglotz cone. The class therefore *admits* `f₁²=0`; no OS-derived, germ-defined predicate separates the interacting point from an atom-free one. Determinacy would be *uniqueness*, not a certifiable inequality (INEQ-T1), and protected moments are finitely many and UV-scheme-dependent; the free **massive** field is the converse — it *has* an atom yet nothing forces it. The axioms admit, never force.

**Mass bound `m₁≥m₀>0` — orthogonal.** `supp ν⊂[m₀²,∞)` fixes *where* `ν` lives, not *whether* it atoms: Lebesgue on `[m₀²,m₀²+2]` is atom-free with edge `m₀²`. Mass and atom are independent coordinates; the support-bounded germ fiber still spans `[0,λ_N]`.

**The forcing subclass exists — and it IS the conditional lemma.** The one added hypothesis that forces a pole is **isolation**: `supp ν={m₁²}∪[Λ,∞)`, `Λ>m₁²`. Then `G−f₁²/(z−m₁²)` is Herglotz on `[Λ,∞)`, and eliminating the rest gives `f₁²≥(ΛM₀−M₁)/(Λ−m₁²)>0` iff `M₁/M₀<Λ` (verified) — exactly the **residue–level-gap lemma** (GAP-G2-edge), with `Λ` a bound on the *second* level. Density/Hardy constraints on the a.c. part are silent on the singular part; boundary Nehari/Pick data either presuppose the pole or give only upper modulus bounds retaining atom-free interpolants.

**Sharpest line.** A pole is a real-axis *boundary* singularity on `supp ν`; the germ is interior-type data at `∞`. Nevanlinna–Pick/Schur admit every atom-free germ-representative, and the atom is upper-, never lower-, semicontinuous — outside every continuous germ-functional's reach. `m₁>0` moves the support, not the atom. Forcing the pole needs isolation (the second threshold `Λ`), and "isolated edge + `Λ`" *is* the residue–level-gap lemma, i.e. `H*(r₀)`. The function-theoretic route terminates at the same `E₂`/gap wall. *(Theorem: CD bound, Krein–Nudelman interval, Schur–Potapov parameter, portmanteau, residue–level-gap. Modelling: UV-divergent moments as an asymptotic germ — irrelevant to the no-go, true at every truncation.)*
