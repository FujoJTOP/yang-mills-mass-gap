# IDEA — Finite-size criterion at physical coupling β≈2.5

**Verdict.** The Nachtergaele martingale criterion is the only YM-applicable uniform-gap machine (the Knabe/Gosset–Mozgunov *finite-size criteria* are frustration-free only; the Kogut–Seltzer YM model is frustrated, so they do not apply). Its two inputs split cleanly: **(i) the finite-block gap** is a *finite certified computation* (a lattice-scale O(1) number, not the wall); **(ii) the local-gap-stability = the wall**, and it is provably equivalent to the k→0 transverse dynamical mass, i.e. to the gap itself. Pinned, not attacked.

**The criterion, exactly.** For concentric blocks Λ_j (side ~2^j), the carré du champ Γ, and conditional expectations E_{Λ_j}, Var_μ=Σ_j μ Var_{Λ_j}(E_{Λ_{j-1}}f). Each increment obeys a conditional Poincaré inequality with constant 1/γ_j, where γ_j is the **conditional gap of the block Λ_j given the exterior boundary condition**. If γ_j≥γ>0 uniformly in scale *and* boundary configuration, and the base block has gap ≥γ, then Δ_Λ≥γ for all Λ ⇒ bulk gap [Nachtergaele, CMP **175** (1996) 565; Nachtergaele–Sims survey, MPRF **12** (2006)].

**Input (ii), precisely.** LGP = "finite-volume spectral gap stable under boundary/parameter change": γ_j≥γ for **every** boundary configuration the exterior supplies, at every scale. The dangerous exterior carries a slowly varying transverse background; the block then hosts the transverse mode of momentum p~2^{-j}, free eigenvalue λ_min=(4/a²)sin²(πa/2^j)→0 (= P1's marginal mode). LGP requires this lifted to ≥γ uniformly in j. **That is exactly m_eff²(0)≥γ>0**, the W6 induced mass / P1's c=m². So LGP ⟺ transverse dynamical mass: massless ⇒ γ_j≲(π/2^j a)²→0; gapped ⇒ LGP. Not weaker than the gap — it *is* it, rephrased as boundary-uniform conditional stability.

**Why SC works and β=2.5 fails.** At β<1/48 the conditional gap is bounded by the *local plaquette* term, uniformly in boundary data (measure near-product/Dobrushin). At β≈2.5 the binding is long-range and the k→0 mode is unprotected, so the largest annulus's conditional gap is set by the would-be massless transverse mode. The regime, not the technique, is what strong coupling buys.

**Openings examined, all closed.** (a) Combes–Thomas localization: gap-stability under a boundary change decays e^{-ℓ/ξ}; needs ξ<∞ = finite correlation length = the gap (circular). (b) Perron–Frobenius: gives RP-sector uniqueness/positivity, not 1−λ₁; quantitative PF is e^{-cV}. (c) Quasi-factorization / approximate conditional independence: high-temperature only; degenerates at low T exactly at the gap. (d) *Probabilistic* LGP (exponentially small weight on bad boundary conditions): the large-deviation rate is the effective action whose k=0 curvature is W6's G″≈−0.165; collapses to the sign of the marginal curvature = the gap. (e) Certified tiny-block numerics: valid for (i) but blind to large-j LGP.

**Input (i).** A β=2.5 single-plaquette/2⁴ block measure e^{βReTrU} sits in the concentrated β>1 regime with O(1) gap; a rigorous certified lower bound is a finite (interval) computation — the *easiest* piece, but only the base case. It cannot supply the physics-bearing constant.

**Sharpest line.** At β≈2.5 the finite-size criterion is non-circular but relocates the gap into one boundary-uniform conditional-gap lemma that equals the k→0 transverse mass; it does not weaken the problem.
