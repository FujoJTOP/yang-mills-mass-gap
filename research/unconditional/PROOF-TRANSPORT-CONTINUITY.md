# PROOF-TRANSPORT-CONTINUITY — the clopen strategy for `f₁²` closes at openness, fails at closure by assuming the isolation it must transport

> 2026-11-23 · Attempted proof that `S = {β∈[1/48,2.5] : f₁²(β)>0 and the 0⁺⁺ atom is isolated below threshold}` is clopen, hence all of the interval, transporting the residue to `β=2.5`. **Verdict: the topological skeleton is sound and the OPEN half is a fixed-`L` theorem (Kato–Rellich + continuity), but the CLOSED half fails. Closure needs *two* pointwise-to-uniform upgrades: `m₁(β_n)` bounded below (the mass gap at `β*`) for *isolation*, and `f₁²(β_n)` bounded below (the binding) for the *residue* — neither is provided. "The lightest state cannot dissolve" is the wrong picture (a lowest isolated eigenvalue dies by *rising into its threshold*, `Λ−m₁²→0`), Elitzur forbids a local order parameter, not a phase transition, and `2.5<β_c` holds only at `T=0` / `N_t≳8`; at `N_t=4` SU(2) deconfines at `β_c≈2.30<2.5`. No transport theorem.**

**1. The skeleton is sound — and empty.** `[1/48,2.5]` is connected; a clopen subset is `∅` or all. `1/48∈S` (strong coupling: PROOF-P3's `f₁²≥r₀>0`; the kinematic isolation of PROOF-ISOLATION). So *if* `S` is clopen, `S=[1/48,2.5]`. The topology is correct; all content sits in the two half-properties.

**2. OPEN closes, at fixed `L`.** On `T⁴_L` the transfer matrix `T_β` is a finite matrix analytic in `β` (character sum). The `0⁺⁺` atom is a **simple** eigenvalue `e^{−a m₁}`, isolated with `Λ−m₁²=δ>0`. Kato–Rellich gives an analytic branch and an analytic rank-one projector `P(β)=|G(β)⟩⟨G(β)|`, so `f₁²(β)=|⟨Ω|O|G(β)⟩|²` is continuous; `f₁²(β*)>0` plus continuity gives `f₁²>f₁²(β*)/2>0` on a neighborhood, and `δ(β)` stays positive. Hence `S` is open. (At `L=∞` the bound state below threshold is Kato-stable under a small relative perturbation — but this is the *easy* direction and is not the wall.)

**3. CLOSED fails — dissolution, precisely.** Take `β_n→β*`, `β_n∈S`. Upper semicontinuity `f₁²(β*) ≥ limsup f₁²(β_n)` makes the *residue* half free **whenever** `f₁²(β_n)≥r>0` uniformly. Two requirements remain and neither is supplied by pointwise membership:
- *Residue:* the sole escape is `f₁²(β_n)→0`. On a finite lattice `f₁²` is real-analytic and may **touch zero** (`~(β−β*)²`), keeping `f₁²>0` nearby while `f₁²(β*)=0`; usc permits exactly this. Excluding it needs `⟨Ω|tr F²|G⟩≠0` — the glueball decay constant, dynamical; the trace anomaly fixes only the *diagonal* `⟨G|T^μ_μ|G⟩=m_G`.
- *Isolation:* closure needs `m₁(β_n)` bounded below, or `Λ−m₁²≥κ` at `β*`. In the grading `Λ≈(1.5 m₁)²`, so `Λ−m₁²≈1.25 m₁²` vanishes **iff `m₁→0`** — i.e. the gap closes at `β*`.
The claimed guard *"lightest ⇒ stable ⇒ cannot dissolve"* is misdirected: a lowest isolated eigenvalue is not swallowed from below; it dissolves by **rising to meet its absorption threshold** (or the threshold descending onto it), and that merge condition *is* `Λ−m₁²→0`, hence `m_{0⁺⁺}→0`. The guard assumes its conclusion. *Elitzur* is a non-sequitur: it forbids a local **gauge-variant** order parameter, and is silent on gauge-**invariant** local observables (energy density jumps at a first-order transition); the deconfinement order parameter is the nonlocal Polyakov loop.

**4. Even the gap does not close it.** PROOF-F1SQ's admissible OS counterexample — massive free scalar, `m>0`, gap present, `O=:φ²:` — has `f₁²=0`, spectrum on `[4m²,∞)`. A **binding** datum, not the gap, manufactures the residue; closure needs the isolation lemma `Λ_β−m₁²≥κ` (verbatim `H*(r₀)`), strictly above the mass gap.

**5. `2.5` vs `β_c` does not rescue it.** At `T=0` (the Clay setting, `N_t→∞`) there is a single confining phase and `β_c(N_t)→∞`, so `[1/48,2.5]` is transition-free — but uniform-`T=0` control is itself unproven. At finite `N_t`, SU(2) deconfines at `β_c(4)≈2.30`, `β_c(8)≈2.51`, so `2.5<β_c` needs `N_t≳8` (numerical input), and at `N_t=4`, `2.5>β_c` with the interval straddling the second-order transition. `β=2.5` is intrinsically the SU(2) window (SU(3): `β_c(4)≈5.7`); "below `β_c`" is not a theorem either way.

**6. What would close it.** A uniform lower bound on `[1/48,2.5]` of `m_{0⁺⁺}`, of `Λ−m₁²`, and of `f₁²` — i.e. Theorem A's `I1∧I2` all along the window. The clopen route does not bypass the analyticity wall (W2); it relocates it to one interior point and demands the same input there. A clean reformulation, not a proof.

**Sharpest line.** Clopen + connected + nonempty ⇒ all is valid and vacuous: openness is a fixed-`L` theorem, closure is circular. A lowest isolated eigenvalue dies by *rising into its threshold* (`Λ−m₁²→0`, i.e. `m_{0⁺⁺}→0`), not by a crossing from below; Elitzur is a non-sequitur; and `2.5<β_c` fails at `N_t=4`. And the gap is *insufficient* — a gapped free field has `f₁²=0` — so closure needs the isolation/binding lemma, strictly stronger than the gap. The residue is transported only by proving exactly the object whose transport was the goal.
