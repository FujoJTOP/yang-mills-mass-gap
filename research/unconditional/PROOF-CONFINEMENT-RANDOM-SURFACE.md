# PROOF-CONFINEMENT-RANDOM-SURFACE — does the CPS/CNS surface framework give σ>0 at β=2.5?

> 2026-11-23 · **Verdict: No.** The Cao–Park–Sheffield / Cao–Nissim–Sheffield surface-and-loop-equation framework proves a rigorous area law only in a **small-coupling ('t Hooft-scaling) regime** $\lambda<\lambda_0$, uniform/improving in $N$ — *not* at finite $N$ and $\beta=2.5$. There is **no positive-weight regrouping** reaching the crossover: the surface sum is the signed Weingarten expansion, and the positive cancellation is the missing ingredient they flag. So (1) σ>0 is **not** proven at 2.5; (2) no such regrouping exists in these papers; (3) the confinement theorem and the $\sigma\!\Rightarrow\!m_{0^{++}}$ scaling are therefore **conditional**, and the second step is a measured transmutation identity, *not* a consequence of the area law.

**What CPS–CNS actually prove (verified from full texts).** CPS (arXiv:2307.06790, *Comm. AMS*) give an **exact** expansion of $\langle W_\gamma\rangle$ as a sum over embedded maps for *any* $N\ge1,\ \beta>0,\ d\ge2$, with edge weights the **Weingarten function** — signed. It is a representation, not an area law: no convergence, decay, or positive measure. CNS-a (arXiv:2505.16585, Thm 1.2) proves area law for pure $U(N)$: $\exists\,\lambda_0(d)>0$ with $\lambda<\lambda_0(d)$, all $N\ge1$, $\ |\langle W\rangle|\le C_{1,d}C_N^{|\gamma|}e^{-C_{2,d}\mathrm{area}(\gamma)}$. Its own Remark 1.3: "really a result about large enough N." CNS-b (arXiv:2509.04688, Thm 1.6) extends via the Durhuus–Fröhlich mass-gap condition + SZZ Bakry–Émery to the **'t Hooft regime** $\lambda\in(0,\lambda_0)$, $\lambda_0^{SU(N)}=1/(8(d-1))$, for $G\in\{U(N),SU(N),SO(2(N-1))\}$, $N\ge2$. Both are small-$\lambda$ (small-coupling) theorems.

**Where β=2.5 sits — far outside.** Standard $\beta=2N/g^2$; SU(2) at $\beta=2.5$ has $g^2N=2N^2/\beta=3.2$, SU(3) at $\beta=5.7$ likewise $\approx3.2$. The CNS 't Hooft threshold is $\lambda_0=1/(8(d-1))=1/24\approx0.042$ (d=4) — the crossover coupling is $\sim75\times$ above it. CNS-a Remark 1.3(5)/1.4 state outright: "it remains a major open problem to show area law for large $\lambda$ and $d=4$ for arbitrary $N\ge1$ (e.g. $N=2$ or $N=3$)"; "treating small N and large $\lambda$ is of particular interest." β=2.5 *is* that open corner.

**Positive regrouping? No.** The CPS surface weights are signed at the local level (Weingarten; Euler-characteristic sign for SU(2)). CNS-a Remark 1.4 says extending the regime "might require showing that a certain sum of surface weights is small, even though the sum of the absolute values … is large; … the positive and negative weights very nearly cancel." That cancellation is the open sign problem (cf. PROOF-RANDOM-CLUSTER); no positive Gibbs/FKG measure is constructed.

**Q3 — the conditional theorem and scaling.** *Grant* σ(β=2.5)>0, uniformly in $L$. Then (i) **confinement**: $V(R)=-\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\log\langle W_{R\times T}\rangle\ge\sigma R$, a linear static potential; (ii) the **1-form / flux-tube** mass is $m_1=\sqrt\sigma>0$. But $m_{0^{++}}$ is a *0-form* (plaquette) transfer-matrix gap — logically **not** implied by the area law (PROOF-STRING-TENSION-STABILIZER, area law ⇏ gap). The relation $m_{0^{++}}=c\sqrt\sigma$, $c=m_{0^{++}}/\sqrt\sigma\approx3.5\!-\!4$ (Morningstar–Peardon, constant across the scaling window), is a measured transmutation identity, not a theorem. So even granting σ(2.5)>0, $m_{0^{++}}>0$ still needs the open glueball–string step.

**Sharpest line.** The random-surface framework proves area law only for *small λ* (their 't Hooft/small-coupling parameter, best at large N); at the finite-N crossover β=2.5 the physical 't Hooft coupling $g^2N\approx3.2$ is O(1), ~75× above the threshold $\lambda_0=1/24$, and the authors themselves list "small N, large λ" — exactly this point — as open. Proven: σ>0 for λ<λ₀ (and the 't Hooft regime) at any N≥2. Open: σ>0 at β=2.5; any positive regrouping; and σ>0 ⟹ m_{0⁺⁺}>0 (a physical identity, not a corollary).
