# ATOM — How far can strong-coupling bounds reach toward β≈2.47?

**Verdict.** The peer-reviewed mass-gap radius ends at Shen–Zhu–Zhu's β<1/48 (Hooft-scaled), equivalently Hooft coupling λ≡g²N>96 — about 13× too large in λ versus the crossover λ≈7.3 (β_W≡2N/g²≈2.47, SU(3)). Curvature refinements buy ≲30% (β<195/7168, unrefereed); no 2023–2026 result reaches β~1 or ~2. Area-law methods reach far larger β but prove confinement, not the transfer-matrix gap. The barrier is structural for the absolute-convergence class — an analyticity wall, not a constant to optimize — though not a thermodynamic transition.

**1. The mass-gap reach.** Osterwalder–Seiler (Ann. Phys. 110 (1978) 440): a volume-uniform mass gap by convergent cluster expansion at small β, with a threshold that shrinks as N grows under Hooft scaling. Shen–Zhu–Zhu (arXiv:2204.12737; CMP 400 (2023) 805) prove LSI + Poincaré + exponential clustering, hence a strictly positive mass gap, for SO(N)/SU(N) in every d under |β|<1/(16(d−1)); in d=4 this is β<1/48, independent of N. Their action is S=βN Σ_p Re Tr U_p, so β<1/48 ⟺ λ>96. The crossover λ≈7.3 gives β≈0.27 in the same units (β_W≈2.47 in standard Wilson β_W=2N/g²). The naive "1/48 vs 2.47" ratio is inflated by the N² in the Hooft convention; the honest distance is ≈13× in λ (or β_W: 0.19 vs 2.47).

**2. New techniques.** For the mass gap: none. The only 2026 claim (gauge-orbit-curvature preprint, unrefereed) pushes 1/48→195/7168≈0.027. Cao–Nissim–Sheffield (2505.16585) prove the Wilson AREA LAW for β<c_d, independent of N; their dynamical sequel (2509.04688) reaches the Hooft regime β~N. Both are confinement results — area-law ⇒ mass-gap is unproven. Adhikari–Cao (Ann. Prob. 53 (2025) 140) work at weak coupling but only for *finite* gauge groups — no transfer to continuous SU(N).

**3. Technical or structural?** Structural for cluster/LSI/BPHZ. Each is an absolute-convergence argument whose radius is the failure point of the high-temperature/Dobrushin expansion — an analyticity wall that better constants shift only by O(1), never cross. Its constructive-RG shadow is the harmonic scale-sum Σ_j g_j²~log J (W3-marginal): no cutoff-uniform absolute BPHZ bound. Beyond the radius the uniform Poincaré/LSI constant *is* the k→0 transverse mass = the gap (P4; IDEA-finite-size), so "extending the radius" = proving the gap. This is not a true phase boundary — SU(3) has a single confining phase and a smooth crossover — but the expansion still cannot be analytically continued across it.

**Sharpest reachable β:** mass gap β<1/48 (λ>96; β_W<0.19 for SU(3)); ≈0.027 unrefereed. **Exact obstruction:** the convergence/analyticity radius of absolute methods. The one non-absolute escape — the loop-equation/area-law route — crosses to large β but stops at area law, one hard step short of the mass gap. That step is the Clay problem.
