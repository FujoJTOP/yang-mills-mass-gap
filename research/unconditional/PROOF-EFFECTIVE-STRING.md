# PROOF-EFFECTIVE-STRING — can the Nambu–Goto string give m_{0⁺⁺}, or is it a long-string tool that stops at 1/√σ?

> 2026-11-23 · Effective string theory (Lüscher–Weisz; Aharony–Komargodski) is rigorous **only as a long-string asymptotic expansion**, valid for `L√σ≫1`. **Verdict: no rigorous intermediate-`R` closed-string spectrum, no `m_{0⁺⁺}` from NG + corrections, and no rigorous positive lower bound.** The glueball is a closed flux tube of length `L~1/√σ`, where the expansion parameter is `1/(σL²)~O(1)`: the EFT sits exactly at its breakdown. The leading Lüscher term is universal, but *as a coefficient of a large-`R` expansion*; above the universal orders every coefficient is non-universal and unknown, and the truncated NG closed-string energy has **no interior minimum** (monotone, then complex at `L≲1.5/√σ`). Effective string is a large-`R` description that does not reach the glueball scale. The rigorous `m_{0⁺⁺}>0` is the lattice gap (SZZ), not the string; the value `m_{0⁺⁺}/√σ≈3.3–3.9` is measured, not derived.

## (a) What is rigorous — and it is asymptotic in 1/(σL²).
For an open flux tube (static quarks) the long-distance potential is `V(R)=σR−(D−2)π/(24R)+O(1/R³)` (D=4: `σR−π/(12R)`); for a closed tube wrapping a length `L` it is `E(L)=σL−(D−2)π/(6L)+O(1/L³)`. The `1/R` (Lüscher) term is fixed by the central charge `c=D−2` of the transverse modes and is universal (Lüscher–Symanzik–Weisz; Lüscher–Weisz 2002, who confirmed it and noted the crossover at "surprisingly small distances"). For **long closed strings**, Aharony–Komargodski prove that all terms in the `1/L` expansion **up to and including `1/L⁵` are universal**; **non-universal terms appear at `1/L⁷`**, and past `O(1/L⁵)` the EFT loses predictivity to the string core. This is the precise content of "long-string universality": a statement about the *coefficients* of an asymptotic expansion, controlled by `1/(σL²)`.

## (b) The glueball is at L√σ~1 — the edge of validity.
The flux tube has width `~1/√σ`; a closed tube of the glueball's size has `L~1/√σ`, so `1/(σL²)~1`. Every term in the expansion is then as large as the leading one, the unknown `1/L⁷`+ operators carry the microscopic (`Λ^{-1}`) scale, and their coefficients have no symmetry protection. NG can even be fitted down to `L√σ≈2` (the tube "only twice as long as it is wide") — but that **precocity is empirical**, outside the radius of convergence, with an uncontrolled error bar; it is not a theorem that the EFT is controlled there. At the glueball scale **universality fails**: only the qualitative flux-tube picture survives, no rigorous closed-string spectrum.

## (c) No lower bound.
The NG closed-string ground-state energy `E₀(L)=σL√(1−π(D−2)/(3σL²))` is **monotone increasing** and reaches `0` at `L_c=√(π(D−2)/(3σ))≈1.45/√σ` (D=4), below which it is **complex** — the tachyonic/unphysical regime. It has no interior minimum, so it yields no positive bound; the naive `E≥σL` fails because a closed loop's length is unbounded below (`σL→0`). The mass is set by the cutoff — the string thickness `~1/√σ` (or `Λ^{-1}`) — a UV datum absent from the EFT. So the effective string gives **no mass lower bound**; `m_{0⁺⁺}>0` is the lattice mass gap, and `m_{0⁺⁺}/√σ` is the measured transmutation ratio (PROOF-STRING-TENSION-STABILIZER), not a string prediction.

**Sharpest line.** Effective string is a controlled asymptotic expansion whose control parameter is `1/(σR²)`; the glueball is the one closed string it cannot describe, because it sits exactly where that parameter is `O(1)`. The Lüscher term is a universal *coefficient of a large-`R` expansion*, not a tool that reaches the glueball — the honest reading is that `m_{0⁺⁺}`, like the gap itself, is a short-distance datum the long-wavelength string does not contain. NG is not even a fundamental string (light-cone quantization is anomalous in `D≠26`); it is an EFT whose higher terms vanish only as `1/(σL²)→0`.
