# PROOF-COMPUTER-ASSISTED — validated numerics for a finite-lattice gap and residue f₁²>0

> **Verdict.** Both candidate routes are feasible *in structure*, are the *same* engine, and yield a genuine **finite-volume** theorem — but not the physical object. The engine: Peter–Weyl truncation of the spatial state space ℋ=L²(G^{E_V}, Haar), an electric-energy tail floor, a Feshbach/Schur reduction, and a certified (interval / Krawczyk / Kato–Weyl) eigenvalue enclosure. It returns a rigorous lower bound on the gap E₁(H_V) and — with certified eigenvector enclosure — a finite-volume residue f₁²_V>0. It cannot reach the mass gap: a finite-V gap is an **upper** bound, Δ_V≥Δ_∞=inf_LΔ_L, so certifying Δ_V>0 certifies the box, never the limit. And the gauge-invariant truncated space grows ~exp(c·L³·log d): only the 0+1D extreme 1³ (3 links) is certifiably diagonalizable; 2³ (24 links, ≳10¹⁰ states) is not. The real gain is that the analytic isolation-regress becomes a *certified finite matrix*; the wall relocates unchanged to uniform-in-(V,a) control.

**Why the transfer matrix is infinite-dimensional, and where the tail is still controlled.** With H_K the Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian and T_V=e^{−aH_V}, the operator acts on ℋ=L²(G^{E_V}), infinite-dimensional even at V=1³ (a Peter–Weyl ⊕). Direct diagonalization is impossible. Two facts make a *rigorous* truncation possible. (i) E² is diagonal in the representation label with eigenvalue the Casimir C₂(R), unbounded above: a tail keeping a large rep has a computable energy floor. (ii) The magnetic term is a *bounded* multiplication operator carrying one fundamental per plaquette link — a one-step fusion — so it couples the retained space only to the adjacent representation shell; ‖PHQ‖ is explicitly bounded, not assumed.

**The exact procedure** — `(a)` Rayleigh–Ritz+Kato/Weyl and `(b)` tiny-volume interval arithmetic are the same object seen two ways. Fix a ceiling Λ; P_Λ = reps with C₂≤Λ, Q=1−P_Λ. On Q, QHQ ≥ γ=(g²/2)c(Λ)−b, where c(Λ) is the next Casimir above Λ and b≥‖V_mag‖ ≤ N_p·N/g². Form A=P_Λ H P_Λ (algebraically exact or interval), certify its spectrum with a verified eigensolver (Krawczyk / interval Newton / IntLab). For E<γ the Schur complement gives spec(H)∩(−∞,γ)=spec(F), F(E)=A−PVQ(QHQ−E)^{−1}QVP; the bounded correction yields E_i(H)∈[a_i−η, a_i+η], η=‖PHQ‖²/(γ−E_max). Hence the gap bound E₁≥a₁−η, and an enclosure of the first-excited eigenvector (Davis–Kahan) gives f₁²_V≥c_V>0 once 0 is excluded from the certified overlap box. Nothing is sampled. *(Pleijel's nodal-domain theorem, listed among candidate "lower-bound" tools, is not a spectral-enclosure lemma and supplies nothing here; the operative bounds are Weyl/min-max and Kato–Temple.)*

**The finite-volume statement.** "For the lattice Λ_{V,a} (spatial torus V, spacing a, coupling g), with per-link rep ceiling Λ whose electric floor γ exceeds the certified window: the transfer matrix e^{−aH_V} has E₁(H_V)≥a₁−η>0, and the lightest 0⁺⁺ state has residue f₁²_V≥c_V>0." All quantifiers are finite — a true theorem about the regulated lattice.

**The exact obstruction to anything more.** (i) *Direction:* Δ_V↓Δ_∞, so this is an upper bound; a lower bound on Δ_∞ needs E₁ uniform in V, i.e. the gap. (ii) *Volume:* gauge-invariant dim ~ exp(c·L³·log d) — 1³ is tiny (3 links) but the 2³ threshold (24 links) already exceeds certified dense linear algebra, and the cost is exponential in the L⁴ Euclidean site count; one cannot march to L→∞. (iii) *Continuum:* a→0 forces β→∞, Λ→∞ along the RG trajectory with no uniform control of γ or η. (iv) *Residue:* f₁² is upper-semicontinuous, so c_V>0 at fixed V does not transport — the state can dissolve into a continuum threshold.
