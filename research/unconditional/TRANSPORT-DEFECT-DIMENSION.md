## §2 The structural comparison — why 2D point defects work and 4D ones do not

The task's premise: 2D `CP^{N−1}` has a proven gap via a *dilute, controllable* point-defect gas; 4D YM does not. The structural difference is **dimension**, in three coupled ways.

**2.1 Codimension is dimension-relative ⇒ "point-like" does not transfer.**
A defect of codimension `k` in `D` dimensions has a `(D−k)`-dimensional worldvolume.
- In **`D=2`**, the `CP^{N−1}` instanton is **codim-2 = 0-dimensional = a point**: the transverse space is `0`-dimensional, so it has **no shape and no shaping entropy** — only a position. Its interactions form a **2D Coulomb gas** (logarithmic potential), for which Debye/Kosterlitz–Thouless screening and a **convergent cluster expansion** are available (`[R]` Fröhlich–Spencer 1981; the rigorous Coulomb-gas/vortex literature). The topological susceptibility `χ_t~Λ⁴` is **IR-finite** — there is no `∫dρ` over a size that can diverge, because there is no independent size.
- In **`D=4`**, the **same codimension-2** object (the center vortex) is a **2-dimensional surface**: shape/area moduli, **extensive entropy** `~e^{+cA}`, an **area-law/perimeter competition** rather than a log plasma, and (non-abelian) **signed** surface weights ⇒ no FKG/positive percolation. The would-be point is the **codim-4 instanton** — but a codim-4 solution in a classically conformal 4D theory carries a **scale (dilatation) modulus `ρ`** whose one-loop distribution (below) peaks in the **IR**, not the UV.

**2.2 The scale modulus of the codim-4 point: benign in 2D, malignant in 4D.**
Both the 2D and 4D instantons are *classically* conformal, so the *classical* action is `ρ`-independent (`S_cl = 2π|Q|/g²` in 2D; `8π²/g²` in 4D). The difference is the **one-loop density and its IR behaviour**:
- **4D** (`[V-here]` Part 1, standard `[R]` Callan–Dashen–Gross; Schäfer–Shuryak):
  `D(ρ) ∝ ρ^{−5}[8π²/g²(ρ)]^{2N}e^{−8π²/g²(ρ)}`, `8π²/g²(ρ)=b\ln(1/ρΛ)`, `b=11N/3`.
  Peak at `\ln(1/ρΛ)=2N/(b−5)` ⇒ `ρ* = e^{−2N/(b−5)}/Λ`, with `b−5>0` ⇒ the power `ρ^{b−5}` **grows toward the IR**. The dimensionless packing `Π := ρ*⁴ D(ρ*)/C₄`:

  | `N` | `b=11N/3` | `ρ* (1/Λ)` | `S_inst(ρ*)` | `Π` (shape/overlap) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 7.33 | **0.180** | 12.6 | **0.48** (marginal) |
  | 3 | 11.0 | **0.368** | 11.0 | **8.0×10¹** (DENSE) |
  | 4 | 14.7 | 0.437 | 12.1 | 5.8×10³ |
  | 5 | 18.3 | 0.472 | 13.8 | 5.5×10⁵ |

  (`C₄` = one-loop prefactor, `O(1)`–`O(10⁻¹)`; quote the **order**.) The **mass-generating instantons are the largest in the problem** (`ρ*~O(1)/Λ`, IR) and the gas is a **liquid** (`Π≫1` for `N≥3`). The Kotecký–Preiss/Ursell expansion (which makes the mass a theorem) needs `𝔡<1` — it **fails exactly where the mass lives**. This reproduces `E2-FLIP-TRANSPORT §4.1` with a clean `N`-scan.
- **2D:** the defect is a point with no independent size that can grow while the position is fixed (the dilatation mode is a conformal modulus mixed into the position by the `D=2` conformal group); the density is a 2D Coulomb gas at `n~Λ²` with **IR-finite** `χ_t`, and the mass `m~Λ` is the **single** scale (`[R]` Witten 1978). **Diluteness and mass-generation coincide at `Λ` — and they can, because the object is a point.**

**2.3 The honest caveat on the 2D side.** Witten's `1/N` is a controlled **large-`N` asymptotic** result for `m~Λ`; the *rigorous cluster/instanton-gas* mass gap is established for the abelian-Higgs/Coulomb-gas/vortex cases (`[R]` Fröhlich–Spencer) and for `O(3)`/`CP¹` in the dilute regime; a fully rigorous all-`N` finite-`N` `CP^{N−1}` gap is itself a hard open problem. What is unambiguous — and all that the comparison needs — is the **structural** fact: in 2D the defect is a **point** with **log interactions** and an **IR-finite** density, so a *dilute gas at the mass scale* is geometrically possible; in 4D the codim-4 point has a **scale modulus with an IR density peak (packing `O(1)`)** and the codim-2/3 objects are **extended with shape entropy**. **That** is the dimension-dependent obstruction.

## §3 The dichotomy — statement and proof sketch

**Theorem (defect-channel dichotomy, for the standard 4D defect family). [NG for the named class; with [T] channel + [T] min–max + [V-here] packing]**
Let `𝔇` be a standard 4D gauge-theory defect of codimension `k ∈ {1,2,3,4}` carrying a diluteness control parameter `𝔡` (fugacity `ζ` or packing `n·(\text{size})^d`). Suppose `𝔡≪1` **at the mass-generating scale on `ℝ⁴` (undeformed)**. Then `𝔇` is **not** in the neutral 0-form `0⁺⁺` channel; precisely, one of the following holds:
1. `k=1` ⇒ **not dilute** (codim-1 = volume-filling; extensive, sub-extensive entropy), **and** at `θ=0` the object **does not exist** (unique vacuum). ⟹ wrong phase.
2. `k=2` (vortex) or `k=3` (monopole/'t Hooft line) ⇒ **1-form** (magnetic `Z_N^{(1)}`) order parameter (non-local surface/line), **or** its order parameter is gauge-dependent after abelian projection (Singer/Elitzur). A local point operator carries no 1-form charge, so `tr F²` is blind; the controlled mass is `√σ`/`m_D`, reaching `f₁²` only via the measured `m_{0⁺⁺}=c√σ`. ⟹ wrong channel.
3. `k=4` (instanton) ⇒ the controlled (dilute) end is `S_inst(ρ)≫1 ⟺ ρ→0` (UV / lattice `a→0`), where the generated mass `→0` in the continuum; at the physical `ρ*~1/Λ` the packing is `O(1)` (dense), so "dilute" fails. Its native dilute-gas order parameter is the **topological susceptibility `χ_t` / `U(1)_A` (pseudoscalar `0⁻⁺`) mass**, not the scalar `0⁺⁺` pole. ⟹ wrong phase (and native channel `0⁻⁺`).

*Proof.* (1) is the codimension–entropy fact (`[T]` geometric: a codim-1 object's entropy is `~(\text{area})^{...}` sub-extensive vs its tension `∝` volume^{`(d−1)/d`}; one wall dominates, never a dilute gas) plus the `θ=0` uniqueness (`[T]`, `THETA-PI`; walls exist only at the `θ=π` level crossing). The four horns are **exhaustive** because a defect is a submanifold of definite codimension `k∈{1,2,3,4}` in `D=4`, and each `k` is one horn. ∎ *(Quod erat: for the named family. This is a classification of standard defects, not a proof about non-defect mechanisms.)*

**Why this is one dichotomy, not four accidents.** The codimension `k` fixes the **sector**: `k=2` (surface = symmetry operator) and `k=3` (line = charged object) are the **same 1-form / magnetic** sector; `k=4` is **local** (the only 0-form/dressed by the instanton number); `k=1` is an **interface** (0-form order parameter, but not a gas). ## §4 The near-exception, stated precisely — the magnetic bion

The **only** object in the table that is genuinely *both* dilute (`ζ~e^{−S₀}≪1`) *and* massive is the **magnetic bion / caloron** on `ℝ³×S¹` (Ünsal `[R]` 0709.3269; Ünsal–Yaffe 0803.0344; Poppitz 2111.10423). - **(iii) Backward direction.** By min–max `m(S¹_L) ≥ m(ℝ⁴)`, so a small-`L` lower bound is an **upper bound** on the physical gap (`SMALLCIRCLE-ADIA §3`); locating the crossover `L_c=1/T_c∝1/Λ` reintroduces `c=m₁/Λ`.

So the bion is dilute and massive but **fails channel (i) and theory/direction (ii,iii)**. It is the exception that **confirms** the dichotomy once the dichotomy is stated correctly: *dilute at the physical scale ⟹ 1-form OR wrong phase/theory.* The bion is the "dilute" case whose escape is precisely the 1-form channel.
