# PROOF-NO-MELT-PHASE — β_c≈2.47 is a running-coupling crossover and a finite-N_t deconfinement, not a T=0 transition; on a single-phase T=0 interval the spectrum is real-analytic, so a *discontinuous* melt is excluded

> 2026-11-23 · Phase-structure test of "does the 0⁺⁺ glueball melt at β_c≈2.47?". **Verdict: β_c≈2.47 is not a T=0 infinite-volume transition. The number conflates two distinct structures and neither lives at T=0. (1) It is the λ≈7.3 crossover of the running 't Hooft coupling in the SU(3) 't Hooft normalization, `β_eff=2N_c h∨/λ=18/λ=2.47` — a *crossover*, a rapid but finite-derivative change, not a non-analyticity. (2) It is the SU(2) finite-T deconfinement at fixed `N_t≈7–8` (`β_c(N_t): 2.30→2.51`), a genuine Svetitsky–Yaffe transition but on a finite temporal circle — gone as `N_t→∞` at T=0. On any interval where the T=0 theory is a single connected confined phase, every correlator and the glueball mass are real-analytic in β (exactly on finite `V`; at infinite `V` by Kato plus absence of Lee–Yang/Fisher zeros), so a *discontinuous* reorganization at 2.47 is forbidden. Two honest limits fix the scope: (i) transition-freeness of all of `[1/48,2.5]` is unproved — only `β<1/48` (Osterwalder–Seiler) and the AF/continuum limit are controlled; (ii) a *continuous* dissolution (`f₁²→0`, `m₁` rising into threshold `Λ−m₁²→0`) is analytic, not a phase transition, and remains the dynamical (K2) question.**

**1. What β_c≈2.47 is — two objects, neither T=0.** *(a) Running-coupling crossover.* With `λ=h∨g²`, `β=2N_c h∨/λ`; for SU(3) `β_eff=18/λ`, so 2.47 ↔ λ≈7.3 (the project's λ≈7.3 "crossing point" numerics). This is a smooth, sharp-but-analytic crossover of `λ_G(μ)` — a finite slope, no singularity, hence no spectrum reorganization in the thermodynamic sense. *(b) Finite-T deconfinement.* SU(2) deconfines at `β_c(4)≈2.30 … β_c(8)≈2.51`, passing 2.47 near `N_t≈7`. Svetitsky–Yaffe fixes the class by the center `Z_N` (3D-Ising second order for SU(2); first order for SU(N≥3)); it is a true transition, but only at fixed `N_t` (`T=1/N_t a>0`). For SU(3) the bare β=2.47 sits far below deconfinement (`β_c(4)≈5.7`), so there it is not even a deconfinement. The 2.47 coincidence is two structures; neither is a T=0 phase transition.

**2. The T=0 β-axis: one confined phase, no known transition.** Confinement persists at every β at T=0; 4D pure SU(N) has no bulk transition (contrast compact 4D U(1)). The area law is *proved* for `β<1/48` (Osterwalder–Seiler; SZZ radius) and expected throughout; scaling is smooth. Elitzur forbids a *local* gauge-variant order parameter, so no local symmetry breaking can force or forbid a transition — but Elitzur does **not** by itself exclude a transition (the finite-T order parameter is the nonlocal Polyakov loop, and there is none at T=0). The supported picture is a single connected confined phase on `[1/48,2.5]`; rigorously the middle is open — only the strong-coupling and asymptotic-freedom ends are controlled.

**3. Analyticity ⇒ no discontinuous melt; continuous dissolution is separate.** On finite `Λ` the Wilson kernel is entire in β, so every correlator and `m₁` (Kato, where `λ₁` is simple) is real-analytic. The infinite-volume limit can lose analyticity only where Fisher/Lee–Yang zeros reach the real axis — i.e. at a phase transition. On a single-phase interval `m₁` and `f₁²=a(β)²` are real-analytic: no jump, no branch point, no sudden reorganization at 2.47. A *continuous* melt — `a(β)` passing through 0, the atom rising into the `(gg)` threshold (`Λ−m₁²→0`, `m_{0⁺⁺}→0`) — is analytic and *allowed*; it is the (K2)/isolation dynamical event, not a thermodynamic transition.

**Sharpest line.** β_c≈2.47 is the λ≈7.3 running-coupling crossover (SU(3): `18/λ`) and/or the SU(2) finite-`N_t` deconfinement (`→2.51` at `N_t≈8`) — both non-T=0. On the T=0 axis there is no known transition in `[1/48,2.5]`; if single-phase, the spectrum is real-analytic and a *discontinuous* melt is impossible. But transition-freeness is unproved across the whole interval, and a *continuous* dissolution is analytic — a dynamical question, not a phase-structure one.
