# PROOF-HEAT-KERNEL — the character/heat-kernel gap is the fiber, not the 0⁺⁺ mass

> **Verdict.** The Wilson transfer matrix is a heat kernel only in the Hamiltonian (a_t→0) limit and only in its **electric** factor; at finite a it is a character-expanded operator equal to no single K_t. The heat-kernel/character gap — the lowest Casimir step C₂(fund)=(N²−1)/2N [SU(2): ¾; SU(3): 4/3], with the adjoint one step higher at C₂(adj)−C₂(fund) — is a **per-link, electric, UV** datum. It controls the fluctuation fiber ∇⊥ (the gluon/vertex sector), is not gauge-invariant, and is annihilated by the coarse low-pass. It therefore reproduces the already-proved **H2** and cannot reach the coarse ∇∥ 0⁺⁺ mode. At β=2.5 T_β is not any K_t and no comparison theorem is available, so heat-kernel positivity does **not** give the 0⁺⁺ gap; the missing estimate is H1 itself, on the **coarse** half.

**The exact kernel.** Osterwalder–Seiler give T_β as a positive kernel,
$$T_\beta[U,U']=\!\int\!\prod_x dU_4\;e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}[S_{\rm sp}(U)+S_{\rm sp}(U')]}\,e^{-\beta\sum_{\rm tp}(1-\frac1N{\rm Re\,tr}\,U_{\rm tp})},\qquad T=D^{1/2}\tilde T D^{1/2}.$$
The exact single-plaquette character expansion is $e^{\frac\beta N{\rm Re\,tr}\,U_p}=\sum_R c_R(\beta)\chi_R(U_p)$ with all $c_R>0$ (Drouffe–Itzykson). The heat kernel is the special coefficient set $c_R=d_R e^{-tC_2(R)}$: $K_t=\sum_R d_R\chi_R e^{-tC_2(R)}=e^{t\Delta_G}\delta$. It enters exactly in two ways. (i) **Hamiltonian limit** a_t→0, β_t→∞: $T\to e^{-a_tH_{\rm KS}}$ (Kogut–Susskind), whose electric factor is $\exp(-a_t\tfrac{g^2}{2}\sum E^2)=\bigotimes_{\rm links}K_t$ with $t\asymp a_tg^2$. (ii) **Heat-kernel action** (Menotti–Onofri): replace the plaquette weight by $K_t(U_p)$ — a distinct model, same continuum limit, $T_{\rm HK}\neq T_\beta$.

**The heat-kernel gap.** On L²(G), $K_t$ has eigenvalues $e^{-tC_2(R)}$ (multiplicity $d_R^2$); the spectral gap (rate) above the vacuum is $C_2({\rm fund})$. The task's $C_2({\rm adj})-C_2({\rm fund})$ is the further step to the adjoint. Both are per-link Casimir numbers.

**Why it cannot close 0⁺⁺.** Three exact reasons. *(a) Gauge invariance:* the lightest eigenvector is a single colored fundamental link — not a Gauss singlet; the lightest gauge-invariant electric state is a closed loop of energy (length)×C₂(fund), not a fixed per-link gap. *(b) Kinetic ≠ full:* $T_\beta$ is electric(heat kernel)⊗magnetic(Wilson potential); the potential carries confinement and the Savvidy negativity (Hess S≤−1.1β, W6), so bounding the electric factor does not bound $T_\beta$. *(c) Sector:* the Casimir is UV per-link, living in ∇⊥ (the transverse gluon, ATOM); the 0⁺⁺ mass is the k→0 delocalized coarse mode (GAP-G2 edge/localization), invisible to any per-link datum — exactly why H2 is easy (PROVE-H2) and H1 is the wall.

**β=2.5: T_β ≠ K_t.** The implied per-representation time $t_R=-\ln(c_R/d_R)/C_2(R)$ is R-dependent, spreading already between fundamental and adjoint, so $c_R(\beta)\neq d_R e^{-tC_2(R)}$ for any single t. The character expansion converges only at β<1/48 (SZZ/P3), where the gap is trivially large ~ln(1/β); β=2.5 is the crossover, where neither the convergent-character nor the small-t Gaussian regime is controlled. The per-link gap *is* b-independent, so it would give a b-uniform H2 if a uniform Gaussian comparison held — but the shared-link coupling of the block measure re-introduces the oscillation factor, leaving PROVE-H2's open item (ii) open too.

**Sharpest line.** Even the ideal identity $T_\beta=K_t$ would not help: the heat-kernel *lattice gauge theory* has the same open IR gap as Wilson, because the Casimir is a UV electric datum and the physical mass is the electric–magnetic balance of a delocalized loop. The missing estimate is the volume-uniform blocked log-concavity **H1** (sign of $G''(0)$, VAR-FINAL −0.165) — the coarse ∇∥ half; heat-kernel positivity lives on the fiber ∇⊥ half and leaves H1 untouched.
