# INEQ-R1 — Perron–Frobenius / positivity-improving: does it force f₁²>0?

> 2026-11-23 · Direction check: PF is the one structure whose native inequality points *atom-ward* (a lower bound on an atom weight, not a moment), so in principle it is the right shape to evade the off-diagonal / upper-semicontinuity obstruction of EDGE-f1sq. **Verdict: not constructed.** PF is anchored at the vacuum; f₁² lives at the first excited level; and even an ideal PF output (the gap) is strictly weaker than an atom. Free field is positivity-improving with f₁²=0. Honest; no fabricated bound.

**Proposed mechanism.** `T_t=e^{−tH}`, RP cone `C`. Assume (i) `T_t` positivity-*improving*, `T_t(C∖0)⊂int C`; (ii) `O≥0`, `O|0⟩≠0`. Then `O|0⟩` is a positive vector of the natural cone (positive op on strictly-positive Perron `Ω`). Naive hope: `⟨G|O|0⟩≠0` because its source is positive.

**What PF proves — the vacuum, not the edge.** Positivity-improving gives: `Ω` simple and strictly positive; for positive `u,v`, `⟨u|T_t v⟩>0` and `⟨u|T_t v⟩/⟨Ω|T_t v⟩→⟨u|Ω⟩` (**vacuum dominance**); with Krein–Rutman compactness, `λ₁<λ₀` (a **gap**). All three are anchored at `Ω` (the `s=0` vacuum). The only Perron amplitude PF bounds is `⟨0|O|0⟩>0` — the **diagonal** (INEQ-T4's object), never `f₁`. The KL correlator `C(t)=⟨0|O e^{−tH}O|0⟩=∫e^{−ts}dν` has **no vacuum pole**; its leading large-`t` term *is* the edge atom. PF's strength, vacuum dominance, is precisely the term absent here. PF removes the vacuum; the edge is what survives that removal, and PF leaves it free.

**Why indefinite `|G⟩` blocks a sign.** `f₁=⟨G|O|0⟩`. Since `Ω∈int C`, every excited eigenvector `ψ₁` is ⊥ the strictly-positive Perron functional `ℓ_Ω`, hence lies in the hyperplane `⊥ℓ_Ω` — it cannot be interior to any cone whose dual contains `ℓ_Ω`. So `|G⟩` is **necessarily sign-changing**. `⟨G|O|0⟩` is an indefinite functional evaluated on a positive vector: an unsigned integral. `O≥0` confers no sign; strict improvement spends itself making `Ω` positive and `|G⟩` oscillatory. Positivity of `ν` gives `ν({m₁²})≥0` but nothing forbids `0`: `C(t)` is a positive Laplace transform whether `ν` is atom or cut.

**Even ideal PF under-delivers.** The gap is `spec(H)⊂{0}∪[m₁,∞)`, `m₁>0`: `m₁` is the **bottom of the continuum**, not an eigenvalue. An atom `ν({m₁})>0` needs `m₁` to be an *isolated eigenvalue* (a stable glueball) below the 2-body threshold — a bound-state statement **strictly stronger** than the Clay gap. PF/gap does not supply it. (In 2D `P(φ)₂` a PF+gap argument does give a positive bottom residue, but only because the gap is independently proven *and* `φ`'s one-particle pole is isolated below `2m`; 4D YM has neither, and `O=tr F²` is composite.)

**Second cone absent.** To make `|G⟩` a Perron vector one needs a cone `C′` on the `ψ₁`-sector with `O|0⟩∈int C′`. RP supplies **one** cone; the excited sector is the face `⊥ℓ_Ω`, where `int-C′` degenerates. A flag of invariant cones (`ψ_n` in the `n`-th) is **total positivity** (Karlin sign-regularity) — RP gives only the top cone; total positivity of the 4D YM kernel is unproven and not a consequence of reflection positivity.

**Regime wall / circularity.** Strict improvement (`kernel>0`) is provable only in strong-coupling lattice `β<β₀` (cf. EDGE-f1sq r3, SCHUR r3), which cannot cross `β_c`; Krein–Rutman compactness fails in continuum 4D. "Positive-improving ⇒ gap" in 4D *is* the mass-gap problem, and even granting it yields an eigenvalue, not a residue.

**Free-field counterexample.** `g=0` is reflection-positive with Gaussian (strictly positive) transfer kernel and `O=−tr F²≥0`; the gauge-invariant spectrum is a two-gluon **cut** from 0, so `f₁²=ν({0})=0`. Thus positivity-improving + `O≥0` ⇏ `f₁²>0` — false as stated (SCHUR-CONVEX master obstruction, PF face).
