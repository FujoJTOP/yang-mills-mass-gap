# PROOF-DISSOLUTION-SPECTRAL-DENSITY — spectral density + OPE fix the *mechanism* of "dissolution" but not the *value* `f₁²`: the lightest 0⁺⁺ cannot empty into its own `(gg)` cut, so the dangerous dissolution is the OPE-blind one

> 2026-11-23 · The "(gg)-cut dissolution" question from the Källén–Lehmann / OPE side. **Verdict: the literal mechanism — the lightest 0⁺⁺ pole merging into the two-glueball cut — is spectrally impossible: the atom sits at `m₁² < (2m₁)² = s_th`, a β-\*independent\* factor-2 mass gap, and its nearest spectral neighbour is a same-channel *excited atom* (an avoided crossing), not the cut. But this constrains only the *form*. The residue can still vanish at a *fixed* below-threshold pole (the free-scalar mechanism), and that `f₁²→0` is invisible to the OPE (`s→∞`), to unitarity (upper only), and cancels in the trace-Ward (INEQ-T4). So spectral density / OPE do **not** force `f₁²>0`: it is again the usc / unprotected-edge wall.**

**Setup.** KL measure `ν(s)=Σ_i f_i²δ(s−m_i²)+ρ(s)`, `f_i²≥0` (OS/RP); atoms = stable one-particle states, `ρ` = multiparticle continuum. Interpolator `O_S=¼tr F²`, `f₁²=ν({m₁²})`.

**1. `s_th`, and why it is not the operative edge.** In pure YM the lightest glueball *is* 0⁺⁺ (lattice: `0⁺⁺<2⁺⁺<0⁻⁺` at every β through β_c; stable in N). The lightest multi-glueball 0⁺⁺ configuration is therefore **two ground 0⁺⁺ glueballs** (`0⁺⁺⊗0⁺⁺→0⁺⁺`, S-wave), so
` s_th = (2m_{0⁺⁺})² = 4m₁² `, `m₁≡m_{0⁺⁺}`.
The atom is at `m₁²`, **one quarter of `s_th`** (half in mass) — a ratio fixed by two-body kinematics, β-independent while 0⁺⁺ stays lightest. *But* support beyond the ground atom does not wait for `s_th`: C and P cannot separate `gg` from `(gg)(gg)` in 0⁺⁺ (both C⁺P⁺), and the excited 0⁺⁺ tower is also 0⁺⁺. The first excited 0⁺⁺ sits at **`≈1.5m_g<2m_g`** (PROOF-ISOLATION §4.4), so the operative `Λ=inf supp(ν−f₁²δ_{m₁²})` is the **excited atom, `(1.5m₁)²`** — the nearest competitor is another *atom*, 1.5× in mass above.

**2. Kinematic exclusion of pole-into-cut.** A state is stable iff `m<√s_th`. For the lightest 0⁺⁺, `m₁<2m₁=√s_th` at every β. A real isolated pole below its own continuum threshold cannot merge into the cut — that needs `m₁→2m₁`. Pole-into-`(gg)`-cut dissolution of the lightest 0⁺⁺ is therefore **spectrally impossible** (modulo confining-phase premises: 0⁺⁺ lightest, multi-glueball asymptotic completeness — both dynamical). The only same-channel coalescence is ground↔excited 0⁺⁺; those carry *identical* quantum numbers, so by von Neumann–Wigner they **avoid** crossing (a real crossing needs >1 parameter) — they repel, masses stay separated, residues vary continuously. No real-axis emptying. (This sharpens PROOF-TRANSPORT-ANALYTIC/CONTINUITY's "dissolution at `β*`": with `m_g=m₁` that merger would need `m₁→2m₁`; what survives is the avoided crossing or operator decoupling, not a continuum merger.)

**3. What OPE says about `ν` — and does not.** The trace anomaly is exact, `T^μ_μ=(β/2g)O_S`, the unique bridge into 0⁺⁺. The OPE `O_S·O_S` fixes the **`s→∞` asymptotics**: identity/central-charge coefficient `∝1/x⁸` (`c>0`), dim-4 gluon-condensate `⟨tr F²⟩` mixing `∝1/x⁴`, β-function logs. These are aggregate/UV/total data — they normalise the high-s tail of `ρ` and make the channel non-empty, but the OPE is a **short-distance expansion**, blind to a long-distance pole at `x∼1/m₁`. "Mixing with `⟨tr F²⟩`" is a Wilson-coefficient/contact statement, not spectral redistribution; an OPE cannot *leak* an atom into a continuum nor *absorb* a bound pole. Unitarity (`ν≥0`) is Cauchy–Schwarz/PSD — it bounds the continuum *above* (Caron-Huot–Pokraka–Zahraee: "positivity alone forces the minimal residue to zero"). The trace-Ward at the pole is homogeneous in `f₁²` (`f₁²⟨G|T|G⟩=(β/2g)f₁²g_GGG ⇒ f₁²` cancels), pinning `g_GGG`, never the edge (INEQ-T4).

**4. Same channel, different energies — the free-scalar template.** Atom at `m₁²` and continuum at `4m₁²` are the *same* 0⁺⁺ channel but spectrally *separated* by a fixed kinematic gap. The massive free scalar makes the logic exact: `O=φ` has `ν=Zδ(s−m²)` (atom, no cut); `O=:φ²:` — same gapped theory, same lightest scalar — has **`ν({m²})=0`** with support on `[4m²,∞)`. The residue is an *operator overlap*, not a spectral invariant of the state. YM's `O_S=¼tr F²` is the **`:φ²:`-analogue** (composite, dim-4); unlike the free `Z₂` there is **no selection rule** zeroing it (PROOF-TRANSPORT-ANALYTIC §4), so *identical* vanishing is excluded — but an **isolated overlap zero** at a fixed below-threshold pole is not.

**Sharpest line.** Spectral density / OPE **constrain the mechanism**: the lightest 0⁺⁺ is its own two-body threshold ingredient (`√s_th=2m₁`), so it lives at a quarter of `s_th` behind an avoided crossing — "(gg)-cut dissolution" as a pole emptying into the continuum is **not a real spectral possibility**. They **do not constrain `f₁²`**: the residue can still fall to zero at a *fixed real* pole (the free-scalar `:φ²:` mechanism), which is exactly the `s→∞`-blind, unitarity-invisible, trace-Ward-cancelling object. The honest reading is the usc wall restated: the *pole* is kinematically protected, the *weight* `f₁²=ν({m₁²})` is not — dissolution survives only in the operator-decoupling sense, which spectral density, OPE, unitarity and the anomaly all decline to constrain.
