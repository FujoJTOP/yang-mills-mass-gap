# OTHER-F1SQ-TRANSPORT — exact multiplicative transport + Kato analyticity + no-dissolution + strong-coupling I2 do NOT compose to `f₁²(2.5)>0`; the wall is isolation `I2` again, not the single-phase part

> 2026-11-23 · PROOF-MODE re-attack. Transport `f₁²(μ)=Z_O(μ,μ₀)²f₁²(μ₀)` (INVENT-TRANSPORT: exact, `Z_O²` a square, sign-controlling, `0→0`) composed with **(A)** Kato analyticity in `β` (PROOF-TRANSPORT-ANALYTIC), **(B)** kinematic no-dissolution (PROOF-NO-DISSOLUTION-KINEMATIC), **(C)** the strong-coupling I2 attempt (SUB2). **Verdict: no theorem. THE 5 scorecard — (1) nonperturbative ✔; (2) sign-controlling ✔ but hollow; (3) non-circular ✘ (each piece quotes the gap); (4) non-SUSY, fails free field ✔; (5) uniform lower bound ✘ unconditionally. Exact failure = `I2`, the upper gap one rung above `m_g` — NOT the single-phase part `[CP-a]`, which is *entailed by* `[CP-b]=I1∧I2` (CONTINUUM-CP §2).**

**The chain, and where each link quotes itself.** **(C)** P3 gives `f₁²(β_eff)≥r₀>0` at `β_eff<1/48` — **I1 only**; P3 explicitly *retracts* I2 (PROOF-P3 §5: the cost grading gives the unique leading tube = I1, *not* separation from the excited `0⁺⁺`). So the SUB2 direction supplies no strong-coupling I2 to extend. **(A)** Kato continues `f₁²` from the P3 base point only while the lightest `0⁺⁺` is simple and isolated (`λ₁>λ₂`, `m₁<2m_g`): that hypothesis *is* I2, and in the `L→∞`/continuum limit `λ₁` meets the continuum, so Kato isolation *is* `m₁≥μ`, the gap (PROOF-CP-COMBINE §3, PROOF-TRANSPORT-CONTINUITY §3). **(B)** "lightest cannot dissolve" ⇒ "no `β*`" only under (K-A) a *uniform* gap and (K-B) ordering — both unproven — and it pins the atom below the `2m₁` multi-glueball continuum, while the operative `Λ` for I2 is the C/P-blind **excited `0⁺⁺` at `~1.5m₁`** (PROOF-ISOLATION §4). So B bounds the wrong threshold, and "`m₁<2m₁`" is a tautology for `m₁>0`.

**Why the five fall as they do.**
1. *Nonperturbative* — ✔. Polymer expansion (P3), Kato spectral theory, kinematic margin: no coupling expansion anywhere.
2. *Sign-controlling* — ✔ but hollow. `Z_O²` is a square `>0` and *multiplies*, so the "total≤atom" coercion `1≤0` (B1) and the Schur-concave sign wall (EDGE-f1sq) never appear; yet it **carries** a sign that must already exist at 2.5 — a weight `Z_O²` multiplies cannot manufacture one.
3. *Non-circular* — ✘, the kill. (A) presupposes I2; (B) presupposes the gap + ordering; (C) supplies I1 not I2; and transport is an exact covariance *within one phase*, so it **is** `(CP)`, not a bridge to it (PROOF-CP-COMBINE §4). Circular twice.
4. *Non-SUSY / fails free field* — ✔. Pure YM; transport sends `f₁²≡0` (`:φ²:` in the massive free scalar, `a≡0` by `Z₂`) to `0`, the acid test passed by a homomorphism. The free field is precisely the `a≡0` overlap-zero case, excluded only by K2.
5. *Uniform lower bound* — ✘ unconditionally. Analyticity + `a≢0` gives only `f₁²(2.5)≥0`: a nonnegative real-analytic function may touch zero quadratically at 2.5 (an overlap zero `a(2.5)=0`); upper semicontinuity `f₁²(2.5)≥limsup` preserves a positive bound only if approaching values stay `≥r>0` — the residue bound again. `>0` needs K2 `a(2.5)≠0`, the endpoint residue itself.

**Exact failure — isolation, not single-phase.** `[CP-b]=I1∧I2`; I1 closes at strong coupling (P3, direct tube-recoupling positivity), I2 does not, and `[CP-a]` (single `T=0` phase) is *entailed by* `[CP-b]`, never the bottleneck. Uniform `I2` is `Λ−m₁²≥κ`; in the grading `Λ≈(1.5m₁)²`, so `Λ−m₁²≈1.25m₁²→0` **iff `m₁→0`**: uniform isolation ⟺ the gap. The transport law would force `f₁²=Z_O²r₀>0` across the window — *precisely because* it presumes the single phase with the atom carried through, i.e. `(CP)`. The composition therefore reduces `(CP)` to "pointwise `m₁>0` on `[β_eff,2.5]` + `a(2.5)≠0`" — the gap and the endpoint decay constant, unmoved.

**Sharpest line.** The multiplicative transport is the strongest piece — an exact, sign-controlling square that rides the free field to zero — but it is a *scale-covariance within one phase*, so it presupposes `(CP)` rather than supplying it. The three would-be suppliers of `(CP)` each quote it back: P3 supplies I1 and retracts I2; Kato's analyticity domain *is* I2; no-dissolution needs the uniform gap and aims at the `2m₁` continuum instead of the operative `1.5m₁` excitation. The failure is isolation `I2` — the upper gap, `H*(r₀)` — again; the single-phase part is downstream of it and unreachable without it.
