# CONTINUUM-FREDHOLM — the determinant zero `z₁=e^{am₁}` has no continuum limit: its renormalization *is* the marginal gauge BPHZ, and the zero coalesces into the Källén–Lehmann pole

> **Verdict (判词).** **The zero is not continuum-robust, and no renormalization makes it so; `f₁²>0` in the continuum is not obtained.** The fixed-`a` identity `f₁²=−(1/z₁)\,N_O(z₁)/D_O′(z₁)`, `D_O(z)=det(1−zT_O)`, is exact. Two things happen as `a→0`. *(i) Kinematic coalescence (new, coordinate).* `z_n=e^{aE_n}→1`: the atom sits at `z₁=e^{am₁}`; the next zero `e^{aE₂}` and the two-glueball branch point `e^{2am₁}` are only `~a(E₂−m₁)` away, so the isolation gap `→0`. Kato/spectral-flow tracking needs a bounded gap **and** a fixed Hilbert space; both fail (gap `→0`; `dim~Va⁻³→∞`). In the zoomed variable `E=(ln z)/a` the zero becomes the **Källén–Lehmann pole** at `m₁<2m₁` (PROOF-DISSOLUTION) — no longer a determinant. *(ii) Divergence = the same wall.* `Tr T_O^k=Z_{S¹_k×T³}` is a closed-4D partition function; its continuum limit needs gauge BPHZ counterterms (vacuum energy + marginal `Tr F²`) and the running coupling. **Renormalizing `D_O` literally *is* the marginal 4-D gauge BPHZ problem** (W2/W3/G1). The analytic obstruction is the **same marginal-BPHZ wall**; the only genuinely new fact is negative — the determinant zero is not an `a`-uniform object and reduces to the KL atom (`P3`).**

**1. Setup and the two limits.** At fixed `a`, finite `T³_L`, `T_O=P_O T P_O` (`T=e^{−aH}`, `O`-cyclic, vacuum subtracted) is finite-dimensional: `D_O=∏_{f_n≠0}(1−z e^{−aE_n})`, zeros `z_n=e^{aE_n}`, smallest `z₁=e^{am₁}>1` (no zero for `z≤1`); its physical content is `m₁=lim_{a→0}(\ln z₁)/a`. As `a→0` the pole count `~Va⁻³→∞` and the zeros fill `{e^{aE}:E∈spec H_O}`.

**2. (i) Coalescence blocks the Kato argument.** Kato needs the tracked eigenvalue simple, `dist(λ₁,spec∖λ₁)≥d₀>0` uniformly, on a *fixed* space. In `z` the gap is `z₂−z₁≈a(E₂−m₁)→0`; the ambient dimension diverges, so no fixed-space analytic family exists across `a→0`. Hence no `a`-uniform spectral flow: the zero is isolated at each `a` but its isolation radius `→0`. The ratio `m₁<2m₁` is `β`-free (PROOF-DISSOLUTION), so the *atom-pole* is isolated in `E` — but there it is the resolvent pole, not a determinant zero.

**3. (ii) The divergence is the gauge BPHZ divergence.** `log D_O(z)=−Σ_{k≥1}z^k Tr T_O^k/k`, `Tr T_O^k=Z(S¹_k×T³)`. The `a→0` singularity is the standard Seeley–DeWitt–BPHZ one: `∼k\,V₃\,a⁻³` (the `c₀` vacuum-energy term), the marginal `log` renormalising `Tr F²` through `g(a)∼1/(2β₀\ln(1/aΛ))`, plus the total-derivative `Tr F\tilde F`. Since `Tr T_O^p∼Va⁻³` diverges for **every** `p`, Carleman `det_p` fails at all finite `p`, and zeta resums only by presupposing the nonperturbative spectral determinant `= the measure`. This is verbatim W3's `Σ_jg_j²∼\ln\ln(1/a)` and G1's (★).

**4. Can any renormalization make the ZERO robust?** No. *Multiplicative* (`D_reg=e^{analytic}D_O`) preserves each zero but supplies no isolation — it moves neither (i) nor the all-orders character of (ii). *Zeta/Carleman* either alters the zeros (not a multiplicative factor) or diverges at every finite `p`. The only continuum-stable object is the resolvent `G`, whose atom is a **pole** at `m₁` with residue `f₁²=ν({m₁²})` — the original `P3` object. Renormalization relocates, never dissolves, the wall.

**5. Where it blocks; new or same?** The *analytic* block is the **same** marginal-BPHZ/analyticity wall — the determinant's counterterms are the gauge BPHZ ones and the residue still needs `P3`/isolation. The *new* ingredient is negative and kinematic: the determinant zero is a coordinate artifact of `z` that coalesces into the KL pole. So INVENT-NEW-IDENTITY does **not** trade a linear-moment blindness for a continuum-computable determinant; it trades it for a KL pole that *is* the atom.

**Sharpest line.** The determinant is a perfect probe at fixed `a` — a pole below a cut, exact, acid-testing — precisely because `z` magnifies the gap `am₁`; in the continuum that same magnification collapses, `z_n→1`, and the "isolated smallest zero" becomes the edge of a cut, i.e. the Källén–Lehmann pole. Renormalising the determinant is not a technicality: its divergent part **is** the marginal 4-D gauge BPHZ flow, its finite part **is** the spectral measure. The zero was never an `a`-uniform invariant; `f₁²>0` in the continuum remains the atom (`P3`) — the same wall in a new coordinate.
