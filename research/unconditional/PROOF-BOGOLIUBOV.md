# PROOF-BOGOLIUBOV — can the Bogoliubov correlation inequality LOWER-bound the gap `m₁` or the residue `f₁²`?

> 2026-11-23 · The Bogoliubov inequality stated for the lattice transfer matrix and pushed against `tr F²`. **Verdict: direction fixed and wrong.** It is Cauchy–Schwarz for the Kubo–Mori form: an **upper** bound on the order parameter `|⟨[A,B]⟩|²` (Mermin–Wagner), and — consuming a nonzero order parameter as *input* — an **upper** bound on the gap (Goldstone). No rearrangement lower-bounds `m₁` or `f₁²`; the only "lower" quantity it carries, the f-sum ratio `⟨[[B,H],B†]⟩/⟨{B,B†}⟩ ≥ m₁`, lower-bounds the *mean* excitation energy, not `m₁`, and holds at `m₁=0`. Pure YM supplies no local order parameter to feed it. No fabricated proof.

**Setup and the inequality.** ℋ = L²(∏ spatial links G, Haar), `T=e^{−aH_K}` (`H_K` Kogut–Susskind), vacuum `Ω`; a gauge-invariant `O` has `C(t)=⟨Ω|\bar Oe^{−tH_K}\bar O|Ω⟩=∫e^{−ts}dν(s)`, `m₁=inf supp ν`, `f₁²=ν({m₁})`. For bounded A,B at inverse temperature `β_τ` (**not** `β_lat=2N/g²`):
$$|\langle[A,B]\rangle|^2 \le \tfrac{β_τ}{2}\,\langle\{A,A^\dagger\}\rangle\,\big\langle[[B,H_K],B^\dagger]\big\rangle.\qquad(\star)$$
Both RHS factors are `≥0`: `{A,A†}=AA†+A†A` is a positive operator, and the double commutator is the "stiffness," equal in the proof to `β_τ([X,H_K],[X,H_K])_{β_τ}≥0`. *Proof*: `(X,Y)_β=β^{-1}∫₀^β⟨e^{λH}X†e^{−λH}Y⟩dλ` is positive semidefinite with identity `([X,H],Y)_β=β^{-1}⟨[X†,Y]⟩`; Cauchy–Schwarz and `(Y,Y)_β≤½⟨{Y,Y†}⟩` give `(⋆)` (Bogoliubov 1962; Griffiths 1966; Simon 1993). The direction is that of the inner product, not a tunable sign.

**`(⋆)` upper-bounds the order parameter.** LHS is `|order|²`, RHS a product of two positive norms. Mermin–Wagner: `B=φ(k)` gives `[[φ(k),H],φ(k)†]=O(k²)·stiffness`, so `|⟨[A,φ(k)]⟩|²≤Ck²`; as `k→0` LRO is forced to `0`. `(⋆)` *forbids* symmetry breaking; it cannot certify `⟨[A,B]⟩≠0`.

**Gap content is Goldstone — an upper bound.** Solving, `⟨[[B,H],B†]⟩ ≥ (2/β_τ)|⟨[A,B]⟩|²/⟨{A,A†}⟩`: given `⟨[A,B]⟩≠0`, the B-spectrum must carry weight at energies `≤ stiffness/(order)²`, so a continuous symmetry gives no positive edge, `m₁=0`. Equivalently `m₁ ≤ (controlled)/(order)²` — an **upper** bound. The contrapositive `m₁>0 ⟹ ⟨[A,B]⟩=0` holds for every `m₁>0` including `m₁→0⁺`, hence says nothing about `m₁`.

**Why it cannot be turned.** `(⋆)` reads `order² ≤ positive×positive`; a small RHS gives `⟨[A,B]⟩` small, never `m₁` large. The apparent exception — the f-sum `⟨[[B,H],B†]⟩=∫s\,dρ_B`, `⟨{B,B†}⟩=∫dρ_B`, so `ratio=∫s\,dρ_B/∫dρ_B ≥ m₁` — lower-bounds the *mean* energy **by** `m₁` (`m₁ ≤ mean`); when a massless mode exists the weight slides to `0`, so it cannot force `m₁>0` (as in LEVER §3).

**`tr F²` concretely.** `O(x)=tr F²(x)` on a time slice is a **multiplication operator**; two such spatial-link functions commute, `[O(x),O(y)]=0`, so `(⋆)` is vacuous for the natural 0⁺⁺ pair. A nonzero commutator needs `B∋E`; then `⟨[A,B]⟩` is the canonical/f-sum moment — **not an order parameter**, decaying as `|x−y|→∞`. The pair making the double commutator controllable makes the order parameter vanish; the pair that would be an order parameter does not exist in pure YM.

**No local order parameter (independent wall).** `(⋆)` needs `⟨[A,B]⟩≠0` as input. Pure 4D YM breaks no **local** symmetry (confinement ≠ SSB; center symmetry is 1-form, its order parameter the Polyakov loop, nonlocal, `⟨P⟩=0` at zero T). The Goldstone hypothesis is empty — the symmetry-unprotected wall of LEVER §3 / GAP-G2.

**Residue `f₁²`.** Even granting `m₁≥m₀>0`, `(⋆)` constrains `ν` only through **integrals** (Duhamel/anti-commutator = weighted moments). The atom `ν({m₁})` is upper-semicontinuous, invisible to finite integral data (LINEAR-DATA/LEVER §0B; portmanteau INEQ-T2; sign obstruction EDGE-f₁²). The free field (`g=0`) satisfies `(⋆)` with `ν` a continuum from `0`, `f₁²=0`. No residue bound.

**Sharpest line.** `(⋆)` is `|order|² ≤ positive×positive`: an upper bound on the order parameter, and — given one — an upper bound on the gap. To lower-bound `m₁` one must certify `⟨[A,B]⟩≠0`, but `(⋆)` only bounds it above; the residue is an integral-insensitive atom; and pure YM has no local order parameter to insert. The inequality is correct and cannot be turned — turning it around *is* proving the gap. *(Theorem: `(⋆)`, Kubo–Mori derivation. No-gos: free-field adversary, commuting interpolators, usc atom. Nothing constructed.)*
