# Can (b) be a theorem? — "the lightest bulk state couples to `tr F²`"

> CPB-attack probe (round 5). Verdict: **No.**

## Trace anomaly

In 4D the trace anomaly / Ward identity gives

```
T^μ_μ = (β(g)/2g)·tr F² + (Euler density) .
```

So `tr F²` is the **spin-0 (dilatation-charged) part** of the stress tensor: it interpolates
exactly the spin-0 states (the Euler density is a total derivative, creates none). Since `T_{μν}`
couples universally, the spin-0 channel is always populated and `tr F²` is its operator.

## But (b) needs more

(b) needs that the **lightest** bulk state is spin-0. The trace only ties `tr F²` to the spin-0
*sector*, **not** to the *lightest* state. The lightest could be spin-2 (created by `T`, not by
`tr F²`). The lattice finds `0^{++}` lightest (Morningstar–Peardon) — a dynamical fact, **not** a
symmetry consequence.

## Weakening (not promotion)

The defect-4pt bulk channel also sees `T_{μν}` (spin-2) via its defect one-point function, so one
could bound the spin-0 **and** spin-2 channels and take the min. Then (b) weakens to "the lightest
state couples to the bulk-defect OPE (`D·D`)" — still dynamical; the OPE coefficients are
dynamical (`OPE-COEFF.md`).

## Status

A **necessary relation** (`tr F²` = spin-0 trace), **not** (b). No Ward identity, positivity, or
symmetry forces the lightest state into the `tr F²` channel. **(b) is a physical input.**

Sources: Adler–Bardeen trace anomaly; Ward identities; Morningstar–Peardon lattice glueball
spectrum.
