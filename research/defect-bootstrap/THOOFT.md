# 't Hooft (magnetic) line sector

> CPB-attack probe (round 4). Verdict: the 't Hooft sector is the **dual** of the Wilson sector,
> **not stronger**; both are probes of charged sectors, not the neutral bulk gap.

## Setup

Pure `SU(N)` YM has an electric 1-form `Z_N^{(e)}` (Wilson lines) and a magnetic 1-form
`Z_N^{(m)}` ('t Hooft lines) — the two halves of the mixed `Z_N×Z_N` anomaly (Witten effect: a
`Z_N^{(e)}` rotation shifts `θ→θ+2π/N`). The 't Hooft line is a **magnetic disorder operator**;
its defect operators are the same kind (displacement `D^a`, `Δ=2`, …), graded by magnetic charge
`m∈Z_N`, and its crossing has the **same form** `Σ_O C²𝒢 = Σ_i a_i²𝔽_i` but with the anomaly
phase **conjugated** (`e^{iθ/N} → e^{−iθ/N}` under `e↔m`).

## Comparison

The 't Hooft sector is the **dual** of the Wilson sector, **not stronger**: each carries one half
of the `Z_N×Z_N` anomaly; combining them gives the full anomaly data, but neither alone is
stronger. Two asymmetries:

(i) the 't Hooft line is **non-perturbative even at `g=0`**, so its free benchmark is **not
Gaussian** (the Wilson line's is) — harder to set up;
(ii) in the confined phase the Wilson loop has an area law, the 't Hooft loop a **perimeter
law** — the defect regimes differ.

## Honest boundary

Neither charged line directly constrains the **neutral** bulk gap; both are probes. The dual
crossing with magnetic grading is convention-dependent.

Sources: GKSW, arXiv:1412.5148; 't Hooft, *Nucl. Phys.* B138 (1978); Cordova–Tachikawa
(symmetry TQFT).
